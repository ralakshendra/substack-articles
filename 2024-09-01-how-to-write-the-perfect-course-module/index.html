The 5 Components Of A Perfect Course Module

Every single module in your online course curriculum needs to have these five components:

  1. Reasons Why (Benefits)

  2. Mistakes to Avoid (Problems)

  3. Steps How To (Action)

  4. Commonly Asked Questions (Objections)

  5. Walkthrough Example (Proof)

Let’s break down each of these components and why they’re crucial to the success of your course.

1. Reasons Why (Benefits)

Before you dive into action steps, you need to ground your students with the “why.”

This isn’t just about telling them what to do but explaining why they should do it this way. For example, in our brand new course, The Prompt Engineering Playbook, we start by explaining why “grinding it out” as a writer is a thing of the past. We outline everything that’s wrong with thinking AI will steal your job as a writer and emphasize the benefits of leveraging technology instead. If you don’t take the time to address these foundational questions, you’re starting from a flawed place of understanding.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Dc1_!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F06e66e91-3344-4dd3-8aed-fe7a42bc0a31_2170x818.png)

It’s not enough to say, “Here’s what you should do.” You need to say, “Here’s why I recommend this, and here are the benefits of doing it this way.”

2. Mistakes to Avoid (Problems)

Once you’ve established the benefits, it’s time to warn your students about the common pitfalls they might encounter.

This step is crucial because it builds trust. You’re not just showing them the path; you’re also guiding them around the obstacles. For example, in our beginner writing program, Ship 30 for 30, after explaining the benefits of writing on social platforms, we point out the mistakes most people make when they start writing online, like getting too caught up in virality.

By highlighting these pitfalls, you help your students avoid common errors and gain confidence in the process.

3. Steps How To (Action)

This is the meat and potatoes of your course—the action steps.

But notice, you can’t just dive right into the steps. First, you need to establish the thinking and direction, address the mistakes to avoid, and then—only then—can you give them the tangible steps to take. A common mistake I see in online courses is diving straight into the “how to” without properly setting the stage. Another mistake is providing surface-level instructions that leave too many open loops.

You need to go seven layers deep in your action steps, removing every single piece of friction possible.

4. Commonly Asked Questions (Objections)

After you’ve provided the action steps, it’s important to address the objections your students might have.

I’ve found that objections are often disguised as questions. For example, someone might ask, “Is my niche really on LinkedIn?” when what they’re really saying is, “I don’t think this will work for me.”

By addressing these objections head-on, you dismantle faulty beliefs and reassure your students that they’re on the right track.

5. Walkthrough Example (Proof)

Finally, you need to provide some sort of walkthrough example.

This isn’t just a testimonial; it’s a tangible example that shows exactly how you or another student did it. In our PGA curriculum, for instance, we include a video where I demonstrate how to do free consulting via Loom. Then, I take it a step further by recording myself rewatching the video, pausing to explain why I did what I did.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!uIC0!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fff49f18e-3690-4146-86e6-a48ebda60333_2252x1276.png)

This level of depth instills trust and confidence in your students. By the time they finish the module, they should have no other questions. They should know exactly what to do, why to do it, what to avoid, and believe that it will work for them.

Write Your Course Module With AI

Now that you understand the five key components of a perfect course module, let's look at how you can use AI to speed up the creation process.

You can use this prompt with ChatGPT (or any other LLM) to write your first draft.

Here’s the prompt:

Act as an expert online course creator and write a 900-1200 word lesson on [TOPIC] for [TARGET AUDIENCE] that follows my [TEMPLATE].

<VOICE> 
Plain and simple. Keep the language straightforward and concise. 
</VOICE>

<TEMPLATE> 
# {Lesson} 
Introduce the problem the lesson addresses, explain its importance to the learner, and outline what they'll be able to do by the end of the module.
Segue to next section.

## {Why X Is It Important?} 
Intro the section.
Explain the rationale behind your approach, address potential doubts, and highlight the benefits. Use examples or analogies to illustrate.
Segue to next section.

## {The {Num} Biggest Mistakes X Make To Y} 
Introduce the mistakes.
Explain the common mistakes related to [TOPIC] and their consequences.
Segue to next section.

## How To [PERFORM SPECIFIC SUBJECT]: 
Introduce the steps section.   
Break down the process into clear, actionable steps.
For each step: 
- Intro the goal of the step
- Explain why the step matters
- Provide a concise explanation of exactly what to do. 
- Include a realistic and relatable example to illustrate the step. 
- If applicable, mention common pitfalls or mistakes to avoid.
- Transition to the next step
Use h3 for the main steps "Step {Num}:{Step}" 
Incorporate relevant tips, tricks, or best practices throughout this section.

## {Insert 1 specific audience Objection As a Quote} 
Introduce the objections.
Address potential objections. Focus on dismantling faulty beliefs that might prevent action. Offer explanations to dispel doubts.
Segue to next section.

## Example Intro the section.
Present a detailed, real-world example of applying the lesson's knowledge. If possible, include a step-by-step demonstration. Explain your thought process and decision-making throughout.

## Wrapping Up 
Summarize key takeaways and segue to the next lesson.
</TEMPLATE>

<Output_Format> 
Ensure a natural flow of information with clear transitions between ideas. 
Vary sentence structure and paragraph length to maintain engagement. 
Use a conversational tone while maintaining professionalism. 
</Output_format>

Let me know when you are ready for the topic, target audience, and any optional context.

As you can see, the prompt gives you a solid starting point.

Here's what this means for you as a course creator:

  1. You don’t have to reinvent the wheel every time you sit down to write a lesson. The structure is there, ready for you (and AI) to fill with your expertise. As a result, you're less likely to go off on tangents or forget important points.

  2. You can concentrate on what matters most - your insights. The structure handles the “how” of teaching, freeing you up to focus on the “what.”

  3. Consistency becomes easier. Each module follows a similar pattern, making your course feel cohesive and well-thought-out.

Whether you're creating your first course or your fifteenth, this prompt helps you turn your expertise into an actionable lesson quick.

Now, it’s important to internalize that you probably won’t get all of this right the first time.

I didn’t.

Every time we launch a new product, I see room for improvement. The question is whether you’re willing to do the hard work of improving it. For example, I’ve rewritten the curriculum for Ship 30 for 30 and PGA countless times, each time refining it further.

Building an online course isn’t about recording a few videos and making passive income. It’s about helping someone achieve a specific goal, dismantling the faulty beliefs surrounding that goal, and doing it with such depth that your students leave with full confidence in their ability to execute.

The courses that have the worst reputations are often those that lean too far in either direction—either all mindset and no action, or all action and no mindset.

To build an amazing course, you need both.

If you can master this framework, you can build a course that students will love—and more importantly, a course that truly helps them succeed.
