How To X Without Y: Train ChatGPT To Write Headlines That Promise “The Outcome” Without “The Obstacle”

This is literally the easiest headline format you can follow.

Author: Dickie Bush
Date: 2023-05-24

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!RO5z!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F59f152b4-7ed2-4e82-a90b-3365b24c5a8f_1260x900.png)

Ahoy, Digital Writers!

Today we want to walk you through a very simple framework you can use with ChatGPT to write attention-grabbing headlines.

For anyone who has taken Ship 30 for 30, you know how much emphasis we place on writing headlines first. We never write the title last. Because before you write anything, you have to figure out where you are going.

And if you start the writing process by writing the first sentence without a title—you are lost.

A headline gives the reader (and you) direction.

It’s like a mini Hero’s Journey highlighting where the story begins and ends.

  • [Beginning] Where are they starting?

  • [Conflict] What’s the problem—and why does it matter?

  • [Solution] How do they solve this problem?

  • [Transformation] And how their life is different as a result?

Readers read because of who and what they believe they will BE “on the other side.”

So, don’t make them guess.

Your headline should say exactly what the reader can expect (before they click and start reading).

Here’s a simple test:

Read a title and ask yourself, “Would I want to read this? Is this a good book, article, thread, etc.?” You don’t need to read or write an entire piece to know if it’s a good idea. And whether the answer is yes or no, you have an opinion before you even read the piece.

Because it’s clear.

For example, readers don’t know what these headlines mean:

  • “Writing 101”

  • “The Dream Brigade”

  • “Double Or Nothing”

There are missing pieces.

The reader doesn’t have what they need to help them make a decision. Because they don’t know if it’s for them or not. And when the reader hesitates, you lose them.

To help you avoid this, we want to give you a very easy framework to follow.

We use it all the time inside Ship 30 for 30. Cole uses it. Dickie uses it. Every great copywriter uses it. And all great authors unconsciously understand how to use this framework.

The framework is called:

The “How To X Without Y” Framework

The “How To X Without Y” framework is the scaffolding for every good success story.

  • How to “the outcome” without “the obstacle”

  • How to “get what you want” without “the hard part”

  • How to build a house without having to build it yourself

  • How to make tons of money without working 60 hours per week

  • How to become a Digital Writer without spending $120K on an English Degree

You need to be able to answer the “How To X Without Y” question before you start writing.

In fact, the majority of your time should be spent figuring this out. Because if you can’t answer the question, you don’t know what you are writing about. So, go through as many iterations as you need until you are clear on the outcome and the obstacle.

Then, you can you proceed with the writing process.

When you use this framework, the reader will immediately understand what they are going to get, “become a Digital Writer,” and they’ll understand why it’s so compelling, “they don’t have to spend $120K on a degree.” Your job is to fill in the gaps and tell them the story of “how.”

The key here is helping the reader successfully overcome a conflict.

3 Steps To Craft 49 “How To X Without Y” Headlines Using ChatGPT

This is a multi-step prompt.

All you need is one simple topic, ChatGPT, and a double-espresso!

Let’s dive in!

1. Use the Endless Idea Generation prompt to generate 7 subtopics (”X”)

The first thing you are going to do is generate a list of 7 subtopics for your specific topic.

Each subtopic is the action your target reader is looking to take. This is the “X” in the “How To X Without Y” framework. It’s the outcome the reader ultimately wants to get when they consume your writing.

  • Build a house

  • Make tons of money

  • Optimize a meal plan

  • Become a Digital Writer

  • Build running endurance

Start with a topic and run the Endless Idea Generation prompt to get your 7 subtopics.

When you are done, you should have something that looks like this:

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!tiJG!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa35986bd-65fd-4335-b101-12efd121366f_1304x938.png)

Let’s keep going.

2. Identify the 7 biggest problems or potential objections (”Y”) someone might face when trying to accomplish the outcome (”X”)

Anyone who has ever tried to accomplish anything knows: where there’s action there’s friction.

And in order for readers to care about your writing, they first need to understand what PROBLEM it solves. This is the “Y” in the “How To X Without Y” framework. It’s the reason the reader thinks or believes they can’t get what they want. It’s the conflict in your mini Hero’s Journey.

  • They don’t know how

  • They don’t understand

  • They don’t have the time

  • They don’t have the money

  • They don’t think it’s possible

You have a list of 7 subtopics from Step 1 and now, since this is Write With AI, we are going to generate 7 problems that a reader is likely to encounter for each subtopic.

Run the prompt in the same ChatGPT instance you started in Step 1:

Great, now for each of the subtopics, I want you to generate article titles using the "How to X (without Y)" format.

"X" is the goal of the subtopic.

For example:
If the subtopic is "Developing a training plan for first time marathoners."
Then the headline should be something like "How to develop a training plan without Y"

"Y" will be common obstacles people would face or objections as to why they wouldn't be able to accomplish "X."

For example: 
If the subtopic is "Developing a training plan for first time marathoners." 
And "X" is "develop a training plan."
Then the headline should be something like "How to develop a training plan without spending all your time on it"

Step 1: You are going to generate 7 common problems or objections ("Y") someone might have in accomplishing each subtopic outcome ("X"). Wait for my confirmation before moving to Step 2.

Step 2: On my confirmation, you will make 7 headlines for each subtopic, one for each of the problems for that subtopic.

Remember: You are creating "How to X without Y" titles for each objection someone might have in accomplishing the goal of the subtopic.

Understood?

I got 49 problems, but figuring out what to do with them ain't one!

After running the prompt, you should have a looooong list of problems that your reader potentially needs help solving—49 to be exact!

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!aQxI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F91834c44-f1b6-4dfa-9d69-0654b9269c49_1298x2896.png)

Note: In this prompt we teach our Digital Intern three things. We define “X.” We define “Y.” And we teach it how to write a “How To X Without Y” headline by providing two simple examples. We also break the prompt into two steps. Step 1 “brainstorm 7 problems” and Step 2 “write the headlines.” We break this into 2 steps so you can check-in with ChatGPT on the problems and make any adjustments you need.

3. Turn each of those problems into “How to X without Y”headlines.

Let’s put everything together.

Once you are happy with the problems ChatGPT created for you, offer some gratitude to your diligent Digital Intern and send it off on the final task in this prompt. ChatGPT will then take your subtopics and problems and turn them into 49 “How To X Without Y” headlines.

Boom!

That’s 1 newsletter topic for every week of the year (with a break for a 3 week vacation!)

The “How to X without Y” framework is literally the easiest headline format you can follow as a Digital Writer.

  • It keeps your writing in focus

  • It sets the reader’s expectations up front

  • It promises a desired outcome while tackling a common problem

  • And reader’s love it!

Just remember, you are training your Digital Intern to “do” and not “think.” Thinking is your job as the virtuoso writer. So, as with any prompt you run, always elevate yourself above the AI and do what you do best—think.
