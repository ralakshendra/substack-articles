    Ahoy, Digital Writers!

    Justin Welsh says, “The fastest way to come up with content ideas is by using systems.”

    We agree.

    And you’re not going to believe you’re eyes when you see how many different LinkedIn posts you can create using this easy 3-step prompt system—straight out of the “Golden Age” of copywriting.

    Let’s get into it (so you can start filling up your content calendar for the next month)!

    Step 1: Pick An Opportunity

    Think about your reader / audience / customer:

    1. What do they want right now?

    2. What are they hoping for in the future?

    3. What are they afraid will eventually happen?

    4. What frustrations do they want to get away from?

    These are all problems.

    And each one of these problems is your opportunity as a writer to help! Which is ultimately the job of your content—to educate, inform, entertain, or motivate. What you write about helps the reader transform from one way of thinking, doing, being, or believing to another way of thinking, doing, being, or believing.

    Make a list and pick one opportunity.

    (Try the below prompt if you need a few ideas to get started.)

    TOPIC = {e.g. education, yoga, sales, Solopreneurs, YouTubers, etc.}

Please to identify and succinctly list the top 12 prevalent challenges associated with the given topic, using no more than 12 words for each problem. Use a complete sentence.
- 3 Fears
- 3 Frustrations
- 3 Wants
- 3 Aspirations

Your response must be in a person's own words, like you would get in a survey, a product review, or in a conversation with a friend.

Be colloquial and plain.

Prioritize authentic and vivid expressions of the challenges.

    Once you have an idea, move on to the next step.

    Step 2: Choose A Post Angle

    Every opportunity (idea) can be reframed in 1,000 different ways.

    Inside Ship 30 for 30, we call this the 4A Framework. Other creators, like Justin Welsh, refer to it as the content “Style.” It doesn’t matter what you name it. Both deliver the same result: endless ideas (solutions) your readers can’t resist!

    Run this prompt to create 13 different ideas for your 1 opportunity.

    I want to create a post that helps solve the problem: 

{PROBLEM}

For the topic:

{TOPIC}

For the following types of posts, create 2 ideas: 

1) Ask a question
2) Make people think
3) Give one tip 
4) Share an anecdote/story
5) Provide an analogy
6) Give an example 
7) Compare two things
8) Share an observation
9) Teach people how to do something
10)Break a belief
11) Share something that is against the grain
12) Point out the difference between today vs future
13) Present an interesting or shocking fact/data

    Wowza!

    Pick 1 angle.

    (We’ll use this one in the next step: “Try a basic Child’s Pose before attempting a Full Wheel.” )

    Step 3: Write Your LinkedIn Post

    Now we’re going to turn 1 idea into 7 different LinkedIn posts.

    And we’ll use 7 different copywriting frameworks!

    1. BAB (Before, After, Bridge)

    2. PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solve)

    3. AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)

    4. QUEST (Question, Unique, Explain, Story, Tie-back)

    5. ACCA (Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action)

    6. SLAP (Startling Fact, Link to Audience, Application, Punchline)

    7. BISCUIT (Brief, Interest, Story, Conclusion, Unique, Invitation, Target)

    Copywriting frameworks have been around for over 100 years—for good reason.

    Because they tap into human psychology, guiding the reader along a proven path towards making a change. And they work! Read any ad from David Ogilvy or Gary Halbert and you’ll understand why.

    Choose 1 copywriting formula and 1 idea from Step 2.

    Then plug them in to the following prompt.

    Topic = {TOPIC}

Problem = {PROBLEM}

Angle = {ANGLE}

Write a compelling LinkedIn post about this topic following the {ACRONYM} (EXAPANDED ACRONYM) formula.

You are a LinkedIn post writer. Your job is to write compelling posts that get engagement.

For this post, use the {ACRONYM} formula. 

# Personality

You write in a simple, direct, and human-sounding tone that a 10 year old would understand. 
You favor short sentences.

# Avoid

- Emojis
- Hashtags
- Labels
- Pre-text

    Fair warning. ChatGPT may show its stubborn side with this prompt, plaster the post with emojis, drop hashtags, and label everything. If it fails to follow instructions, remind it: “Remember—no emojis!” it will usually correct itself.

    You may also notice ChatGPT leans on question openers and some overused phrases like “You’re not alone.” These aren’t bad. But if you want your intern to mix things up—tell it what you want!

    For example:

    • “Hey ChatGPT, please avoid using the phrase: you’re not alone.”

    • “Hey ChatGPT, please use a strong, declarative opener instead of a question.”

    • “Hey ChatGPT, please describe the steps between Child's Pose and Full Wheel.”

    Keep in mind, 80% done is the target you are shooting for.

    So don’t kill yourself trying to prompt your way into the perfect post.

    Flex those editing skills!

    Quick Recap

    1. We found a problem to write about.

    2. We generated a list of ways to frame the problem.

    3. We wrote a LinkedIn post using 7 different proven copywriting frameworks.

    If I’m doing my math right, 7 frameworks * 13 ideas = 91 posts—for 1 problem.

    Now that’s an opportunity!
