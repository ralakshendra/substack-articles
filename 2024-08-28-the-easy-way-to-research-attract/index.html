Hey there, Digital Writers!

Customer research, surveys, and interviews are the best way to get to know your reader.

But…not everyone has time for that!

So, we wrote a prompt to help you “get to know” your audience by learning from your competitor’s content. Think of it as mind-reading for marketers. This technique comes from The Steal Club by Alex Llull, where Alex shares the tactics and playbooks of the internet's top creators every week.

And this week we’re heisting the heist…

…and turning Alex’s steal into 4 simple prompts to help you quickly identify WHO you're talking to and what makes them tick so you can turn those insights into a brand new long-form article to attract your ideal readers.

Here’s how it works:

Step 1: Steal Reader Pains & Gains From A Popular Article

First, grab a proven article from a competitor or industry leader.

Google a key topic in your field. Pick one of the top-ranked organic (non-ad) results. These articles are usually well-optimized and come from trusted sources, meaning they’re likely already popular with readers similar to your target audience.

Which means they’re a good resource to steal from.

For example, if you're in fitness, you might Google "how to build muscle fast."

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ENlY!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F17a3626d-9e05-4d7b-be3d-e7dbb5f34c51_1706x1584.png)

Open one of the top search results.

Then use AI to dissect and answer the following 3 key questions:

  1. Who's the target audience?

  2. What pain points does the article address?

  3. What desires and motivations are emphasized?

Paste your article into the prompt below and run it. If you're using ChatGPT 4.0, simply paste the link, as version 4.0 has internet access.

You are an expert marketer. 

You are empathetic and able to deeply understand a person's needs.

1. Name the target audience

2. Make a list of reader pain points, desires, and motivations.

Use 12 words or less for each point.

TEXT = {COPY/PASTE ANY ARTICLE HERE}

Next, we’re going to brainstorm headlines.

Step 2: Generate New Headlines Using Reader Pains & Gains

In this step, we create 8 headlines using fill-in-the-blank templates.

Each template is designed to focus on a desirable outcome or trigger an emotion. When you plug in the pains and gains you pulled from Step 1, you’re speaking directly to what your audience cares about most. That means more engagement, more clicks, and more people tuning into what you have to say.

Here’s the prompt:

Great, now write 8 headlines using inspiration from the pain points, desires, and motivations.

1. How I [SPECIFIC, DESIRED OUTCOME] in [RELATIVELY SHORT TIME]
2. Save yourself [SPECIFIC TIME / MONEY] [DOING WHAT] and steal my [SYSTEM NAME]
3. (This is) how I get [SPECIFIC DESIRED OUTCOME] in [SHORT PERIOD OF TIME]:
4. [NUMBER][RESOURCES] to [DESIRED OUTCOME] (steal these):
5. My [NUMBER] biggest struggles as a [ROLE] were:
6. [NUMBER] steps to [DESIRED OUTCOME] ([IN SHORT TIME]):
7. Everybody thinks [MAINSTREAM OPINION] , but I think [COUNTERINTUITIVE OPINION]
8. How to [do something desired by audience] without [pain]

Step 3: Choose A Headline And Write The Outline

Now, pick your favorite headline and build an outline around it.

In this prompt, we instruct AI to use “fascinations” for each main point. Fascinations are a copywriting technique for writing sales bullets that tease product benefits. They also make good subheads because they build curiosity in your reader.

To run this prompt, choose 1 headline from Step 2 and specify your target audience.

I need you to help me create a detailed outline for the headline:

{HEADLINE}

I want to target:

{TARGET AUDIENCE}

Generate 5 subheads that:
1. Use fascinations from direct-response copywriting
2. Are 9 words or less each
3. Are benefit-driven and surprisingly unconventional
4. Deliver on the headline's promise
5. Build logically on each other

Ensure each subhead:
- Sparks curiosity
- Highlights a specific, enticing outcome
- Challenges a common assumption in the field

Format:
[HEADLINE]

1. [Subhead 1]
2. [Subhead 2]
3. [Subhead 3]
4. [Subhead 4]
5. [Subhead 5]

Return only the headline and numbered subheads. No additional explanation.

Step 4: Turn Your Outline Into A Long-Form Article Draft

Continue with the same chat you started in Step 3.

Run the following prompt to write the first draft of an article that speaks to the same pains and desires you uncovered from the article you started with in Step 1.

Great, now turn the outline into a 650-800 word Medium style article.

Write in a plain, simple, and straightforward style.

Use short paragraphs, sometimes single sentences, for easy reading.

That’s it.

In just 4 steps, you can now:

  1. Quickly understand your audience

  2. Create attention-grabbing headlines

  3. Develop a compelling outline

  4. Draft a full article

This method lets you skip lengthy research and create targeted content fast.

Give it a try. Your readers (and your metrics) will thank you.
