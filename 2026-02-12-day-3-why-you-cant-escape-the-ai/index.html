    Hey there,

    Yesterday we broke down the three things everyone tries when they want to get off the AI Hamster Wheel:

    • Better prompts

    • Custom GPTs

    • Learning to code

    These all seem pretty sensible, right?

    It's counterintuitive — but they're not getting you off the wheel. They're just making it spin faster.

    Now, we’re not saying prompts are useless.

    After all, a good prompt gets you a better output. But it won’t build something that works without you — and that’s the actual problem.

    Which brings us back to Skills.

    As a reminder, Skills are how you teach Claude to work like you.

    And Claude Cowork is the no-code AI agent that runs those Skills for you—so while you’re on a 5-mile hike at 9am on a Monday and doing your best thinking, your assistant is:

    • Prepping your presentation

    • Writing your objection handlers

    • Getting everything ready for your afternoon sales call

    So why isn’t everyone building them?

    Because getting here requires breaking through 2 faulty beliefs:

    Faulty Belief #1: “My work is too complicated.”

    This one sounds almost like humility.

    You tell yourself: “AI is great for simple stuff. But what I do requires real judgment. You can’t just bottle that.”

    And there’s something honest in it — your instincts do matter.

    But here’s what this belief is actually doing to you:

    It’s treating your work as one giant, inscrutable thing. So nothing ever gets broken down, or handed off, and so you end up starting from scratch every single time.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5w11!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb9da8fd1-5bdf-4b7d-a711-83214e45546b_1788x1350.png)

    The pattern is always the same:

    • Work feels too complex to touch...

    • So nothing ever gets delegated...

    • Which means every task starts from zero...

    • So when you’re tight on time you reach for a prompt to cope...

    • And the wheel keeps spinning, exactly where it was.

    Why?

    Because the work has never been unbundled.

    For example, “content creation” isn’t one thing — it’s research, outlining, drafting, and scheduling. Once you break it apart, it stops feeling mysterious. Because you can see how your work becomes a collection of specific, repeatable pieces.

    Now, we bet you’re thinking: “Okay fine. Maybe some of my work CAN be broken down. But the stuff that actually matters still needs me in it — the minute I step back, something gets lost.”

    Ah-ha! Maybe you’ve cracked it!

    But unfortunately, that’s the next faulty belief…

    Faulty Belief #2: “If I’m not the one doing the work, it won’t be right.”

    When the “it’s too complex” belief starts to crack, most people swing straight here instead.

    “Fine, I’ll delegate the simple stuff. But anything that really matters needs my hands on it. Quality requires my presence.”

    Sounds like someone who cares about their work.

    And honestly — we get it. Dickie, Cole, and Mitch all sat in the same spot.

    But then reality hits:

    The simple stuff never gets delegated because you’re buried in the important stuff. The important stuff suffers because you’re buried in the “doing.”

    You’re the bottleneck for everything — and you can’t step back because you’re also what’s holding it together.

    Plus, the real problem follows you everywhere. A better prompt doesn’t fix the belief that your presence is what makes the work good. You can upgrade your tooling all you want, but you can’t change the mental pattern that says it only counts when you’re the one doing it.

    Instead, you need a way to stay inside the work without being the one doing all of it.

    Skills are how you bridge these two beliefs.

    Because you need both:

    1. A way to break your work down without losing what makes it yours

    2. AND a way to step back from execution without your standards slipping

    Skills are how you connect these two — so you can finally build something that works like you, without you in every step.

    And the nice thing is:

    Once you have your first Skill running in Claude Cowork, you stop:

    • Treating every task like it’s too unique to ever hand off...

    • Re-explaining yourself every time you open a new chat window...

    • Being the bottleneck between your expertise and the work actually getting done...

    And instead, your best thinking stops evaporating — and starts compounding.
