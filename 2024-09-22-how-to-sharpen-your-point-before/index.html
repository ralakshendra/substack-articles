    Let’s dive in.

    Every writing guru, English teacher, and reader who’s ever lived has said it: “GET TO THE POINT!”

    It’s good advice.

    Most writers jump into writing and hope their “point” will reveal itself along the way.

    It’s a trap!

    And it leads to fuzzy writing, unnecessary rewrites, and ultimately wasted nights or worse Saturday afternoons.

    Instead, you need to sharpen your point before you even put pen to paper (or fingers to keyboard).

    But what exactly is a point and how do you get to one?

    Let’s clear it up.

    A point is simply the core idea or message you’re trying to communicate. It sounds like it should be 1 thing. And it is. But, when you break it down, it’s really a combination of a few things:

    • What your post is about (the main topic)

    • Who it’s for (your target audience)

    • What problem it solves and what benefit it unlocks (for the audience)

    • The emotional impact (you want to leave on your readers)

    When you leave any of the parts out, your post will feel like…

    …it’s missing the point.

    Without a clear point, your writing wanders. Your readers get lost. And you’ll get frustrated because you end up in an endless loop of edits, wondering what you’re trying to say. Tweaking sentences. Doubting every word. And second-guessing your message.

    However, when you know your point upfront, you avoid all that.

    Everything flows faster. You’re focused, your writing is tighter, and your message hits home.

    Let’s say you’re writing about overcoming writer’s block.

    Here’s your point:

    • Topic: Writer’s Block

    • Audience: LinkedIn writers stuck in a creative rut

    • Problem: They can’t start writing

    • Benefit: You’ll show them three easy ways to break through

    • Emotion: “WOW – that’s amazing”

    Now, every word you write supports the point.

    No distractions. No fluff. Just a clear path to the finish line.

    If you don’t clarify your point, you’ll meander around writer’s block, talking to YouTube creators, DIY Dad’s, and high-school college essay writers about a problem they “might” have.

    But if you do?

    You’ll know exactly what to say, how to say it, and who to say it to. You’ll write with confidence and speed.

    Clarity transforms everything.

    So…get to the point!

    That’s how you write faster and better.

    The Simple Trick To Clarify Your Point Fast

    One of the easiest ways to get clear on your point is to use a template.

    Think of it like a fill-in-the-blank game. The template lays out your main point, your target audience, and the problem you're solving. All you have to do is fill in the gaps.

    Why does this help?

    Because it forces your brain to focus.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!lRV_!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F900f0212-cd1f-4c2d-9bce-bdaa1ebb4852_1260x900.png)

    When those blanks are staring you in the face, you can't help but think about how to fill them in. It’s like having a roadmap that guides you straight to the heart of your message.

    BUT even with a template, filling it in by yourself can be a challenge.

    Sometimes, you just need a brainstorming partner to help you work through it.

    This is where the Clarity Builder Prompt comes in, taking the template concept and turning it into an easy-to-use tool with AI’s help.

    The "Clarity Builder" Prompt

    With this AI prompt, you can take any idea and let the AI fill in those concepts for you—point blank. ;-)

    It’s like handing off your mental roadblocks to a helpful assistant. Then, you can tweak the results until everything’s crystal clear.

    The best part?

    You’re not starting from scratch, and you get to focus on refining your message—without the headache of staring at a blank page.

    Here’s how it works:

    Step 1: Copy A Seed Idea From Your 90-Day Plan

    If you’ve imported your 90-Day content plan into your calendar, open up your calendar to today’s date. Click on the calendar event, and copy the subject line.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!rrRr!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F607f33f5-9bd4-4a4c-bee9-871d727b78f6_375x583.png)

    Here’s my day 1 content seed:

    “Why automation gives burned-out freelance writers more time to focus on high-value tasks.”

    Once you have a seed idea, move to Step 2.

    Step 2: Run The Clarity Builder Prompt For Your Seed Idea

    This prompt will help you get clear on what your post is about, who it’s for, and the emotional response you want to evoke.

    The AI:

    • Generates 3 focused headline variations to sharpen your point.

    • Fills in your audience, their problem, and the benefit of reading your post.

    • Defines the next step your reader should take after finishing your piece.

    Here’s the full prompt:

    Now, for the following topic(s):

{Insert Topic or Section}

For each topic, I want you to:

1. Create 3 title variations max 65 characters. Important: Absolutely NO compound headlines, dual-focus headlines, or ":'s" Be clear on the benefit for the reader (e.g. "A 3-step guide to...", "How to...")

2. Clarify the point by filling in this mad-lib:

This piece is about {Topic}.

It’s written for {Audience} who have {Problem}.

They will {learn, have, be able to} X with the {tangible benefit for reading e.g. detailed example / template / checklist / steps / etc.}

The reason the reader should believe me is because {credibility}.

By the time they finish, they will feel {emotion}, such as:
{pick one:
LOL – that’s so funny
WTF – that pisses me off
WOW – that’s amazing
NSFW – that’s crazy
OHHH – now I get it
FINALLY – someone said what I feel
YAY – that’s great news
OHH – that’s smart, I hadn’t thought of that}

After reading this piece they will realize [objective/point], so they will [next immediate, specific, tangible desired action, belief, or thought].

The immediate next step the reader should take is {CTA}.

    How to Use This Prompt:

    1. Copy the full prompt into ChatGPT or your AI tool of choice.

    2. {Insert Your Topic} from Step 1.

    3. Run the prompt and let the AI generate title options, clarify your message, and map out the key elements of your post.

    Now, you have an idea to work with.

    Which takes us to the next step.

    Step 3: Edit

    It’s time to edit and refine.

    AI has done the sneaky trick of showing you what your point could be. Now, it’s easier to decide what to keep and what to toss. Your job? Turn it into what it should be.

    Of course, even with a clearer point, the AI’s output might still be boring and generic.

    So what do you do?

    Bonus: Push AI To “Think”

    If AI’s output still feels generic or unclear, try pushing it to “think” deeply about your audience’s specific pains, problems, and daily struggles. Here’s an additional prompt to do just that.

    (Yes, you are the one who should be doing the thinking. But you can still use AI to get your creative juices flowing.)

    Now, I want you to dig deeper.

Based on the problem, ask yourself:

1. What specific scenarios or actions typically lead to this problem?
2. What does daily life look like for someone dealing with this problem?
3. What fears, thoughts, or misconceptions commonly arise when facing this problem, and how do they affect the person in the moment?
4. What are the direct consequences of this problem on their daily routine or overall day-to-day experience?

Next, think about the strong benefits and outcomes the audience can achieve once the problem is solved. Focus on the visceral, sensory details and emotions they’ll experience.  Back it up with tangible proofs or examples.

Your output must be specific, tangible, clear, and objective, highlighting real-life daily pain points, problems, or obstacles to attract and resonate with my target audience.

    That’s it.

    Where do you go from here?

    You’ve learned how to sharpen your point before you start writing, saving yourself time and energy by avoiding unnecessary rewrites. You’ve also seen how the Clarity Builder Prompt helps you get clear on your message, your audience, and the emotional impact you want to create.

    Here’s how to interpret the results:

    • If your point feels sharp: You’re ready to write! Dive into your post with confidence.

    • If you’re still unsure of your point: Go back to the prompt output and refine your ideas further. Adjust the problem you’re solving.

    • If your point is sharp, but you don’t know what to say: Pick another idea.

    Start small. Focus on getting clarity for just one post.

    Once you get the hang of it, you’ll be able to apply the process to every post in your 90-day plan—writing with confidence, clarity, and building your momentum one day at a time.
