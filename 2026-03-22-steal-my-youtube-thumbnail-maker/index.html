Steve Jobs wore the same black turtleneck, Levi’s 501s, and New Balance sneakers every single day.

He refused to waste a single brain cell on decisions that didn’t matter. I thought about that last week while I was 45 minutes deep into making a YouTube thumbnail.

For one thumbnail. I cycled through:

  • DALL-E (looked like a stock photo from 2009)

  • Gemini (closer, but still not right)

  • Canva (dragging boxes and fighting with templates)

I needed a preview image for a live session replay of Substack Starter Sprint (just one bootcamp in a series we’re running every month for the rest of the year).

Which means I’d be doing this same 30–45 minute process again and again. So I stopped trying to “get better at making thumbnails” and had Claude build me a custom app that handles the whole thing in under 60 seconds. Now when I need a thumbnail, I open the tool, upload a photo, pick a template, and download.

Today I want to show you exactly how I built the app, because the same approach works for any repetitive task that’s quietly eating your creative energy.

At the end I’ll give you my thumbnail maker so you never need to use Canva for your YouTube preview images again.

Let’s dive in!

Step 1: Describe the problem you’re trying to solve for Claude.

I used Claude Cowork to build this app, but you can use regular Claude too.

Either way, your first job is to explain what you’re trying to do, why the current options frustrate you, and what your ideal version looks like.

Here’s the prompt I used:

I make YouTube thumbnails every week and I’m tired of using Canva.

I want a simple tool that helps me:

  • Upload a photo

  • Add a title

  • Pick from a few proven YouTube templates

  • And then download the thumbnail as an optimized image for YouTube

What ideas do you have?

That last part matters.

When you ask Claude for ideas, you’ll immediately see how it thinks about your project. Collaborate on your idea until your happy with the direction.

Pro Tip: Claude works way better when it can see what you’re aiming for.

So if you have a screenshot of the end result you want, even if it’s rough sketch on paper, upload it.

I sent Claude a screenshot of a thumbnail I’d made manually and said:

“Here’s the style I’m going for.”

You’d be surprised how much better the output gets when Claude has a visual reference.

Step 2: After Claude builds the first version, tell it exactly what’s missing.

Every time I’ve asked Claude to build an app for me, I’m blown away by the result.

But the second you start to interact with your new tool, you’ll see all the details you missed.

So, your next step is to give Claude some feedback:

I want 8 different layout templates instead of 2. Each one should have a spot for a photo, a title, AND a subtitle, and a small logo in the corner.

I also want choose colors for the background.

The more specific you are, the fewer rounds of back-and-forth.

But beware of making too many changes in a single prompt.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!dRig!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F688b7fdc-44b6-4c70-9077-aac94efc68c8_694x1180.png)

Step 3: Isolate your changes by adding one feature at a time

This keeps Claude focused and it keeps you from turning your app into Frankenstein.

Avoid dumping 15 requests into one message.

Instead, ask for one feature per prompt. Test it. Then ask for the next one.

  • “Add a text effect option. I want to choose between no effect, a drop shadow, or an outline around the text.”

  • “Add a badge option. A small colored circle in the corner that says ‘NEW’ or ‘LIVE’ or whatever I type in.”

  • “Let me control the font size with a slider instead of typing in a number.”

  • “Add an AI prompt generator for background images.”

Each one took about 30 seconds. And because I tested between each prompt, I caught problems early.

Note: Describing UI problems in words is a challenge. But Claude can see images. So just screenshot what you want to change and send it. I sent about 15 during my build and each fix took under a minute.

“The text is invisible on this template when I use the orange color palette. See screenshot. Can you fix the contrast so the text is always readable?”

[

Step 4: Once the basic tool works, apply a brand style to make it yours.

I uploaded our PGA brand style guide and said:

_“Attached are my brand colors and fonts.

Add these as preset color options and make this font the default.”_

Now every thumbnail I make automatically matches my brand.

If you don’t have a brand style guide, pick a brand you like and ask Claude to model the style.

For example:

“This looks amazing. But I want to use Substack brand colors.”
