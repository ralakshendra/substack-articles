Before we get to the answer and the prompt let’s look at why Mr. Beast’s hooks crush.

Mr. Beast knows most viewers decide whether to keep watching his YouTube videos within the first 5 seconds.

The YouTube algorithm prioritizes videos with a high average watch time.

And Mr. Beast does 2 very important things to capitalize on this fact in every intro he writes. He knows he has one chance to convince you to keep watching. So, he gets to the point, FAST. And he piques your interest by opening a curiosity gap.

If he can get you to stay for just a few seconds, you're hooked.

Let’s look at one of his INCREDIBLE hooks:

“I tied up this FBI agent.

If he steals this $100,000 dollars from me before midnight, he keeps it.

Here’s a knife — good luck!

Let’s go Tarik — our first stop’s through the woods!”

Take a second and study that to soak in just how FAST it gets to the point.

The hook works because he sets up a big challenge with even bigger rewards. It’s clear, relatable, and includes an unexpected twist to grab your attention, “Here’s a knife-good luck!”

Here's another:

“I’m going to spend the next 50 hours buried alive in this coffin.

Probably the dumbest thing I’ve ever done.

Boys, seal me up!

Welcome to my coffin — I have a camera here, here, and here.

Alrighty — let’s do this, bury me alive.”

There’s a reason Mr. Beast is the most popular YouTuber in the world!

The secret sauce?

Mr. Beast tells his audience exactly how the video ends in the first 30 seconds.

Creator Nathan Baugh calls this, “Jenga Storytelling.”

It’s named after the game Jenga because players often pull a piece from the very bottom of the tower and place it on top to start the game. In storytelling, you take the end of the story and hint at it right at the beginning! Which makes the audience wonder, “what happens?!”

It creates a sense of mystery that the reader / viewer wants filled.

Once you create a vacuum in the reader’s mind—they’re hooked!

Now scroll back up and re-read the 3 hooks.

Let’s look at each one.

  • Hook #1: You have an $800K island at stake between 10 friends. There are an “unknown” number of challenges each participant must endure to win the island. And the first challenge—swim with the sharks! You probably agree swimming with sharks is challenging (and scary) BUT what is the the real challenge here? Is it getting in the water with sharks? Is it avoiding a shark bite while the water is chummed? You, as the reader, are left with unclear boundaries about the story. And they are subtle enough to dissuade you from diving in.

  • Hook #2: 10 people, 10 challenges, 1 island, and a race to put a coconut in a hole. It doesn’t get any clearer than this. You immediately want to know who wins the island and the coconut challenge. Classic Mr. Beast.

  • Hook #3: While the hook opens with the ending, the stakes are unclear. The rules are unclear. And the hook follows a classic “clickbait” formula of promising a big reward and then asking the audience to “stick around till the end” which feels inauthentic. Immediate no.

And the winner is?

Hook #2!

Hooks #1 and #3 were written by the ever positive Digital Intern. (No feelings were hurt in this little game.)

Which means you can fine-tune your prompt until you get the exact results you want or until you get the output to a place where you are happy to manually edit the last 10%.

And now you get to pick up where I left off.

  • Start with a topic

  • Run the prompt below

  • And start writing cold opens like the master of YouTube attention.

This storytelling hook works for any topic.

You don’t have to give away an island, bury yourself alive, or recreate Squid Game!

Here’s the prompt:

Act like Mr. Beast: master of online attention. 

I want you to help me write hooks like "Mr. Beast."

- They start with an intriguing premise with an outlandish, audacious, or incredible challenge.
- They include high stakes (a large sum of money, a demanding task, or a mental challenge).
- They define clear and simple rules for the challenge.
- They create visual imagery by describing the setting or challenge.
- They add a human element by involving yourself or others in the challenge.
- They include an unexpected twist to the challenge. 

---
Here are 6 examples:
---
Example 1: 
"I re-created every single step from "Squid Game" in real life.

And whichever one of these 456 people survives the longest wins $456,000.

The first game we are going to play is red light Green Light.

And they have 30 minutes to cross the red line on the other side of the room.

So, without further ado—green light!"

Example 2:
"I put 100 people inside of a giant red circle.

And whoever leaves the circle last wins $500,000.

But if you touch the red line, it's game over.

I don't care how long this takes, whichever one of you leaves last wins $.5M."

Example 3:

“I’m going to spend the next 50 hours buried alive in this coffin.

Probably the dumbest thing I’ve ever done.

Boys, seal me up!

Welcome to my coffin — I have a camera here, here, and here.

Alrighty — let’s do this, bury me alive.”

Example 4:
"I'm going to spend the next 50 hours alone in solitary confinement.

This is the room I will be suffering in for the next 2 days.

Normally I would do 24 hours in a challenge like this, but I decided I would push myself and go for 50."

Example 5:
"I rented a $1M a night hotel room.

And I also rented this $1,000 a night cave.

This $10,000 a night underwater palace.

This $50,000 a night island.

And so more.

We're going to spend the night in all of them.

Starting with the world's cheapest hotel.

All the way in India!"

Example 6:
“I tied up this FBI agent.

If he steals this $100,000 dollars from me before midnight, he keeps it.

Here’s a knife — good luck!

Let’s go Tarik — our first stop’s through the woods!”
---

I am going to give you a topic, and you are going to write 3 "Mr Beast" hooks.

The key to writing a great hook in this style is the very last sentence. 

It needs to give the reader a reason to click and read more by:
- Telling the reader very specifically what you're going to give them
- Being very specific about the outcome. We want tangible, noun-oriented outcomes

A few rules:
- Use numbers, potent verbs, and precise nouns to create a flaming image in the reader's mind.
- Hit the reader's subconscious and make them curious about what could happen next.
- Keep the structure (line spacing) of each hook.
- Be ambitious. Go big! But be punchy and concise (use 8-12 words per sentence).
