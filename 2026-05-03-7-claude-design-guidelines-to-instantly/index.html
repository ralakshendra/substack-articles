Claude dropped a full design tool last month, and we’ve been obsessed.

For writers, or anyone who isn’t a professional designer, it helps build complete visual assets with AI.

Without you having to think about using any code.

It’s called Claude Design. You describe your brand with a handful of reference screenshots, a few font names, and a couple of words about the feel you’re going for.

Then, Claude builds your structured design system inside the platform: palette, typography, voice rules, signature components. Then Claude uses that system to produce everything downstream from the same coherent brand.

Now, here’s why this matters for you.

If you write, you’re also a marketer.

(Whether you signed up for that job or not)

  • Your newsletter needs a landing page.

  • Your course needs a sales page.

  • Your book needs a cover.

  • Your emails need headers that don’t look like default Mailchimp.

Every piece you publish lands harder when it looks like it came from the same confident brand.

Before this month, that kind of consistency meant one of three paths:

  1. Hire a designer. Expensive. Slow. And often you’re stuck with their taste more than yours.

  2. Learn design yourself. Years of practice to match what a pro creates in an afternoon.

  3. Templates and hope. Passable. Forgettable. Everyone else is using the same ones.

Claude Design is now a fourth path—and it’s the first one built for people who don’t want to become designers.

However, if you want to get the best outputs from Claude Design it’s important to learn to think like a designer. Because “Make it look better” is not a design instruction.

And the best way to get better design output from AI is to use design language.

So, we created a 13 page Design Lexicon you can download at the end of this post. It has 30+ terms and “prompt tweaks” to help you get the most from Claude Design when you’re working with the model.

Now, here are 7 to get you started.

7 design guidelines to instantly improve Claude visual assets:

Let’s dive in.

Guideline #1: Use an 8-point spacing scale

When a layout feels “off,” spacing is the problem 90% of the time.

But when every margin, padding, and gap snaps to a multiple of 8 (8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64), the layout instantly looks intentional.

Also worth knowing:

  • Padding = space inside an element

  • Margin = space between elements

They fix different problems.

Prompt: “Use an 8-point spacing scale throughout.”

Guideline #2: Establish hierarchy between your H1 and subhead

Hierarchy = the visual order of importance.

If your H1 and subhead look like siblings instead of parent and child, you have a hierarchy problem.

Prompt: “Establish stronger hierarchy between the headline and subheadline.”

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Ji2K!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdce23e17-fb6e-47c6-8f72-5f9e8517c40b_1924x730.png)

Guideline #3: Constrain the measure to 65 characters

Measure = the width of a column of text.

If text stretches edge-to-edge:

  • Your eyes travel too far

  • You lose your place

  • Reading slows down

To fix this, you constrain it.

  • ~60–75 characters per line

  • Fixed max-width (e.g. 600–700px)

Now the layout feels tight and readable.

Prompt: “Constrain the measure to ~65 characters.”

Guideline #4: Set breakpoints at 375, 768, and 1024

Breakpoints = the screen widths where your layout changes.

Prompt: “Responsive at 375, 768, and 1024.”

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!NNYe!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8a64d86a-72e4-432f-9e92-b29c3f22469d_1696x1180.png)

Guideline #5: Constrain anything that sprawls

Constrain = the word that fixes things that feel too big.

It applies everywhere:

  • Max-width

  • Measure

  • Container

Whenever something sprawls, constrain it.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!h0NX!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3f3536c8-1f2b-49cb-82aa-41b2150184ac_1846x770.png)

Guideline #6: Use density instead of “less cluttered”

Density = how tightly packed the elements are.

Said differently:

  • “Reduce density” = breathing room

  • “Increase density” = pack more in

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!J4sq!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd7337136-a693-49d2-9e0b-36f31651cda9_1866x816.png)

Guideline #7: Ensure touch targets are at least 44px

Touch targets = the tappable areas on mobile.

Apple’s spec: 44 pixels minimum.

Prompt: “Ensure all touch targets are at least 44px.”
