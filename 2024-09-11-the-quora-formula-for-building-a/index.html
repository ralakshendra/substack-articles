    The Quora Formula For Building A Daily Writing Habit

    Author: Nicolas Cole
    Date: 2024-09-11

    Hey there Digital Writers!

    In 2014, I challenged myself to write one Quora answer every single day.

    I built a routine around taking whatever I had learned that day at work and used it as inspiration for my writing. I focused all of my energy into making my answer the most relatable to whoever was experiencing (or wanted to experience) similar things in life. I tried to think about exactly when I had learned the answer to each question, what had happened, what was said to me, when it had “clicked,” and then shared that.

    Day in, and day out, I’d combine whatever I’d learned that day with the larger life lessons I’d learned as a pro gamer, or bodybuilder, and write my answer to a question I found on Quora.

    The more answers I wrote:

    • The more my writing improved

    • The more I realized what people enjoyed about my writing

    • The more I learned what topics resonated with the most people

    As a result, my writing exploded. By the end of 2014, I had accumulated more than 3,000,000 views.

    “This simple daily process transformed my writing career into a multi-million dollar business.”

    Writing every day sounds simple.

    But when you’re staring at a blank screen with zero ideas, it’s easy to feel stuck—and suddenly, that “simple” habit becomes hard to maintain. Everyone knows the secret to building a habit is consistency, but the secret to consistency is having a repeatable formula that makes it even easier to show up every day.

    You don’t need to reinvent the wheel each time you sit down to write. Instead, use a simple mechanism that eliminates the guesswork, gets you started, and keeps you going.

    The Quora Formula For Building A Daily Writing Habit

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!bszj!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdbd9edc9-2bf8-4aea-bfbf-69713f6bb02f_1246x1156.png)

    Here’s my Quora formula:

    1. Pick a question your audience is already thinking about.

    2. Answer with a quick story that relates to the question.

    3. Connect the story to a lesson or insight.

    4. End with an actionable takeaway they can use immediately.

    With this framework, you can write an email, blog post, or even a journal entry daily without getting overwhelmed. The key here is to keep it simple by picking one question.

    Here's why it takes the guesswork out of your writing:

    • Questions give you a clear direction.

    • Stories are based on what you’ve lived, so they’re natural to share.

    • Takeaways show what you’ve learned, which makes it easy to explain.

    Let’s look at a few examples so you can see this method in action.

    Example 1: "How do I stick to a new diet?"

    • Story: Last year, I tried going vegan. The first week, I caved and ate an entire block of cheese...

    • Lesson: Small, consistent changes beat drastic overnight shifts.

    • Takeaway: 30-day habit tracker template

    Example 2: "How do I start investing with little money?"

    • Story: When I was 10, I started a lemonade stand. But instead of pocketing the cash...

    • Lesson: Start small, reinvest gains, and play the long game.

    • Takeaway: Beginner's guide to micro-investing apps

    Example 3: "How do I keep the spark alive in my marriage?"

    • Story: For our anniversary, I planned a surprise trip. But when we got to the airport...

    • Lesson: It's not about grand gestures, but consistent small acts of thoughtfulness.

    • Takeaway: "Date night ideas" generator

    You don’t need to be an expert on the topic. You just needed to be self-aware enough to articulate how, where, and when you’ve learned that lesson too. Plus, you can even use AI to surface questions your readers are already asking—so you’re never left wondering, what should I write about today?

    Easy, right?

    Find “Quoraish” Questions Fast With AI

    You can easily spend hours scrolling through Quora, hoping to find a question that triggers your inspiration.

    Don’t get me wrong, Quora is great—it worked for me. But in 2024, it's not the same platform it was 10 years ago. There’s a lot more noise.

    With AI, you can cut through that noise and quickly find Quora-style questions that are open-ended, relatable, and specific—giving you exactly what your audience cares about, on demand.

    Here's how it works:

    1. Start by asking AI for the obvious questions your audience is asking.

    2. Then, push for the less obvious but necessary questions.

    3. Next, focus on specific pains, objections, or misconceptions.

    4. Tap into fears and frustrations with emotional questions.

    5. Finally, ask the “Question Behind the Question” (QBQ)—what’s really going on?

    For example, if you write about productivity for busy parents the questions might break down like this:

    • Obvious: "How do I manage my time better?"

    • Less obvious: "How do I get sh*t done while being present for my kids?"

    • Specific: "Where do I find energy when I'm running on 4 hours of sleep?"

    • Emotional: "Will my kids resent me if I focus more on work?"

    • QBQ: "Am I a bad parent for wanting to work?"

    See the difference?

    Most people will never ask that last question out loud, but I guarantee if you’re a working parent you’ve thought about it...that's $$$ for an engaging piece of content.

    Once you have a question to work with, the rest is simple. All you have to do is write your answer from your personal experience.

    Here’s the prompt:

    I want to better understand my customers.

Topic = {Insert your topic}

Target Audience = {Insert your target audience}

To do so, I want you to help me generate a list of specific, rarely articulated questions that my target audience might be grappling with privately.

Step 1: First list the 7 most obvious questions.

Step 2: Then list 7 less obvious but necessary questions, that most people don't ask and should.

Step 3: Then list 7 "how to without / even if / when / in / that / so questions" about a very specific, tangible and relevant pain, objection, misconception, assumption, or belief in the target audience's significant phases or milestones in this cycle.

Step 4: Then aim for 7 emotional questions that tap into deep-seated fears, pains, or frustrations. You're looking for questions that implicitly reveal the underlying emotion without explicitly naming it.

Step 5: Then list all the Questions Behind The Questions (just give me the QBQs)

The goal is to uncover real pain points and frustrations that I can address with my content, while appealing to fundamental human motivations like:
- Survival, enjoyment of life, life extension.
- Enjoyment of food and beverages.
- Freedom from fear, pain, and danger.
- Sexual companionship.
- Comfortable living conditions.
- To be superior, winning, keeping up with the Joneses.
- Care and protection of loved ones.
- Social approval.

Think step-by-step.

    How to use this prompt:

    1. Copy and paste the prompt into any LLM (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.)

    2. Insert your topic and target audience into the prompt.

    3. Run the prompt

    4. The AI will output a list of 35 audience-relevant questions.

    Choose 1 question to answer for the day, save the list of questions and answer them 1 at a time over the course of a month, or use them as inspiration to come up with a question of your own.

    And if you need some help writing a story, try this prompt to get started. Use it in the same chat where you created your list of questions.

    Now, please write a write a short story in 1st person narrative that relates to the question below:

Question:

"{Insert Question}"

Connect the story to a lesson or insight.

End with a simple actionable takeaway that the target audience can put to use immediately.

    That’s it.

    This method works for any topic.

    Whether you're writing about personal development, professional growth, or other niche interests, the framework remains the same. Start with a question, tell a story, draw a lesson, and end with an actionable step. Answer one question every single day and you’ll get better line by line.

    Remember, the only wrong answer is the one you didn’t write.
