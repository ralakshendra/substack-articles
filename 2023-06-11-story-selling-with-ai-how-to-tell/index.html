Story Selling With AI: How To Tell A Story That Prints Money Like Twitter “Copythinker” George Ten

This is how you win others to your way of thinking.

Author: Dickie Bush
Date: 2023-06-11

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!1pUj!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe712a936-423e-40df-a4f3-a7ecc5a599f6_1260x900.png)

Ahoy, Digital Writers!

Today we are going to train ChatGPT to tell a story like George Ten (aka The Grammar Hippy).

George is a master copywriter, thinker, and prominent figure on Twitter. He’s written multiple 6 figure sales pages and used his “Copythinking” method to build a $8.5 million store.

George sends a weekly newsletter that magically pulls readers into his products with story.

Recently, he shared his framework for how he does it. And whenever you see a framework, you, my dear Digital Writer, should be thinking, “How can I get my Digital Assistant to do this for me?!”

Let’s dive in!

Telling a captivating story is the most powerful way to win others to your way of thinking.

Stories play a crucial role in your ability to build an engaged audience.

  • They engage and move readers to action through emotion.

  • They add depth and meaning—helping the reader understand “why?”

  • They offer more than “just the facts,” they invest the reader in the outcome.

Whether you are curating stories or telling personal stories, they make it easy for the reader to both identify with you and deepen their relationship with you (especially within a specific niche).

If you can tell a compelling story, you can shape the world!

Viral-worthy stories start with everyday moments.

The best stories are short, personal, and relatable.

Think about it for a minute. What’s the last story you remember hearing? Was it a blockbuster hit or was it something that caught your attention in passing? I am willing to bet what came to mind (i.e. one that impacted or influenced you) was either told by a friend or it was something you read on the internet.

You don’t have to be J.K. Rowling, Steven King, or George Lucas to craft a compelling story.

You just need 3 simple ingredients.

  • Ingredient 1: The Event. Recall the key events that shaped your day. What happened during your day? Find something that stood out to you, a difficult situation, something that made you laugh, or cry.

  • Ingredient 2: The Impression. Identify why each event left an impression on you. Dig in and unpack what happened. Why does the event stand out?

  • Ingredient 3: The Takeaway. Identify the lesson or takeaway. What did your experience say about you, life, or the world? This is the bonding element that allows the reader to see something deeper or more possible for themselves through your story. What’s in it for the reader—problem solved, obstacle overcome, or an “Aha!” moment?

All you need is 1 part "attention" (the events in your day), 3 parts "intention" (building a habit of collecting, writing, and sharing your stories), and a dash of Write With AI—and you'll be off to the races.

Easy!

George Ten’s framework for telling stories that sell.

Now that you have the necessary ingredients for your story, you need to tell it.

  • Tell it to a friend over coffee

  • Ship it in your next email newsletter

  • Share it on Twitter, LinkedIn, your blog etc.

George recommends a 5 part-framework for telling a good story.

  1. Tell the story in 1st person. When you tell a story from your perspective, you build trust and connection with your reader. Tell the story from your own eyes. How did you see and experience the event?

  2. Start from the MOST interesting place. Start your story right before or right after any event you uncovered above. Get to the action!

  3. Follow a logical sequence. Give your story shape—beginning, middle, and end. This happened. So what? So this. Then what? This. So what? So this. Help the reader follow along. I went to the story. I bought milk. I dropped it and it splattered all over the check-out aisle

  4. Keep attention using open loops and sub-headlines. Make sure the story flows. Use emotions and observations as break-points in between to keep the reader engaged.

  5. Plug your product / service. Drop a link at the end or in the middle, it doesn’t matter.

Simple, right?!

Here’s a fill-in-the-blank template you can use to quickly put the ingredients together using George’s framework.

I wanted to ____ but ___ and so ___. The lesson: ___.

For example: **

It was 10 PM and I wanted to go to bed but my dog broke away from the leash. So I had to spend the next half hour chasing her around the yard before catching her. She was lucky I didn't just leaver her in the wild. The lesson: I love my dog and I love my sleep, but when the dog interrupts my sleep all bets are off. Just like a good story in your email that pulls you down the rabbit hole, away from your inbox, and into ChatGPT to write like George!

Let’s see what our Digital Intern thinks.

Train ChatGPT to create George Ten’s “signature” story style guide.

So far, we have the ingredients for a story, we have a recipe to write a story—and now we need to give it some flavor!

The beautiful thing about ChatGPT and other AI tools is they can mimic a writer’s style. If a writer has a large enough public library like Ernest Hemingway, Jerry Seinfeld, or Malcom Gladwell, ChatGPT can instantly remix any writing in a variety of these well-known styles.

And if a writer is lesser known, like George, you’re not out of luck.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!PPdQ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2ff46253-7e3a-4354-9f77-99496781e09d_1080x232.png)

You can train ChatGPT to analyze a writer’s style and have it create a style guide for you.

A style guide is like a ready made BBQ spice you buy at the grocery store. Whether it’s “Killer Hog’s BBQ Rub” or “Meat Church’s Gospel Rub”, you can taste the difference when consumed. The same is true for a writer.

The combination of each of these style elements make up a writer’s “signature flavor.“

When you mix together different elements of “style” in the right doses, you can tell exactly who wrote it.

How many times have you seen a post on social media and immediately thought, “ChatGPT wrote that!”

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sVF5!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F722d0c63-d13f-4376-93d6-16347535e665_1070x414.png)

The reason you can intuit ChatGPT as the writer is because ChatGPT has a default “signature style”—boring!

George Ten is far from boring. So, we need to teach ChatGPT how to write like him in 3 simple steps.

We do this by feeding AI George’s signature style.

Let’s walk through it.

Step 1: Grab a “signature” piece of writing.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HtN_!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F96018783-32db-4e93-9c03-e25af400120a_1260x900.png)

We chose one of George’s epic email stories.

But, you can do this for any writer you are interested in learning about.

Step 2: Prompt ChatGPT to analyze the writing.

You have an expertise in linguistics, natural language processing, and prompt engineering.

Your task is to convert the provided text into an elaborate style guide. 

This guide will serve as a blueprint for creating fresh content on a distinct topic while maintaining the original style demonstrated in the text.

Pay special attention to aspects like:

1. The voice and tone: How formal or casual is the language? Is it authoritative, friendly, neutral, or something else?
2. Mood: What emotional atmosphere is created? Does the text seem cheerful, melancholic, mysterious, etc.?
3. Sentence structure: Are the sentences simple, compound, complex, or a mixture? How long are they typically?
4. Transition style: How does the writer navigate from one idea to another? What types of transition words or phrases are used?
5. Rhythm and pacing: Is the writing fast-paced with lots of short sentences, or is it slow and deliberate with longer sentences?
6. Signature styles: Are there any recurring phrases or unique punctuation usage that stands out? Any patterns or repetitions?

I am particularly interested in the stylistic nuances that set this author's writing apart. 

Identify those elements that could serve as indicators of this author's distinctive writing style.

Let me know when you are ready for the text.

Notice we didn’t just ask ChatGPT to “give me a style guide for X.”

You can do this. And your Digital Intern will create a style guide for you. BUT this is Write With AI—and spending the extra minute to decide what’s most important for you to know about a writer’s style will give you 100X better results.

Since the text we are interested in modeling is a story, we chose stylistic elements that make stories pop: Tone, Mood, Voice, Rhythm, Transitions, etc.

Prompt Writer’s Note: We want to draw your attention to a couple things happening here.

  1. We give ChatGPT an identity—a linguistic, NLP, prompt engineer.

This tells AI exactly how to behave, giving our intern clear guidance on a knowledge set. This is a game changer for any prompt writing you do. Tell ChatGPT to assume the identity that you wish you had time time and experience accumulate for yourself—expert copywriter, email marketing wizard, etc.

  1. We tell ChatGPT what we are MOST interested in collecting about George’s style.

This directs our intern’s attention to the elements that will help us recreate his writing. Most notably we want to know the quirky bits, the signatures, which make his writing uniquely his.

These are two great strategies to employ in any prompt you are creating.

Step 3: Give ChatGPT the sample writing

The last step is to hand ChatGPT the writing you want it to analyze.

And boom!

Now, you’ve got a George Ten style guide on your hands.

Which means, you can ask ChatGPT to write like George!

Train ChatGPT to write a story like George Ten.

Let’s do a quick recap.

  1. You have the ingredients you need for a story: an event, an impression, and a lesson.

  2. You have the recipe to cook up the story: George’s 5 part framework

  3. You have the spice to give the story it’s flavor: George’s style guide

We are almost ready to write.

But, we need one more thing—we need something to cook with!

And what we are going to cook with is a proven story structure. We could prompt ChatGPT using George’s framework, however we can get even more specific with our cooking instructions. These additional instructions will help guide our intern down the path of writing a compelling story. You wouldn’t cook without a pan and some fire would you?!

So, where do you get a structure?

You can literally use any proven structure you want.

  • Pixar’s story spine

  • Joseph Campbell’s Hero’s Journey

  • Anne Lamott’s ABDCE framework

You just have to outline the structure for ChatGPT to follow.

Since we are writing “like George” we are going to reverse engineer George’s story into our own flow. Just like the style guide, George also writes using patterns. These patterns can be studied and reused.

Here’s what the pattern looks like for the story we grabbed.

Time to cook! Let’s put everything together in a single prompt for ChatGPT.

Below is a template for you to follow.

Topic: I wanted to ____ but ___ and so ___. The lesson: ___.

Task: Write a X word story following the author style guide and rhetorical structure

Style Guide:
{Paste in your style guide.}

Rhetorical Structure:
{Paste in the structure you want to follow.}

Let’s see how ChatGPT did.

*Look, I'm no hero, okay?

I'm just a regular dude who likes his Zs, but what happened last night... Man, let me tell you. Just as I was crawling under my sacred shrine, the bed, at the sacred hour of 10 PM, Lady Furrington, my trusty canine, decides to do a Houdini.*

*One moment we're bonding over a late-night pee ritual, the next... POOF!

Lady Furrington has slipped the leash and is running wild like she’s auditioning for the next Lassie movie. And there I was, in my PJs, chasing after her around the yard like a half-crazed insomniac.*

*Picture this.

My backyard isn't exactly Central Park, but let me tell you, it's got its obstacles. Picture me, trying to catch a hyperactive, fur-covered missile while dodging garden gnomes, sprinklers, and a KILLER mosquito swarm.

And what was Lady Furrington doing?*

Living her best life, LMAO!

….

This is a tiny a snippet of the full story.

ChatGPT delivered like a champ (wonder if George would agree?)! Yes, you still have to format for readability, and overly-emphatic use of certain styles, etc. But the last 10% of work is peanuts compared to the creative storytelling that ChatGPT just performed for you.
