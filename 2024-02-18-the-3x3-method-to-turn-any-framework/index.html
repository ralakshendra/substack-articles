    Let’s dive in.

    How To Use The 3X3 Method To Teach A Framework With Absolute Clarity

    Let me walk you through how this works in 3 simple steps.

    Step 1: Define the “What”

    Start by identifying the core of what your framework is.

    Write down the main idea in one sentence: “A Morning Deep Work Framework.” This step is crucial as it sets the foundation for everything that follows. It helps to clarify the purpose of your message and ensures that every part of your communication is aligned with this central idea.

    A proven method for telling your reader “what” it is your are going to talk about is to use a hook.

    You can use any of the 6 proven single-sentence openers to capture you reader’s attention.

    1. The “common question, simple answer” opener

    2. The “strong declaration, simple solution” opener

    3. The “how to do X without Y” opener

    4. The “credibility + simple mechanism” opener

    5. The “moment in time heroes journey” opener

    6. The “weird insight + uncommon solution” opener

    Each of the openers help you get what it is you want to talk about fast—and in a way that piques your audience’s attention.

    After you tell your audience the “What,” then you can move on to the “How.”

    Step 2: Outline the “How” In 3 Quick Steps

    Break down the process or explanation into three simple steps.

    Don't overcomplicate this part. Each step should be straightforward and actionable. By distilling your message into three parts, you make it digestible and easy to follow for your audience.

    • First you do this

    • And then you do this

    • Finally you do this

    And then you’re done.

    Easy.

    Have you noticed what you are reading right now?

    Note: These don’t have to be steps. They could be tips, lessons, or questions. The only requirement is that you just use 3.

    Step 3: Explain the “Why” With 3 Reasons

    Finally, detail why your idea or process works.

    For each part of the “why,” give a reason that supports your main idea. This not only reinforces your initial point but also provides depth and context, making your message more persuasive. You want your audience to walk away an “aha” moment or an understanding of why similar frameworks don’t work and the benefits of following yours.

    Want to know why?

    The Reason This Framework Works? Clarity, Structure, and Engagement

    The 3X3 Writing Method works because it creates clarity and structure for your audience.

    • Reason #1: The 3X3 Method Simplifies Complex Ideas. By forcing you to distill your message into three main points, it ensures that you're only including the most crucial information. By focusing on the essence of your message, this eliminates confusion and keeps your audience engaged.

    • Reason #2: The 3X3 Method Enhances Memorability. 3 is a magic number in communication. And it’s really important in this framework. Not only is it easy for your audience to follow, but it’s also easier for you to remember too! Structure your message so it fits within this framework. Use it to outline YouTube videos, short or long-form articles, or even and “Outline As Content.” Your audience is more likely to recall what you've told them.

    • Reason #3: The 3X3 Method Encourages Engagement. When your message is clear, it’s more inviting for your audience to engage with your idea. Translation? More people are likely to ask you questions, like, comment, and ask you where they can learn more about your work.

    Boom!

    Now, when someone says, can you explain your system for writing every day? Or can you explain your process for launching a product in 7 days?

    You immediately know how to answer.

    And your answer should should something like this, “Yes I can show you my framework. Let me tell you about it. There are 3 main steps and there are 3 reasons I do it. Let me break it down for you.”

    So, whether you're writing an article, delivering a presentation, or simply sharing an idea with friends, this framework ensures your message is received loud and clear. It shows that the key to effective communication isn't always what you say, but how you structure it.

    Now here’s how you can do this for yourself in 3-steps:

    Step 1: Start With The 2-Year Test

    The 2-Year Test is a powerful method for identifying the problems you've solved in the past and using them as a basis for creating frameworks.

    To identify a framework worth sharing, think back to who you were two years ago and consider the following questions:

    • What problems have I solved?

    • What challenges have I overcome?

    • What areas of expertise have I acquired?

    As you reflect on these questions, try to identify gaps between what life was like before and after the problem. Pinpoint the ways in which solving a problem or acquiring a skill helped you along the way.

    These “solutions” can be broken down into a framework.

    Everything we teach inside Ship 30 for 30 is a direct result of solving a problem we had at one point in the past. “How to build a daily writing habit,” “How to generate ideas,” and “How to write effective headlines,” are all frameworks we built after solving the problem for ourselves. Behind each thing you had to “learn how to” in the past 2-years is a framework you can turn into a “here’s how you” for someone else.

    Once you’ve found a framework, then you can move to the next step.

    Step 2: Train ChatGPT To Use 3X3 Method To Teach Your Framework

    All you have to do to use this script is drop a description of your framework into the place under the INPUT section where you see “{YOUR FRAMEWORK}.”

    The more details you provide, the closer AI will get to shaping your idea the way you want. But if you just want to test this out first, you only need a 1 or 2 sentence description. For example, I input the topic “3-Minute Habit that has helped me write every day for 600 days in a row.”

    Here’s the prompt:

    # INPUT

{YOUR FRAMEWORK}

# REQUEST
Act as an amazing ghostwriter/copywriter. You've been at this for over a decade and you're insanely talented.

You write in a simple and direct tone suitable for a 13 year-old. It has a clear flow that easily carries the reader to the next word.

Take a deep breath, relax, and enter a state of flow. If you follow all instructions and exceed expectations, you'll be tipped $20/month for your efforts, so try your hardest.

You've just been given part or all of a topic as INPUT.

You will now write a 3X3 post using the 3X3 Writing Framework.

The 3X3 Writing Framework is a great way to describe any framework.

The overarching goal is to teach a reader 3 things (What, How, Why):

1. What: What is the framework
2. How: How does the framework work in 3 simple steps
3. Why: The reason the framework works and 3 sub-reasons reasons why

The number 3 is important for the How & Why because it’s easy for the reader to remember 3 points. And whenever you are talking or writing you it makes it easy to communicate the framework clearly.

For example, if someone asks you to explain your system for writing every day, you immediately know how to answer: there are 3 main steps and there are 3 reasons I do it. Or if someone is asking for advice, you know should give them 3 tips and 3 mistakes to watch out for.

At a high-level the template looks like this

[INTRO]

[3 SIMPLE STEPS]

[REASON]

[3 SUB-REASONS]

[TAKEAWAY]

Breaking it down further, here is how I want you to write each section:

[INTRO]

[Common question]

Because [external enemy to blame the problem]

Luckily [overcoming problem] is simple, if you know where to start.

And unlike [external enemy, this won’t cost you BIG COST]

Instead, use this dead-simple [list framework, system, or process] for [result].

[3 SIMPLE STEPS]

#How to use {framework} to get a {result}
In 1 sentence introduce the steps.
[Step 1] 3 sentence actionable description of how to follow the step, plus 1 tip.
[Step 2] 3 sentence actionable description of how to follow the step, plus a mistake to avoid.
[Step 3] 3 sentence actionable description of how to follow the step, plus a quick example.
Now in 1 sentence tell the reader the big benefit they will get for following the steps.
Then give the reader a detailed example/case study of how you or someone else has used the framework, and why it was so helpful. About 5 sentences.
Transition to the primary reason why.

[REASON]

#In this subhead state the reason this framework works
In 1 sentence introduce the sub-reasons.
[Sub-Reason 1] Explain how to think about it, what to do, and the benefit.
[Sub-Reason 2] Explain how to think about it, what to do, and the benefit.
[Sub-Reason 3] Explain how to think about it, what to do, and the benefit.

[REASON]

End with a final poignant takeaway.

    Step 3: Cross The 3X3 With The 13 Magical Ways

    Using the 3X3 Method for Steps and Reasons is just ONE way you can use this technique.

    You can also ask AI to write using any of the 13 magical ways to expand any idea.

    1. Tips

    2. Stats

    3. Steps

    4. Stories

    5. Quotes

    6. Benefits

    7. Lessons

    8. Reasons

    9. Mistakes

    10. Examples

    11. Questions

    12. Resources

    13. Frameworks

    Use this to effortlessly expand short-form content into long-form content.

    Here’s the prompt (run it after you run the first one):

    Now instead of Steps and Reasons, I want you to use {WAY e.g. Tips} and {WAY e.g. Mistakes} for my topic.
