How To Turn A Breakout Article Into 3 Fresh LinkedIn Posts With "Parallel Pain"

Author: Nicolas Cole
Date: 2024-04-07

Ahoy Digital Writers!

So, you had a post “pop off?”

Your audience loved it. You received the most likes, shares, and comments since you started posting online. That’s what we like to call… “signal!”

But, now what?

Don’t waste time trying to find something brand new to write.

Instead, give your readers more of what they’ve already told you they want from you.

Double-down on your proven post.

Turn “signal” into more success

For example:

Let’s say you are a veterinarian, and you write a weekly newsletter for your practice. One day, you notice a post about deciding to euthanize a pet is really popular. Your audience is commenting with questions. Customers are mentioning it to you during office visits.

All signs point to the topic striking a nerve with your audience.

So, what related topics pull on the same emotions or problem areas?

How about:

  • Managing a pet’s chronic illness, like diabetes

  • Or making an emergency health decision for your pet

  • Or noticing the early warning signs of disease

And so on.

Each of these problems is related (caring for the illness/injury of a pet) but different (not about euthanasia).

Each problem is the seed for at least 25 new pieces of content.

And with AI, it’s really simple.

All you have to do is teach AI to analyze your content. Then, train it to generate ideas for related but parallel content. And repeat to infinity.

Let’s break it down.

Use AI Turn A Proven Topic Into 25 New Ideas (With A 90% Chance Of Repeat Success)

Ok, so I pulled 90% out of the air.

But it’s at least double the odds.

Whatever the “math” is, when you build on the success of one idea, the “new and related” idea is more likely to work, too.

We are going to do this in 5 quick steps.

Step 1: Train AI To Analyze A Proven Post

The first step is simple. Run the below script.

I’ll explain what it does in the next step.

I am going to give you a text and then I want you to perform the following:

# STEPS

Step 1:

Open <scratchpad> XML tags and make brief, entity dense, sophisticated notes on the following about the INPUT:
- Identify the Specific Target Audience for the post.
- Name be the General Audience for the post. Use 5 words or less.
- Deconstruct the specific topic or idea
- What's the desired outcome/success state for the target audience based on the post idea?
- What's the problem state for the target audience based on the post idea?

Close <scratchpad> and continue with your work having analyzed the problem.

Step 2:

List 20 analogous yet distinct problems/challenges for the target audience in your <scratchpad notes>, avoiding those directly related to the problem in your <scratchpad notes>. 

For example if the problem is euthanising a pet, then "managing a pet's chronic pain" is good because it's tangential and different. While "Dealing with family disagreements on pet euthanasia" is bad because it's the same problem of euthanasia.

Precision is key; think outcomes with specific, concise, tangible consequences. 

Double down on precision and ensure every problem is tangible and specific.

Use 8 words or less for each response.

Do you understand?

Let me know when you are ready for the text.

Step 2: Feed AI Your Proven Post

After you run the prompt in Step 1, AI will ask you for the text you want to analyze.

Copy/Paste the text from your proven post into the chat dialog.

AI will analyze the post and identify the text's Target Audience, Topic, and Problem.

We ask AI to do this first, so it has the proper context for performing the next step in the prompt: brainstorming problems. We give AI a post, it deconstructs it, and then holds the core ideas of the post in "memory" so you can leverage them for more ideas.

Here's an example of how ChatGPT analyzed the post "The Biggest Mistake People Make When Deciding To Euthanize Their Pet."

After AI deconstructs your post, it responds with 10 related but different “parallel” problems for your Target Audience's "proven problem.”

Step 3: Choose A “Parallel” Problem

Pick 1 "similar problem" from the list you created in the last step.

Then, move to the next step. Easy.

For the example moving forward, I selected “Identifying signs of serious illness early on.”

Step 4: Generate 25 New Post Ideas

Add the Similar Problem and your Target Audience into the prompt below.

Run the prompt.

If you've been with us for a while, this prompt will look familiar. It's the Endless Idea Generator—or the "EIG," as we like to call it. You can use the prompt anytime you're feeling stuck, short on ideas, or looking for different angles to write about in your niche.

You can learn more about the EIG here.

# INPUT

Problem = {Similar Problem}

Audience = {Your Audience}

# TASK

Now, generate 1 idea for every categoty in each sub-topic below (using the HEADLINE RULES) for my INPUT organized the same way I have here.

Actionable (here's how)

- Tips
- Tools
- Hacks
- Advice
- Resources
- Frameworks
- Ultimate Guides

- Curation (the best books/podcasts/threads)

Analytical (here's a breakdown)

- Trends
- Numbers
- Reasons
- Examples
- Teardowns
- Swipe files

Aspirational (yes, you can)

- Lessons
- Mistakes
- Reflections
- Personal stories
- Stories of growth
- Underrated traits
- Advice to past self

Anthropological (here’s why)

- Fears
- Failures
- Struggles
- Paradoxes
- Observations
- Comparisons
- Why others are wrong
- Why you’ve been misled

# HEADLINE RULES

An “Irresistible Headline” has these 5 fundamental pieces.

Beside each piece, I am going to give you some examples so you can learn:

- HOW MANY (a number): 6 tips, 7 reasons, 11 mistakes, etc.

- THE WHO (an audience): for project managers, for therapists, for founders, etc.

- THE WHAT (a noun): tips, reasons, mistakes, lessons, examples, templates, case studies, etc.

- THE WHY (a reason/outcome): to get promoted, to not lose your job, to buy your first house, etc.

- “TWIST THE KNIFE” (2-3 additional benefits/outcomes): to attract your first 5 high-ticket clients and move into your dream office.

Step 5: Write Your Post(s)

The next step is to select a topic from Step 4.

So, choose one that:

  • You feel drawn to

  • You are interested in

  • You have more to say about

This will make the writing (or editing after AI does the writing) process easier.

Add the topic to the script below by replacing "{TOPIC}" with your topic.

We will use the problem you select as the topic for 3 new LinkedIn posts. The prompt writes a post which follows each of the following writing patterns:

  • Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS)

  • Attention-Interest-Desire-Action (AIDA)

  • Startling Fact-Link To Audience-Application-Punchline (SLAP).

If you don’t like these patterns, you can replace them with your own. (Click here for a list of 200 different frameworks you can try.)

Now, run the script!

Topic = {TOPIC}

Write a compelling 3 different LinkedIn posts about this topic following the {ACRONYM} (EXAPANDED ACRONYM) formula.

Post 1. PAS (Problem, Agitate, Solve)
Post 2. AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)
Post 3. SLAP (Startling Fact, Link to Audience, Application, Punchline)

You are a LinkedIn post writer. Your job is to write compelling posts that get engagement.

# Personality

You write in a simple, direct, and human-sounding tone that a 10 year old would understand. 
You favor short sentences.

# Avoid

- Emojis
- Hashtags
- Labels
- Pre-text

Now, you've got 3 posts.

Pick the one you like best. Make edits. Or rerun the prompt with a new topic or with different writing frameworks. Remember to publish!

Once you ship the post you extended from the original breakout article, listen for signal. Then, repeat the process.
