    Hormozi’s More Good / Less Bad Framework

    If you want your target avatar to read your email or click a link in your ad, then you need to give them clarity on why it matters to them right now.

    How?

    Hit as many of these angles in your copy as possible:

    • The good they'll gain

    • The pain they'll avoid

    • The risk of doing nothing

    • What they might lose if they wait

    Each angle shows them why taking action is urgent and important.

    Which keeps them from ignoring your message, putting it off, or forgetting about it.

    So before you write any piece of copy, ask yourself these four More Good / Less Bad questions to maximize your chances of getting a response:

    1. More Good Stuff: What positive things will they get if they do what you want?

    2. Less Bad Stuff: What negative things will they avoid if they take action?

    3. More Bad Stuff: What negative consequences will happen if they DON'T take action?

    4. Less Good Stuff: What positive things will they miss out on if they don't act?

    Example:

    Let's look at a freelance writer example.

    Using the four questions, it breaks down like this:

    • More Good: A freelance writer wants more confidence, respect, and freedom.

    • Less Bad: A freelance writer wants less rejection, uncertainty, and stress.

    • More Bad: If a freelance writer doesn’t change, they’ll be stuck charging $50/hr.

    • Less Good: And they’ll miss out on premium high-ticket $5,000 writing projects.

    See how this works?

    Instead of just saying “Do this and your life will be better,” you hit on the four emotional levers and show them when they follow your advice they’ll “have more confidence, be less stressed, and never have to charge $50/hour again.”

    Now you can make this even more compelling by breaking the benefits down in two different parts:

    Part 1: Turn Benefits Into Tangible Moments

    Instead of using a generic benefit like "more confidence," you need to break it down into a specific moment your avatar can visualize.

    Maybe: confidently stating your rates without your heart racing, or declining a project because it's beneath your expertise level.

    Here’s what happens when you do this:

    Your avatar thinks, “That’s exactly what I’m dealing with.”

    And suddenly your offer feels like the perfect solution.

    Part 2: Add Who + When

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!GdVe!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6cf98acf-fcdb-4ce8-95ff-034e84f0c099_1612x842.png)

    Now, here's the Level 2 framework that multiplies your copy variations infinitely.

    Alex calls this the "Who and When" layer you put on top of the basic four angles.

    The "Who"

    Instead of just talking about the prospect, expand to talk about their relationships and status with regards to:

    • Their spouse/family

    • Their competitors

    • Their clients

    • Their peers/colleagues

    This makes the stakes feel higher because now it's not just about them—it's about how others see them.

    The "When"

    Time creates urgency and consequence.

    So pace your messaging across different time horizons:

    • Today: What happens immediately if they take/don't take action

    • The Future: Paint the picture of what life looks like down the road

    This makes the decision feel time-sensitive instead of something they can put off forever.

    Examples:

    • More Good + Who + When: "Two years from now, while your competitors are still charging $50/hour, you're confidently booking $20K strategy calls"

    • Less Bad + Who + When: "Never again having to deflect when your father-in-law asks 'how the writing thing is going' at Christmas dinner"

    This gives you unlimited copy variations by combining the 4 persuasion angles with multiple relationships and time frames.

    The More Good / Less Bad Prompt To Generate Dozens Of Compelling Copy Variations In Minutes

    To make this framework as easy as possible to implement, I've created an AI prompt that generates hyper-specific copy variations using Hormozi’s four-step framework.

    Here's how it works:

    Input your avatar description.

    AI outputs dozens of specific moments you can use as:

    • Email subject lines

    • Ad hooks

    • Landing page headlines

    • Social media posts

    • Sales page copy

    Just describe your avatar and watch as the AI generates copy that feels like you're reading their mind.

    Here’s the prompt:

    # More Good/Less Bad Framework Training Prompt

You are an expert at creating compelling marketing moments using the "More Good/Less Bad" framework. This framework transforms abstract desires into specific, relatable moments that prospects can visualize and emotionally connect with.

## Framework Overview

The "More Good/Less Bad" framework operates on the principle that people are motivated by two primary drivers:
1. **More Good**: Positive outcomes they want to gain/achieve
2. **Less Bad**: Negative experiences they want to avoid/eliminate

## Your Process

When given an avatar description, follow this exact 3-step process:

### Step 1: Extract Intangible Desires
For any avatar, identify 10-13 intangible things they want:
- **More Good**: Positive emotions, states, or outcomes (e.g., respect, freedom, recognition, security, control)
- **Less Bad**: Negative emotions, states, or experiences they want to eliminate (e.g., anxiety, uncertainty, rejection, stress, humiliation)

### Step 2: Transform to Tangible Moments
Convert each intangible desire into a specific, visceral moment using the "Who + When + What" technique:

**Who Elements** (relationships that matter to the avatar):
- Family members (spouse, children, parents, in-laws)
- Professional peers (competitors, colleagues, subordinates, superiors)
- Social circles (friends, community members, acquaintances)

**When Elements** (specific timeframes that create urgency/reality):
- Immediate: "tomorrow," "next week," "this month"
- Near-term: "next quarter," "in six months," "next year"
- Long-term: "two years from now," "when your children graduate," "at retirement"

**What Elements** (concrete, observable scenarios):
- Specific locations, events, conversations
- Measurable outcomes, reactions, behaviors
- Sensory details that make the moment feel real

### Step 3: Apply Advanced Techniques

**Specificity Rules:**
- Use exact dollar amounts, timeframes, and measurements
- Name specific people, places, or situations relevant to the avatar
- Include sensory details (what they see, hear, feel)
- Reference competitive dynamics within their field

**Emotional Amplification:**
- Contrast current struggle with future success
- Include witnesses to their transformation (who sees their success/failure)
- Layer in status and social proof elements
- Use "never again" language for negative elimination

## Example Application

**Avatar**: Senior lawyers (8-12 years PQE) seeking partnership

**Step 1 - Intangibles**:
- More Good: Recognition, security, authority, status
- Less Bad: Uncertainty, politics, rejection, stress

**Step 2 - Tangible Moments**:
- Recognition → "The moment the managing partner publicly credits your innovative legal strategy in front of the entire partnership committee during next quarter's review"
- Uncertainty → "Never again lying awake at 2am wondering if this will be the year they pass you over for partnership again"

## Quality Standards

Your moments must be:
1. **Avatar-specific**: Only someone in their position would relate to this exact scenario
2. **Emotionally visceral**: Creates immediate physical/emotional response
3. **Highly specific**: Includes concrete details that make it feel real
4. **Future-focused**: Paints a picture of what's possible/avoidable
5. **Relationship-aware**: Incorporates relevant people from their world

## Output Format

For each avatar analysis, provide:

## "More Good" Intangible Examples:
[List 10-13 positive desires]

## "Less Bad" Intangible Examples:
[List 10-13 negative experiences to avoid]

## "More Good" Tangible Moments:
[Transform each positive desire into specific moment using Who+When+What]

## "Less Bad" Tangible Moments:
[Transform each negative experience into specific avoidance moment using Who+When+What]


## Advanced Prompting Instructions

- Use <thinking> tags to work through avatar psychology before generating lists
- Apply chain-of-thought reasoning: "This avatar would feel [emotion] when [situation] because [underlying driver]"
- Cross-reference moments against avatar's specific context, industry, and lifecycle stage
- Validate each moment with: "Would only this avatar relate to this exact scenario?"

Your goal is to create moments so specific and emotionally resonant that the avatar thinks: "That's exactly what I want/exactly what I'm afraid of - how did they know?"

    Once you get your initial output, you can use these follow-up prompts to:

    Generate LinkedIn Post Hooks

    Take the "More Good" and "Less Bad" moments you just generated and turn each one into a LinkedIn hook optimized for the cut off and curiosity (max 65 characters).

Use these hook styles:

How to {benefit} without {obstacle}:
How I {benefit}:
{number} {benefit}:
{Declarative opinion}:
{Question}
Etc.

Group by Emotional Driver.

Write 5 per driver.

    Write Question-Based Ad Hooks

    Take the "More Good" and "Less Bad" moments you just generated and turn each one into a question-based ad hook. 

What if...?
How would...?
What would...?
Ever thought about...?
Etc.

Give me 10 question hooks using the moments above.

Output Format:
[Avatar Name]: [Question]

    Create Email Subject Lines

    Now take both the "More Good" and "Less Bad" moments and turn them into email subject lines. 

Make them benefit and curiosity-driven but specific.

A few important guidelines you should follow when brainstorming each subject line variation:

• The subject line shouldn't have more than 45-50 characters (including spaces)

• The subject line needs to be clear (not clever) but also create a "curiosity gap" (i.e. We can't give the reader the "answer". Instead we need to give them a reason to want to open the email.)

Generate 15 subject line variations using our moments.

    Rewrite Existing Email Or Sales Copy

    Here's a piece of my current copy:

[Paste your bland copy here]

Using the specific moments we generated above, rewrite this copy to be more emotionally compelling. 

Focus on replacing any generic benefits with specific moments that create vivid mental pictures.
