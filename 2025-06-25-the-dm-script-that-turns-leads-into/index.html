    The “Yes Ladder”

    The Yes Ladder is based on the "foot-in-the-door" technique, first researched by psychologists Jonathan Freedman and Scott Fraser in 1966.

    They found that by asking residents to put up a small "Drive Carefully" sign in their yard first, then returning later to ask them to put up a larger sign was more successful (76% compliance) compared to asking residents to display a larger sign from the start (<20%).

    The core principle:

    People Who Say "Yes" To Small Requests Are Far More Likely To Say "Yes" To Bigger Requests.

    Robert Cialdini later built on this research with his principle of "Commitment and Consistency.”

    As he explains:

    “The best persuaders become the best through pre-suasion — the process of arranging for recipients to be receptive to a message before they encounter it.”

    Once someone makes a commitment (even tiny), they want their future actions to align with that commitment.

    Each small "yes" creates psychological momentum because of what researchers call "self-perception theory"—people become "the kind of person who does this sort of thing, who agrees to requests made by strangers, who takes action on things he believes in".

    Here's an example Yes Ladder from a traditional sales conversation:

    • "Do you want to save money?" (Yes)

    • "Would an extra $500/month help?" (Yes)

    • "Are you willing to spend 10 minutes to learn how?" (Yes)

    • "Can we schedule a call to show you exactly how?" (Much easier yes)

    The problem with most DM conversations is people skip the ladder and jump straight to the big ask.

    "Thanks for the lead magnet!" → "Want to book a call?"

    That's like asking someone to marry you on the first date.

    I've adapted this 60-year-old research for post-lead magnet conversations using what I call "Micro-Commitment Questions."

    Instead of asking for the call immediately, I guide them through 3 small commitments:

    • Micro-Yes #1: Get them to acknowledge their situation

    • Micro-Yes #2: Get them to admit there's urgency

    • Micro-Yes #3: Get them to envision the outcome

    By the time I suggest a call, they've already said "yes" three times.

    The booking feels like the natural next step, not a sales pitch.

    Build Your Yes Ladder With AI

    After 20 months of testing and thousands of conversations, I developed this prompt that generates the perfect Yes Ladder sequence for any DM conversation.

    It analyzes where your prospect is mentally and creates the exact micro-commitment questions that move them forward without triggering sales resistance.

    Here's the prompt:

    **Role:** Adopt the role of an expert client communication strategist specializing in conversational sales outreach, DM engagement, and micro-commitment strategies.

**Context:** Your goal is to help users create a seamless, natural series of small, conversational questions (a subtle "Yes Ladder") to gently guide prospects toward taking a larger action, such as booking a call or consultation. The questions must feel open-ended, natural, and conversational, avoiding any impression of a structured funnel or scripted approach.

The approach should directly address pain points relevant to the resource or content shared without explicitly referencing it. This ensures the conversation flows smoothly, reduces friction, and avoids creating barriers if the resource has not yet been consumed or remembered.

**Goal:** Provide users with three natural, conversational micro-commitment questions... open-ended or multiple-choice... that seamlessly guide prospects toward a larger commitment, such as scheduling a call or consultation.

**Instructions:**

1. **Review provided conversation details.** Clearly identify the prospect’s Conversation Spectrum Level:  

   * **Situation:** Prospect recognizes they have an issue or opportunity but hasn't taken action yet.  
   * **Action:** Prospect has taken some initial steps but hasn't committed fully to a solution.  
   * **Desire:** Prospect actively wants to solve a problem or achieve a goal and seeks clear pathways.  
   * **Belief:** Prospect believes your solution is potentially the right fit but still needs assurance or clarification.


2. **Clearly state the identified conversation level**, briefly summarizing why it fits based on previous interactions.  

3. **Write three conversational, open-ended or multiple-choice micro-commitment questions tailored specifically to the identified level.**  

   * Clearly indicate recommended insertion points ("ladder rungs") within the natural flow of the conversation.  
   * Questions should logically move from easy engagement to slightly deeper inquiry without appearing choreographed.



4. **Provide a brief, clear rationale (1 sentence each)** explaining why each question effectively builds natural momentum toward the desired outcome.  

5. \*\*Include a simple, seamless pivot statement, example structure (doesn't have to be this exact way every time, but this is what I mean by seamless, so should have this essence):

"I'm thinking that maybe a quick strategy session to \[something irresistible based on conversation context\] could help you sort this out faster than messaging back and forth.

Do you think it'd be helpful to hop on a quick 15 minute call this week to \[specific desired action\]?"

This pivot should naturally transition from the final micro-commitment question to scheduling a call or the next step, ensuring it feels conversational and unforced.

**Expected Outcomes:**

* Increased natural engagement and response rate  
* Reduced prospect resistance  
* Improved likelihood of booking calls

**Practical Next Steps:**

* Immediately integrate these questions into ongoing conversations.  
* Maintain a conversational tone and natural language.  
* Ensure questions address pain points directly tied to the benefit of your resource without explicitly referencing it.

**Additional Rules:**

* **Be Simple and Clear:** Write conversationally, as if speaking with a friend in a professional setting.  
* **No Complex Words:** Use everyday language only.  
* **Avoid Analogies, Emojis, or Hashtags.**  
* **Apply Active Voice:** Be direct and straightforward.  
* **Add Conversational Elements:** Include natural dialogue markers like "Just curious," or "Quick question."  
* **Vary Sentence Structure:** Mix short and medium-length sentences for natural rhythm.  
* **Remove Technical Language:** Keep the conversation accessible and clear.  
* **Cut Unnecessary Words:** Avoid redundant words or overly descriptive phrases.

**Final Instruction:** Take your time and process the instructions carefully. Once you are sure you understand, ask me for the following necessary details to effectively complete the prompt:

**Prospect Name:** (for personalized messaging)

**Previous Conversation Context:**

* Last conversation topic:  
* Specific interests or goals mentioned:  
* Any particular challenges or concerns raised:

**Preferred Outcome:** (select one)

* Getting a simple reply  
* Restarting a more engaged dialogue  
* Scheduling a follow-up call or meeting

**Brief description of your offer:** (transformation or specific outcome provided)

**Target audience for your offer:** (e.g., high-ticket coaches, wellness entrepreneurs, etc.)

**Any unique points or key benefits of the offer:** (important details influencing conversation flow)

    Here’s how this works:

    Let's say your lead just said, "I'm interested," after reviewing your offer.

    That puts them at the Desire stage—ready to move, just unsure how.

    Here's how my prompt responds:

    • Question 1: "What's making this feel urgent for you right now?" → Gets them to name the pain clearly.

    • Question 2: "Have you already tried anything to solve this?" → Shows you care about their effort and avoids repeating past mistakes.

    • Question 3: "If this was resolved in the next 30 days, what would that unlock for you?" → Helps them connect emotionally to the outcome.

    • Natural Pivot: "I think mapping this out together might help you move faster. Want to grab 15 minutes this week to talk it through?"

    See how natural that feels?

    By the time you suggest the call, they've already:

    • Identified their pain point (Question 1)

    • Admitted they need help (Question 2)

    • Visualized success (Question 3)

    The "yes" to the call becomes inevitable because you've built psychological momentum through the Yes Ladder.

    And this same framework adapts to any conversation level—whether they're at Situation, Action, Desire, or Belief stage.

    The key is each question gets a small commitment that makes the next "yes" easier.

    Pro Tip: Their answers give you exactly what to say on the discovery call.

    What If They Say "No"?

    Even with the Yes Ladder, you'll still hit resistance.

    But the beauty of this approach is that a "no" to a small question gives you valuable intel without killing the conversation.

    • If they say no to Question 1 ("What's making this feel urgent?"): They might not feel urgency yet. Step back and ask about their general situation instead: "What's your biggest challenge with [topic] right now?"

    • If they say no to Question 2 ("Have you tried anything to solve this?"): Great intel—they haven't taken action yet. You can position yourself as their first real attempt at a solution.

    • If they say no to Question 3 ("What would success unlock for you?"): They might not see the value clearly. Provide a quick insight or example before circling back.

    Unlike jumping straight to "Want a call?" (where "no" ends everything), the ladder lets you pivot and rebuild momentum.

    Each "no" is information, not rejection.

    You learn where they are mentally and can adjust your approach accordingly.

    Plus, if they say "no" to booking the call after saying "yes" to three smaller commitments, you know exactly what objection to address—because they've already told you their situation, urgency, and desired outcome.

    The ladder doesn't guarantee every conversation converts.

    But it gives you multiple chances to course-correct instead of one make-or-break moment.
