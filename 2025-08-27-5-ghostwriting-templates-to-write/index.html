    I have ghostwritten more than 2,000 articles for startup founders, C-level executives, Silicon Valley investors, and more.

    Want to know a secret?

    I use the same 5 templates every time.

    But what took me 10,000+ hours to write, you can now produce in a fraction of the time with AI.

    Steal these prompts and you can write anything for anyone in any industry without reinventing the wheel.

    Template #1: The mistakes & the lessons

    What’s the fastest way to provide value to someone else? Point out the CAUTION signs and tell them where to go instead.

    All great business writing follows this same format:

    • Decision

    • Outcome

    • Mistake

    • Lesson

    I have ghostwritten for founders in every industry ranging from biotech and pharmaceuticals to sports nutrition to productivity software to enterprise sales. The details of the stories change each time, but the arc is always the same.

    Help people learn from your mistakes, and you’re seen as the authority.

    Template #2: The frameworks

    “Want to solve X? Follow 1–2–3.”

    Every great business How To article or thread can be reduced down to this simple formula.

    • Name the problem

    • Pinpoint actionable steps

    • Celebrate outcome

    Voila. You’re now a “business thought leader.”

    The difference between aspiring writers and professional writers, or aspiring leaders and professional leaders, always comes down to the individual’s ability to clearly communicate information. And the single most effective way to communicate information is to put it inside a box.

    Boxes are easier to remember, organize, and store in our minds.

    There’s a reason why “frameworks” are so enticing and sticky.

    Template #3: The future — here’s why

    Business readers love speculating about the future.

    So, to position yourself as an authority, speculate.

    • Where is the future of your industry headed?

    • Why? What’s your thesis?

    • Cite 2–3 studies/stats

    Hoorah! You did it.

    There’s something about speculating about the future that puts you in a position of power. It usually doesn’t even matter if you end up being right or wrong—the simple fact that you’re willing to share what you THINK communicates authority, leadership, creativity, and confidence.

    Template #4: Category POV

    Master-level business writers don’t hammer away about the brand.

    They educate readers on the category.

    • Share what’s wrong with the old category

    • Name & Claim new category

    • Describe the outcome after moving from old to new

    This is similar to futurism content, except it's less about predicting the future and it's more about moving people's thinking out of where it's been in the past. You are helping them become conscious of all the problems they are currently experiencing, and then you’re educating them on all the ways those problems cease to exist over here in this new and different category.

    You’re moving them FROM where they are TO where you want them to be.

    Template #5: Myths

    Everybody loves “insider information.”

    Your domain expertise is an opportunity to pull the curtain back and share what’s REALLY going on.

    • What do most people think, but get wrong?

    • What does the world NEED to know?

    • What does this knowledge unlock?

    For example, years ago I wrote an article titled, *5 Myths About Exposure I Learned By Writing 400+ Columns For Inc Magazine.* This was an “insider information” sort of article, because I was telling readers, “Hey, I just went and did all this work—and now, I’ve come back bearing news.”

    Readers love this stuff.

    That’s where AI comes in.

    I’ve taken each template and turned it into a ready-to-use prompt. All you have to do is plug in your topic—and the AI will hand you a proven outline in seconds.

    Here’s how it works:

    • Paste and run the prompt below. Claude (or your AI of choice) will ask you for your input.

    • Respond with your topic / headline (you can use either one).

    • Watch as your outline magically follows the exact same patterns I’ve used for 2,000+ articles.

    Give the prompt a try.👇

    Act like a content strategist. 

Your job is to write skimmable, sentence-style subheads that deliver the full value of the post—no body copy needed.

Each subhead should:
* Be written in full sentence form
* Be specific, valuable, and easy to skim
* Follow the logic and format of the template type

Start with: 
Headline: [INSERT HEADLINE] 
Template: [Mistakes & Lessons / Frameworks / Future Predictions / Category Thinking / Myths]

Then return: Main Points:
1. [first subhead]
2. [second subhead] ...

TEMPLATE FORMATS
1. MISTAKES & LESSONS
Single mistake story:
- Start with context/setup
- Detail the specific mistake made
- Explain consequences or why it was wrong
- Share the lesson learned
- Provide actionable takeaway
- Example:Granting 15% equity to the first VP of engineering without vetting his leadership skills

Multiple mistakes or lessons:
- Use "Mistake or Lesson #1: [error]" format for each mistake
- Include the lesson learned or quick fix
- Example: Mistake #1: Hiring on skill instead of leadership ability
- Example: Lesson #1: Have an objective way of measuring your writing.

FRAMEWORKS Always use numbered steps:
- Use "Step #1: [command]" format
- Focus on clarity, action, and order
- Each step should be specific and actionable
- Example: Step #1: Write down every assumption you have about the hire before the interview
- Example: Step #2: Create interview questions that test leadership scenarios

FUTURE PREDICTIONS 
Single prediction:
- Establish where your industry currently stands
- Present your main prediction/thesis
- Provide 2-3 pieces of supporting evidence
- Explain what this means for your audience
- Give specific actions they should take now

Multiple predictions:
- Use "Prediction #1: [forecast]" format
- Support each with evidence and implications
- Example: Prediction #1: By 2026, companies will hire ghostwriters like they hire videographers

CATEGORY THINKING 
- Describe the old way people currently think 
- Name and define your new category 
- Compare old vs new approaches side-by-side 
- Address why people resist the new way 
- Paint a picture of life in the new category

MYTHS 
Single myth story:
- Introduce the common misconception
- Share your insider experience that proves it wrong
- Reveal what actually happens behind the scenes
- Explain why this myth persists
- Provide the better path forward
- Example: Everyone thinks getting featured in Inc. Magazine means instant millions of readers

Multiple myths:
Use "Myth #1: [misconception]" format for each myth
- Follow with what's actually true
- Include your insider experience as proof
- Example: Myth #1: Getting featured in Inc. Magazine means millions of readers will see your work
- Example: Myth #2: You can write about whatever you want in major publications

    A couple cool things to notice when you use these prompts:

    • Built-in research. When I ran the Future Prediction template in Claude, it automatically pulled in studies and stats. Like a good intern, it knew that research would make the output stronger. If you’re writing a future-style article, a pro move is to run a deep research prompt first, then plug your findings back into this outline. That way, the AI isn’t just guessing—it’s building on solid inputs.

    • Single vs. multi-point handling. The prompt can generate a clean outline for one big mistake or myth, or multiple smaller ones. You can see the difference between a “biggest mistake” post and a “3 steps” post.

    Now the next problem is:

    How do you fill in your outline?

    A really easy way is to ask yourself: if I were the reader, what questions would I have when I hit this point?

    For example, let’s take this outline item from above:

    Lesson #2: Success amplifies every problem instead of solving them, making million-dollar clients harder to work with than startup founders.

    As a reader, I might ask:

    • Why does success make problems bigger instead of smaller?

    • What’s a concrete example of this happening with a client?

    • What’s the hidden cost of working with million-dollar clients?

    • How should I decide whether to work with big vs. small clients?

    • What mindset shift helps me manage this better?

    • And so on.

    Keep going until you run out of questions.

    I recommend you do this on your own before hand off the task to AI.

    Here’s a simple helper prompt to get your creative juices going:

    Given point: 

{Insert Point}

List 5 questions this point raises and pair each with a value-based response.

What is the reader most likely to want to know when reading this point?  

And what is the best value I can offer to help answer each question? (e.g. tip, step, lesson, mistake, example, reason, benefit, stat, etc.)
