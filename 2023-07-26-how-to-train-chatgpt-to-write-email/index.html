    Ahoy, Digital Writers!

    Today we are going to ask ChatGPT to help us write email subject lines that lead to higher open rates.

    If you are writing an email newsletter, sending marketing emails, or communicating with your boss at work, it’s important to understand how your content is going to arrive in someone’s inbox.

    Let’s take a look.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9F25!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F014bf09c-6b4d-4d08-889c-ba318a527917_2292x290.png)

    • The first part is the Subject Line. This is the bolded sentence you read first when you see an email arrive. It’s the hook.

    • The second part is the Subtext Line. This is the grey sentence you read second, usually giving a bit more context as to what is “inside” the email—so someone knows what they’re about to click on. It’s like a “Whisper” to the reader. “Hey… pssst…. here’s what’s inside” sentence.

    Nailing the combination of the Subject and Subtext is the difference between a 45% open rate and 5% open rate. And if you a follow a few simple rules and enlist the help of ChatGPT, you’ll have everything you need to fire off an irresistible email today!

    Let’s dive in!

    The 5 Golden Rules For Writing Subject Lines

    By far, the biggest mistake writers make when writing subject lines is making them “too long.”

    If a subject line gets cut off before “the hook” is revealed, the chances of a reader clicking is very low. Why? Because they don't have a reason to! They don't know what you're promising inside—which means they don't know what to expect (or why it's worth their precious attention).

    Which leads us to Rule #1.

    Rule #1: 5-15 words, max.

    A good rule of thumb is your subject line should not exceed 15 words. Ever.

    Less is always more here. If you can say it in 15 words, try to say it in 10. If you can say it in 10 words, try to say it in 7. If you can say it in 7, try to say it in 5 words. However, “less words” doesn't inherently mean the subject line is “better.” The key is to give the reader a “hook” that makes them want to click and open the email—but to say that “hook” in as few words as possible WITHOUT LOSING THE MEANING.

    For example:

    Take the subject line: “The 1 thing you need to do to strengthen the microbiome in your gut,” shortened to: “The 1 thing you need to do.” Yes, it’s shortened (which is good), but the whole idea in the “hook” is lost (which is bad).

    A better rewrite would be something that says the same thing, but in less words, like: “Want to strengthen your gut microbiome? Try this 1 thing.”

    Same idea, but the first subject line is 16 words.

    And the second subject line is 10 words.

    Rule #2: Subject lines should read like you're talking to a friend.

    This comes down to grammar & punctuation more than anything else.

    For example:

    • “3 Mental Models To Quantify Your Financial Success As A Writer”

    • “3 mental models to quantify your financial success as a writer”

    In general, the second subject line here will see better open rates.

    Why?

    Because your inbox is personal. And personal feels relevant (interesting to open) for the reader. Think about it. If you’re writing an email to a friend, are you going to capitalize every word in the subject line?

    Probably not.

    Rule #3: Use 1 of the 4 proven hooks.

    There are 4 types of hooks that work over and over again (regardless of industry, niche, or topic):

    • "Here's a ton of value for minimal time." - Your entire Life Coach career path blueprint… in 1 email!

    • "Here's a ton of value for minimal cost." - Here's our entire $1M marketing plan. Steal it!

    • "Here's how to solve your problem without much effort." - Losing money in the stock market? Just buy these 3 companies.

    • "Here's how to unlock this desirable outcome, instantly." - 3 keys to land $10,000 consulting clients, from home, in your pajamas, right now.

    As much as possible, you always want your subject lines to use 1 of these 4 proven hooks.

    Otherwise, the reader won't have a good enough reason to click, open, and ultimately read your email.

    Rule #4: Use visceral language!

    Now, you can “amp up” your subject lines even more by adding in visceral language.

    Things like…

    • URGENT!

    • Steal this!

    • Hemorrhage

    • Gut-wrenching

    • (I can't believe I'm giving this away.)

    And so on.

    For example:

    • (Original) Losing money in the stock market? Just buy these 3 companies.

    • (Amped Up) Hemorrhaging cash in the stock market? Just buy these 3 companies.

    It's a very small thing, but sometimes adding in visceral language can help increase open rates by a few percentage points.

    Rule #5: Name & Claim ideas (in the subject line)

    Even more than visceral language though, the ultimate goal is to Name & Claim ideas right there in the subject line.

    For example, notice the difference between these two subject lines:

    • (V1) 3 ways to make more money as a life coach

    • (V2, Amped Up) 3 easy ways to make 10x more money as a life coach (nobody tells you this).

    • (V3, Named & Claimed) Life Coach Money Manual: 3 easy ways to 10x your monthly earnings (nobody tells you this)

    You should be able to feel (in your skin!) how much more you want to click on the V3 Subject Line—because “Life Coach Money Manual” feels like you're receiving a “thing.” (Which makes it feel exponentially more “valuable.”)

    This is the “game” of writing subject lines…on your own.

    Now that you know the framework, let’s automate the steps using ChatGPT and write 12 proven hooks!

    We are going to break this into 3 simple steps.

    Let’s go!

    Step 1: Train ChatGPT on the 4 proven hooks

    “Proven hooks” are the meat of this prompt.

    We tell ChatGPT our goal: “capture the audience’s attention using an email subject line.” And then we give it the hooks with a few examples, and then we break out the rules we want our intern to follow.

    I want to write an email subject line to capture my target audience's attention.

Here's what we are going to do:

I will give you a topic, an audience (FOR WHO), and outcome that audience desires (SO THAT).  

You will create 3 email subject lines for each type of the 4 proven hooks. 

Here are the 4 proven hooks:
    1. A ton of value for minimal time.
    2. A ton of value for minimal cost.
    3. How to solve your problem without much effort.
    4. How to unlock a desirable outcome, instantly.

Some good examples to follow:
- "Your entire Life Coach career path blueprint… in 1 email!"
- "Here's our entire $1M marketing plan. Steal it!"
- "Losing money in the stock market? Just buy these 3 companies."
- "3 keys to land $10,000 consulting clients, from home, in your pajamas, right now."
- "5 steps to write your first viral Twitter thread as a Digital Creator (even if you have 0 Followers)"

A few rules:
- Rule #1: Use 15 words, max.
- Rule #2: The subject line should read like you're talking to a friend. (use sentence case)
- Rule #3: Use visceral TANGIBLE language
- Rule #4: Intrigue the reader
- Rule #5: Use numbers
- Rule #6: Be super specific

Remember: DO NOT exceed the word count limit for effective subject lines. 

Do you understand?

    Step 2: Give ChatGPT the Topic an audience (FOR WHO), and an outcome that audience desires (SO THAT) you’d like it to use as the “input” to write 12 irresistible email subject lines.

    These subject lines are solid on their own, but we want to amp things up even more by Naming & Claiming the idea in each subject line.

    Step 3: Train ChatGPT to Name & Claim each subject line.

    Great! Now, I want you to "Name & Claim" each subject line.

The reader should feel like they're receiving a "thing." 

The email should feel exponentially more "valuable."

Named & Claimed Examples:
- "Life Coach Money Manual: 3 easy ways to 10x your monthly earnings (nobody tells you this)"
- "Substack Starter Pack: 5-day crash course to launch your newsletter and quickly get your first 100 subscribers (before even publishing your first post)"

    Boom!

    Notice the weight behind each of these subject lines.

    They feel substantial—like there’s so much more included in the email. You aren’t just getting a list of “7 steps,” you are getting an “Instant Serenity Kit.” Yes, please!
