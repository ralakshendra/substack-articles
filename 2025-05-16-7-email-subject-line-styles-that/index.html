This week’s series is written by Parker Worth—email copywriter and creator of Email 365. He helps entrepreneurs build an email list that earns $2–$5 per subscriber, every single month.

Harsh truth:

You could spend two hours writing the most useful, emotional, persuasive email ever…

But if your subject line doesn’t get the open?

Nobody will ever read it.

It’s like handing someone a winning lottery ticket — then dropping it down a storm drain.

Subject lines are 80% of the game.

So let’s stack the odds in your favor.

Your Subject Line = Your Hook

It’s not a headline.

It’s not a slogan.

It’s the hook that stops someone mid-scroll in their inbox.

Your goal is to make someone pause and say: “Wait… what’s this about?” And when done right, your subject line turns your email from background noise into the main event.

A killer subject line does two things:

  1. It hints at a benefit. For example, “*How to get your first 10 sales (without a big list)” Clear benefit and it removes a mental obstacle.

  2. It creates curiosity. “THIS almost killed my launch” What’s “this”? What happened? Instant curiosity.

If you’ve got one or both?

You’re golden.

The 7 Email Subject Line Styles That Consistently Deliver Higher Open Rates

You don’t need 1,000 templates.

You only need these 7:

  1. Curiosity “THIS changed everything for my business.”

  2. Pain “Still can’t convert traffic into buyers?”

  3. Benefit “How to 2X your leads in 30 days”

  4. Story “I accidentally ordered d*ck cheese” (yes, deliverability took a hit. but replies went crazy.)

  5. Question “Do you make these content mistakes?”

  6. Contrarian “Why storytelling WON’T grow your brand (and what will)”

  7. Proof “How I grew to 70,000 followers in one year”

Use these to match your email type.

  • Sending a story? Use a story subject.

  • Giving advice? Use benefit or question.

  • Writing to sell? Use pain or proof.

Mix them up. Shuffle them often.

They never go stale.

Here’s what NOT to do:

  • Don’t write “Newsletter #3” (instant death)

  • Don’t ask boring yes/no questions

  • Don’t try to be too clever (you are not Hemingway or ChatGPT.)

Your reader isn’t grading you.

They’re skimming for something interesting.

So, give them something that stops the scroll.

How To Instantly Write 100’s Of Mouth-Watering Subject Lines With ChatGPT Or Claude

Drop this prompt into your favorite LLM:

# Context
You are an expert direct-response email marketer who has analyzed thousands of high-performing email campaigns. 

You understand that subject lines are crucial - they determine whether an email gets opened or ignored. You excel at writing subject lines that grab attention, create curiosity, and get readers to open emails.

# Role
As an email subject line expert, your job is to generate multiple irresistible subject line options based on the email content or topic provided. Each subject line should be written to maximize open rates by using proven psychological triggers.

# Task
Generate 8-10 diverse, high-converting subject line options based on the email content, topic, or draft the user provides. Each subject line should follow one of these proven formats:

1. Curiosity-based: Create intrigue with "this" or unexpected elements
2. Benefit-focused: Show clear value without giving everything away
3. Story-based: Hint at an interesting narrative
4. Urgency-based: Create time sensitivity (use sparingly)
5. Question-based: Ask something relevant that prompts thought
6. Contrarian/Controversial: Challenge common beliefs
7. Social proof/Results: Showcase outcomes and success
8. Humor: Use wit to stand out (when appropriate)
9. Pain-focused: Highlight a problem your audience faces
10. Personalized: Make it feel custom-made for the reader

For each subject line, briefly explain which type it is and why it works.

# Guidelines

- Keep subject lines under 60 characters when possible
- Avoid ALL CAPS and excessive punctuation (!!!)
- Don't use "clickbait" tactics that overpromise
- Incorporate the recipient's name when appropriate using the {name} tag
- For curiosity, use the word "this" to create mystery
- For contrarian angles, challenge common advice in your industry
- For benefit-focused lines, highlight specific results
- For pain points, address specific frustrations your audience has

# Examples
Curiosity examples:

"Do THIS before bed to boost productivity"
"This changed everything for my business"
"Stop doing THIS (it's killing your conversions)"

Benefit examples:

"How to 2X your leads in 30 days"
"Get your next 1,000 followers fast"
"Lose 5 pounds this week (without starving)"

Story examples:

"I accidentally ordered **** cheese"
"Social media nearly ruined my business. Here's why:"
"240 volts showed me how to quit my job"

Urgency examples:

"Doors close in 24 hours"
"Final call: Save 50% on enrollment"
"Tonight only: get this free training"

Question examples:

"Do you make these content mistakes?"
"Struggling to lose stubborn fat?"
"Is your marketing strategy outdated?"

Contrarian examples:

"Stop following fitness influencers"
"Why hustle culture is killing your business"
"Why storytelling WON'T grow your brand (and what will)"

Social proof examples:

"Why 10,000 creators joined this program"
"How I grew to 70,000 followers in one year"
"From zero to $10k/month (proof inside)"

# Output Format

Provide 10 subject line options. 1 for each type.

Include:
- The subject line itself
- A brief explanation of why it works
- The psychological trigger it employs

Begin by analyzing the content or topic provided to understand the core message and desired outcome of the email. Then ask me any questions that what help you generate a better result (and avoid guessing). Finally ask me if I want to generate more of any 1 type.

# TOPIC

{INSERT YOUR EMAIL TOPIC/IDEA HERE}

Run it once. Then again with a different topic. Save the good ones.

Use them for future emails — or swipe the best ones for social posts too.

Pro tip:

If you're stuck? Use “THIS.”

It’s the most powerful curiosity word in subject lines.

  • “THIS made me $10K last month”

  • “Avoid THIS disaster at all costs”

  • “I almost lost it all… then THIS saved me”

It works because it withholds the payoff — and the brain can’t stand open loops.

But don’t overdo it. “THIS fatigue” is real. Shuffle often.
