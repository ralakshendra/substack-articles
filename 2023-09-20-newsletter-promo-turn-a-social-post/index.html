Ahoy, Digital Writers!

Think you need a massive following to get newsletter sign-ups?

Not true.

The reality is, even with a small audience, you could be engaging more people and getting more of your current readers to read.

  • Everyone is not subscribed to your newsletter.

  • And you probably don’t have a 100% open rate either.

Which is why every time you publish a newsletter, a long-form article, or even launch a new product, you don’t want to ship it and forget it—you want to promote it!

So, today we are going to use ChatGPT to turn your newsletter into a promotional social post that attracts readers, grows your influence, and let's not kid ourselves, makes you more money.

And no, you won't come off as spammy.

But first…

Are You Leaving Newsletter Subs On The Table?

Your newsletter is like a party invitation.

People come to parties for various reasons: to have fun, to network, and to be seen. And when you promote your newsletter on social it should be like an irresistible invitation that appeals to your audience's deepest desires.

Here’s the thing:

If you're not promoting (inviting) people to your work, who will?

Always promote your newsletter on social.

The *Right* Way To Promote Your Newsletter

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Y32v!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0b2e7754-0a3c-4114-8b1d-9441e5ed00cc_972x1282.png)

If you want some to acquire a newsletter subscriber from social, you want to avoid things like:

  • “Check out my newsletter”

  • “Here’s a great post that everyone should read.”

  • Or worse, just the link and zero context (It’s not 2015).

This is a bad invitation.

Because if you don’t give people a reason to click thru, read, and subscribe—they will ignore you. And they will see you as “spammy.” It’s like telling someone, “Come to my party!,” without giving them any other details like the time, the reason, the attire, the guest list, etc.

Offering value compels people to read further.

Instead, you want to hook the reader with a big benefit, call attention to their desirable outcome, and agitate their problem while teasing the solution (reading your newsletter).

This is what drives readers to get curious and click!

Turn A Newsletter Into An Irresistible Hook Using ChatGPT

Writing a social post that leverages something you have already created is the perfect task for a Digital Intern.

You have already done the thinking. All you need to do now is have your intern rearrange / repurpose your ideas into a format that works on social. You can do this on your own, but let’s be honest, writing hooks is an art and it takes time to learn. And this prompt will get you started.

We are going to tackle this in 5 steps.

Here we go:

Step 1: Grab A Newsletter

First, open up your newsletter platform (Substack, Beehiv, ConvertKit, etc.) and locate your most recent newsletter.

If you aren’t sure which one to choose, pick the one with the most likes, comments, or opens. Why? Because if it resonated once, it will resonate again! In most cases, you will write and publish your newsletter on one day and then direct traffic to it on social a couple days later. For example, we publish our “Start Writing Online” newsletter on Monday’s and then we promote it on Tuesday and Wednesday of the same week.

Once you've selected your newsletter, leave the window open so you can copy/paste it into the ChatGPT prompt in the next step.

Step 2: Input The Newsletter Into ChatGPT

Open ChatGPT and run the below prompt to get started.

You're an excellent copywriter, specialized in writing punchy newsletter summaries and reviews.

I'm going to share with you the title, the URL, and the body of a newsletter breaking down a ChatGPT prompt designed to help writers create engaging content, faster.

And I want your help writing a text that summarizes the newsletter following these rules:

1. Write 3 different texts summarizing the newsletter in 3-4 sentences. Start each text with a strong, declarative statement that highlights the benefit or desirable outcome of solving the problem our newsletter solves. Then, mention the problem and explain why most people usually struggle to solve it. Finally, tease the way we help them solve the problem with the newsletter post.

2. Next,add the title/headline of the newsletter written in italics and followed by a ":"

3. Finally, add the newsletter URL as a hyperlink to the title of the blog post using markdown. (The blog post title itself should link to the blog post URL, we do NOT want to paste the full URL next/after the title).

When writing the summary, please be sure to sure simple words, short sentences, and to speak directly to the reader when it makes sense. Also, avoid using emojis.

Are you ready for me to provide the blog post information?

After you run the first prompt, you will need to run a 2nd prompt with 3 things:

Okay, here's the information:

• Title: {Newsletter Title}

• URL: {Newsletter URL}

• Body: {The Newsletter!}

Drop the title, url, and newsletter text into the prompt and run it!

If your newsletter is really long, consider summarizing it or focusing on the key points. ChatGPT has a token limit, and you don't want to hit that ceiling before the AI has a chance to work its magic.

Let’s keep going.

Step 3: Choose Your Output

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!iiEU!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3c40d270-0bf1-4ebe-9baa-e295534a4f73_1078x1184.png)

You wouldn't buy a car without test-driving a few, right?

Multiple outputs give you options. This approach helps you avoid the tunnel vision of settling for the first thing that comes out, which may not always be the best fit for your audience.

Don't just skim through them; scrutinize each one.

Look for outputs that capture the essence of your newsletter while being engaging and concise. Remember, you want to hook the reader—short, sweet, and to the point. Run the prompt multiple times if you're not satisfied with the first set of outputs.

Pick the one that resonates with you the most, or mix and match to your liking.

Step 4: Publish On X, LinkedIn, or Threads

Before you hit “Publish:, there's one last thing to consider: adaptation.

Different social channels have different rules, and character limits. For example, if you're posting on X (Twitter), target your post to land in the 280-character limit. If it's LinkedIn, you've got more room to breathe, but keep focus on the 3-5 line above-the-fold cutoff.

Pro Tip: Add an image your post (don’t let the platform generate an image preview for you). When you add an image to your post with a link in it, the image will override any link preview on LinkedIn or X (Twitter).The reason this is a worthwhile tactic to experiment with is because sometimes algorithms give more (or less) priority to “image” content opposed to “text” content.

There’s no guarantee that sharing an image means your content is automatically going to perform “better,” however it is fun to experiment with these different forms of native content to see how things perform—and hypothesize on your own about why.

Once your post it ready, hit publish!

Step 5: Review Your Analytics

Once your post is live, give it some time to marinate and then dive into your analytics.

  • How many impressions did the post get it?

  • How many likes?

  • How many engagements?

For a newsletter promotion, you want to focus on engagements (“clicks”)—since you goal is to get readers on social interested in your newsletter post.

Unfortunately LinkedIn and X give preference “linkless” posts (unless you are paying for an advertising). Which means, impressions on a promotional post with a link will be lower than other posts in your feed. Anything that directs attention away from their platforms gets squashed by the algorithm.

But don’t let it deter you from publishing!

That’s it!
