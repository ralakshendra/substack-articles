    5 Steps To Build A Short-Form Writer GPT

    Let’s run through the basics of building a GPT.

    For an in-depth guide, check out Zapier’s step-by-step resource here.

    Below are the steps I followed.

    Step 1: Decide What To Create

    I suggest you start with a prompt you already use. The advantage here is you won’t be starting from scratch. The tighter the focus the better. Don’t attempt to create a GPT that does everything: how to write, short-form, long-form, landing pages, bios, hooks, etc. You will never finish it. Keep it tight.

    I chose writing Short-Form content for X.

    Step 2: Explore!

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!KGqK!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F68b26325-a65b-4f66-a19b-ab8f42de978c_1480x648.png)

    ChatGPT encourages it. Click the Explore button in the left hand nav. 🙂 Poke around and see what other people have created.

    (Unfortunately, there’s not a way to study the prompts behind other public GPTs.)

    Step 3: Create A GPT

    OpenAI walks you through the process using, you guessed it, a chat! I followed the GPT Builder prompts my first time through. I recommend you do the same—it will help you understand how it works. During the process, the GPT Builder does not show you how or why you should upload data files like writing samples, knowledge deep dives, or templates. But you should. It will make your GPT more powerful.

    More data = better results.

    Step 4: Test Your GPT

    The GPT Builder will prompt when it thinks it has all the foundational pieces and will ask you if you are ready to test. At this point, you’ve named your ChatGPT, given it an avatar, and established it’s purpose. It’s enough test. But I made the mistake of continuing to give it more and more information, only to find out that the AI was “simplifying” my instructions for me (not what I wanted). Don’t do this.

    Which takes us to the next step.

    Step 5: Engineer And Publish Your GPT

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!-YZF!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3a6d7381-daf6-4f10-9807-6157fb0d479f_1388x542.png)

    This is where the real work begins. It feels a lot like normal prompt writing. Changing language, re-ordering steps, yelling at AI when it doesn’t do what you want (then realizing it’s not AI’s fault). I spent most of my time tweaking the Instructions and the Conversation Starters before finally deciding to ship it, share it, and get feedback!

    That’s it. It’s dang simple.

    Note: GPTs offer an advanced “Action” feature. These allow you to create automations and write custom code for your GPT to act on. It’s a plug-in system that allows GPT to interact with external data.

    3 Lessons To Build A Better GPT (On The First Go)

    These are the 3 most important things I believe will help you get better results:

    Upload Reference Files

    Put your best work into a text file and upload it as Knowledge for the GPT Builder.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!emUh!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F770248ea-e15d-4194-80bb-4cbdb22d4736_1394x360.png)

    This could be example short-form content, templates, rules to follow, anything you want the AI to “study” and repeat. (I added 100+ samples of my tweets.) This is the best way to teach AI how to write like you.

    Without the text file, AI will generalize how it writes for you.

    Train AI How To Use Reference Files

    Just like writing prompts, you need to teach AI what to do with your reference files.

    For example, if you want AI to follow a particular format, you can describe it in a prompt, or add examples in a .txt file. Then reference your text file in your prompt instructions.

    “Format your output following the guidelines and examples in Format.txt.”

    Don’t Let AI Define The Conversation Starters

    Conversation Starters are the light grey buttons you see above the chat dialog.

    The GPT Builder will create them for you. This is helpful because it give you insight into your prompt definition. If the conversation starters align to the purpose of your GPT, you are on the right track.

    There are 2 mistakes you can make here.

    1. Not using them.

    2. Not changing them.

    In both cases, when you don’t use the conversation starter, you open the conversation to a world of possibilities. And AI with a blank slate is…robotic. Instead you want to give your GPT users a clue as to what they can do. “Help me do X.” And align them with the design of your prompt.

    For example, in my Short-Form writer, I wanted it do 2 things: write short-form, and repurpose text into short-form. That was my goal. Anything else is a gamble (that’s a hint).
