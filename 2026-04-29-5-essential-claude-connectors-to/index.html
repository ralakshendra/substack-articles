Write With AI is the leading paid newsletter for ChatGPT & Claude prompts, and how to turn AI platforms into your own personal Digital Writing Assistant. If you want to stay on the cutting-edge in the new AI Economy, then you’re in the right place. New posts every Wednesday & Sunday.

Claude is one of the most valuable tools in your tool stack.

But most writers are still using it like a vending machine.

  • Punch in a prompt

  • Grab the output

  • Move on

That works fine for one-off tasks.

But the real unlock is connectors: direct lines from Claude to the tools where your writing already lives. Once they’re set up, Claude stops being a chatbot you visit and starts being the studio you work inside.

Here are 5 connectors to upgrade your writing workflow:

Connector #1: Google Drive

Every doc, swipe file, transcript, and old draft you’ve ever written becomes searchable from inside Claude.

The non-obvious use isn’t pulling old work—it’s finding what you haven’t written by asking Claude to give you a fresh take on an old piece of writing.

Try this prompt before your next newsletter:

Search my Drive for documents I’ve created in the last {NUMBER} months that mention {TOPIC}.

List the title, date, and a one-sentence summary of the specific take or claim each piece makes about the topic.

Then suggest 5 new angles:

  • Tips

  • Steps

  • Lessons

  • Reasons

  • Mistakes

For each, tell me:

  1. The angle in one sentence

  2. Why it’s a logical next piece given what I’ve already written

  3. One specific hook or opening line.

You’ll never sit down to a blank page on a topic you know well again.

Connector #2: Gmail

Your sent folder is the largest sample of your real voice that exists anywhere.

Because in your email sent folder, you weren’t trying to sound like a writer. You were just writing.

The Gmail connector lets Claude study that voice and then write in it.

Try this prompt:

Search my Gmail for emails I sent in the last 60 days that are between 100 and 500 words long.

Pick 20 of them at random.

Return three things:

  1. Phrase Bank: 10 specific phrases or sentence openers that appear in 3+ of those emails.

  2. Sentence Patterns: 5 patterns in how I structure sentences (e.g., ‘short fragment + longer expansion + one-line punchline’).

  3. Openers & Closers: My 3 most common ways to open an email and 3 most common ways to close.

Give me a “real” quote for each.

Save the output.

Feed it back to Claude as a guide for every future writing prompt.

Connector #3: Canva

This one’s the time-saver.

Newsletter thumbnails, social graphics, lead magnet covers — generated and previewed inside the chat where you wrote the copy.

Which means you never have to leave Claude.

All you have to do is write and then ask for a visual.

For writers who hate the design step, this is the connector that gives you back the most time per week.

Create 3 newsletter thumbnail variations for an issue titled “{HEADLINE}.”

Constraints for all 3:

  • 1200x630px (newsletter / social share size)

  • Headline text must be readable at thumbnail size (large, high contrast)

  • One accent color, not multiple

  • No stock-photo people or generic AI imagery

Vary them by: typography style (serif vs. sans-serif vs. monospace), background (solid color vs. textured vs. gradient), and layout (centered vs. left-aligned).

Connector #4: Notion

If your second brain lives in Notion, this connector turns it from a graveyard into a generator.

Most writers have hundreds of captured ideas they never write.

Use Claude + Notion to:

  • Surface unwritten ideas tagged by theme

  • Pull customer pain points out of research databases

  • Find the half-finished outline you forgot about three months ago

  • Spot patterns across notes you didn’t realize you’d been making

The trick is to ask Claude to find the connections, not just retrieve the data.

Try this prompt:

Search my Notion: {INSERT NOTION LINK}

Return a list of pages that meet ALL of these criteria:

  • Last edited more than 30 days ago

  • Body contains at least 100 words of notes

  • Title or body contains {KEYWORDS}

For each page that qualifies, return: the title, the date last edited, the word count, and a one-sentence summary.

Rank them by word count, highest first.

The top of the list = my most-developed unwritten ideas.

Connector #5: Slack

Ask Claude to summarize the last 48 hours in your busiest channels.

Have it flag anything that genuinely needs a response from you. Draft those responses in your voice.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Lkru!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1c4716ee-ae05-402a-a665-fb1d627b3fcf_1136x516.png)

Try this prompt:

List my Slack channels by message volume in the last 48 hours, highest to lowest. Show me the top 5.

For each of those 5 channels, pull every message from the last 48 hours where:

  • I’m tagged directly (@me), OR

  • The message ends in a question mark, OR

  • The message contains the words ‘help,’ ‘stuck,’ ‘question,’ or ‘anyone’

For each match, return: the channel, the sender, the message, and a 1-sentence draft response. Mark each response ‘urgent’ (needs reply today), ‘soon’ (this week), or ‘optional’ (would be nice but not required).

Skip messages I’ve already replied to.

That’s it!

Enjoy.
