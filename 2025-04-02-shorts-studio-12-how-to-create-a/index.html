[Shorts Studio] #12 How To Create A Consistent AI Avatar For Talking Head Shorts

Author: Nicolas Cole
Date: 2025-04-02

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!lszF!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1bce3855-eda1-4cac-af40-d7efa6b40be7_1260x900.png)

Let’s talk about the character you’re going to use in your videos.

You’ve probably already seen people using avatars for faceless content—or maybe you’ve even used tools like HeyGen to generate talking head videos with your own voice.

Just like a voiceover makes a video feel human…

A well-made avatar makes it feel real—even if it’s 100% AI-generated.

Once you’ve got one solid avatar, here’s what you can do with it:

  • Drop “yourself” into any AI scene

  • Record once, reuse forever

  • Show up on low-energy days (without showing up)

Want to film in a coffee shop, but you’re home in sweats? Avatar.

Want to walk through a city, but it’s raining out? Avatar.

Want to teach a lesson, but you’ve already recorded it 12 times this week? Avatar.

Building a flexible version of YOU, helps you move faster, stay consistent, and create on your own terms. And once you have it—it’s like flipping a switch. Suddenly, you can be anywhere, anytime, teaching anything.

That’s the power of a good avatar.

But…

You can’t just regenerate a new image from scratch and hope the AI gets it right.

You need a repeatable avatar look—and the only way to get that is by giving the AI multiple, consistent reference images to work from.

Your 3 Avatar Reference Angles

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!t4Az!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbb53d430-a455-4369-853d-eb6116643017_1064x708.png)

To get 80% of the way there, all you need are 3 high-quality reference images:

  1. Front View (Head & Shoulders)

  2. Side View (Full Body or Waist-Up)

  3. Seated View OR Full-Body Standing

Optional: Back View or Mid-motion Pose (like walking)

These help your avatar look consistent across different scenes—whether they’re talking, teaching, walking, or just reacting to voiceover.

So What’s the Workflow?

There are a few different tools you can use—HeyGen, OpenArt, Sora or ChatGPT (to an extent)—but they all need the same thing:

Inputs that give the AI something to copy.

You need:

  • Clear lighting on your face

  • No filters, shadows, or sunglasses

  • Simple clothing (dark shirt, neutral jacket, solid background)

We’re going to do this with HeyGen.

Here’s what you need to do:

Step 1: Take 3 Camera Shots

These are your “master images.”

  • Head-on shot (smiling or neutral)

  • Side shot (same lighting, same clothes)

  • Full body shot or seated shot

You can take these on your iPhone—just make sure it’s well-lit and not blurry.

Save them in a folder. You’ll need them in the next step

Note: You can use more than 3 photos if you want. The important part is giving the AI enough data points to work with.

Step 2: Upload Your Photos To HeyGen

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!1-dH!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Feb55397a-410e-4c2b-8212-8f9b766c2954_1226x1576.png)

  1. Login to your HeyGen account

  2. Click on Avatars in the sidebar

  3. Click Add a New Look

  4. Choose “Photo Avatar” and upload your images

  5. Click Generate

That’s it. In 5 minutes, you just created a base avatar..

Step 3: Create “Looks” For Audio (And Video) Avatar

Once your base avatar is ready, you can add different styles by:

  • Using HeyGen’s built-in Look Packs

  • Or writing your own custom style prompts

Step 4: Animate Your Avatar

HeyGen lets you add motion to any image.

It’s like a lightweight version of what you can do in Sora.

Here’s how it works:

  • Choose your avatar look

  • Click “Add Motion”

  • Type a short prompt describing the motion or behavior

You can make it expressive (talking with hands) or subtle (nodding while listening).

Most animations are 5–10 seconds long and perfect for reaction shots, B-roll, or layering over a voiceover. But for now, focus on getting your base avatar locked in. Upload those 3 reference images. Generate 2–3 different looks. And save them.

These are the assets that’ll save you hours in your video workflow moving forward.

[Bonus]: Create A Custom Avatar With ChatGPT

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!EpOi!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2de0d611-ae94-4cbf-970e-f4f90d6e0302_1090x1076.png)

If you don’t want to use HeyGen—or if you want more control over how your avatar looks and behaves—you can use ChatGPT-4o to generate a photorealistic avatar based on your photos.

It takes a bit more setup than HeyGen, but the results are still customizable.

Here’s how it works:

  • Upload your photos. Start by uploading 3–6 clear reference images of yourself to ChatGPT-4o. (Front view, side profile, full-body, etc.—same as we did for HeyGen.)

  • Ask ChatGPT to analyze and create a base avatar prompt. Once ChatGPT analyzes the photos, it will give you a clean, specific prompt. You can then use that prompt to generate your base avatar image.

  • Generate a character reference sheet. Once your avatar is generated, ask ChatGPT to create a reference sheet with different poses to ensure visual consistency. This gives you a flexible character sheet you can use in future prompts or animations.

  • Generate scenes with your avatar. Now that your avatar is defined, you can drop it into different environments. For example, you can say: “Place my avatar walking through the Fifth Avenue Shopping District in Old Town Scottsdale, Arizona.” Or: “Have my avatar seated at a podcast desk, mid-conversation.” Each scene gives you new content, and ChatGPT can help you write more descriptive prompts for each one.

  • Animate your avatar with Sora. Once you have your base avatar image, you can animate it with Sora (or any tool that supports image-to-video generation).

The main difference between ChatGPT-4o and HeyGen?

With HeyGen, your avatar is saved and can be reused instantly. With ChatGPT, you’ll need to follow this process each time (or reuse the same chat).

And with Sora, consistency matters a lot.

It’s not just looking at your character—it’s also pulling data from:

  • Tone

  • Mood

  • Lighting

  • Camera angle

  • Background style

If your reference image doesn’t match the setting you want (say, you’re wearing a winter coat but want a beach scene), Sora will struggle with the animation. But when the reference image aligns with the setting, everything just works better.
