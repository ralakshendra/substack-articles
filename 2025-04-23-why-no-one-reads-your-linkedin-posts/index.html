Why no one reads your LinkedIn posts

Author: Nicolas Cole
Date: 2025-04-23

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9P-E!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F96d879f4-35e5-4558-9506-ccc42a0bde74_1260x900.png)

If no one’s reading your LinkedIn posts, it’s not because you’re a bad writer.

It’s because there’s nothing at stake for the reader.

The bottleneck to engagement isn’t a skill deficit.

Writing gets devoured when a reader feels like they can’t afford to miss it.

Readers want something that helps them:

  • Do their job better

  • Avoid a costly mistake

  • Or look smart in front of their boss/client

And if your LinkedIn post doesn’t signal that payoff immediately?

They scroll on past.

Which is why, before you write your next LinkedIn post, you need to answer two very simple questions:

  1. What does the reader gain if they read this?

  2. What do they lose if they don’t?

Once you're clear on that, the hook, the structure, and the CTA practically write themselves.

The good news is you don’t have to figure this out from scratch.

Here’s a simple ChatGPT prompt you can use to make sure you always answer: “What’s in it for the reader?”—so you can finish writing faster:

This prompt helps you uncover the what’s at stake before you start writing.

All you have to do is tell Claude or ChatGPT what you’re writing about and who it’s for. It’ll give you:

  • A list of wins (what the reader wants)

  • A list of losses (what they’re trying to avoid)

  • A quick introduction you can use to set up your post

  • And if you want—it’ll stay with you, step-by-step, as your writing coach

Just drop in your topic, hit enter, and let the AI do the heavy lifting.

Here’s the prompt:

I'm writing about [TOPIC] for [AUDIENCE] who want [RESULT].

What are 3 things the reader wants (wins)?
For each win, explain:
  • Why they want it
  • Functional benefit – What does it help them do?
  • Emotional benefit – How does it make them feel?
  • Dimensionalized benefit – What does this look like in real life? (e.g. A calmer morning, more time with kids, fewer late nights)

What are 3 things the reader wants to avoid (losses)?
For each loss, explain:
  • Why they want to avoid it
  • Functional cost – What does it block or break?
  • Emotional cost – How does it make them feel?
  • Dimensionalized cost – What does this look like in real life? (e.g. Staring at a screen at midnight, rewriting the same paragraph five times)

Help me write a quick introduction to my TOPIC using the wins and losses.

Here's how:

- Open with a single “If/then” sentence to frame why this topic matters.
- Follow with three plain, direct sentences to explain the impact of the problem.
- Set up the solution with a strong reason why.
- End with a transition that previews the format of the solution (like ‘3 steps’ or ‘5 prompts’) and tells the reader what outcome they’ll get.

Finally, ask me if I would like help finishing the piece. 

If yes, begin with my input. Act like a writing coach, and help me think through the rest of the post one question at a time.

When to use this prompt:

  • You’ve got a topic, but you’re not sure how to start.

  • You keep writing feels “nice” but not necessary.

  • You want to write faster—and more persuasively.

And there you have it.

With one quick prompt, your writing has stakes. And once your reader has something to win or something to lose they’ll be more likely to care what you say next. Which is half the battle.

So, give it a try it on your next LinkedIn post, then take 5 minutes to work with AI to help you finish it, and watch how everything else flows from there.

I promise you’ll feel the difference.

And more importantly, so will your reader.

Because let's be honest...

Nobody really reads for fun on LinkedIn.

And if your post doesn’t give them a reason to read? It’s ignored.

So next time you write? Don’t start with your idea. Start with your reader’s outcome.

That’s how you earn attention.
