    One of the biggest mistakes I see LinkedIn creators make?

    They chase more engagement instead of understanding who’s engaging.

    Why? Because engagement feels like progress. More likes, more comments—it must mean your audience is growing, right?

    But numbers alone don’t tell you who actually matters. Without a clear way to analyze who’s behind the engagement, it’s easy to focus on the wrong people. If you know exactly who is interacting with your content, you can tailor your messaging, refine your offers, and turn commenters into customers.

    So I built a system to reverse-engineer my audience and pinpoint my best leads.

    I call it the Audience Mapping Method—and it’s shockingly simple.

    5-Steps To Turn LinkedIn Commenters Into Customers

    Here’s how it works:

    1. Pull a list of people who commented on your most popular LinkedIn posts.

    2. Ask AI to categorize your list into natural audience segments.

    3. Sort by size to see which groups dominate your audience.

    4. Identify high-value segments (i.e. who is most likely to convert)?

    5. Get a list of 3-5 people you should DM right now.

    In less than 30 seconds, this tells you exactly who’s engaging (and who’s worth selling to).

    Note: If you’re starting out, you can follow this same process on a competitor’s post to study who you should target.

    Why LinkedIn Works Best For The Audience Map Strategy

    On LinkedIn, job titles appear right next to a commenter’s name—making it perfect for audience segmentation.

    Other platforms? Not so much. You’re lucky if you can see someone’s real name.

    With LinkedIn, every comment is instant audience research.

    My audience is more diverse than I expected.

    I assumed this post would mainly attract newsletter creators.

    Turns out, growth marketers, startup founders, and coaches also found it valuable. Each segment had different motivations for engaging.

    • Newsletter writers wanted a proven system to improve their onboarding.

    • Startup marketers were interested in automating lead nurturing.

    • Coaches and solopreneurs saw this as a way to turn subscribers into clients.

    The best part?

    I can now tailor future offers for each group. Instead of promoting the same message to everyone, I can position my products / services differently for each segment.

    For example:

    • For creators: "Turn subscribers into superfans with a welcome sequence that builds loyalty."

    • For startup marketers: "Automate your inbound funnel with a high-converting email sequence."

    • For coaches: "Nurture leads and increase bookings with a warm, high-touch welcome email flow."

    This one simple AI workflow gave me a data-backed way to help me refine my messaging.

    AND..

    It also gave me a targeted list of high-value leads I could DM to start meaningful conversations and turn engagement into sales.

    Here’s the LinkedIn Audience Mapping prompt:

    I will provide you with a long LIST of names of people who have commented on a post.

Your task:

1. Act as a Data Analyst specializing in Audience Segmentation and Conversion Strategy.
2. Analyze the list and determine natural audience segments based on common patterns in their titles.
3. Categorize them accordingly, grouping similar roles, industries, or expertise.
4. Provide three verbatim examples of job titles under each segment.
5. Indicate the percentage of the total audience that each segment represents.
6. Sort segments by size (largest first).

<LIST>
{Copy/paste your list of LinkedIn comment.}
</LIST>

<Output Format>
## {Segment Name}: {Percentage of Total Audience}%
{Implication (problems / desires)}
- {Title 1} {(Name)}
- {Title 2} {(Name)}
- {Title 3} {(Name)}
</Output Format>

    Now, job titles tell you who your audience is…

    But we can take this one step further.

    Your best leads are already telling you their biggest problems (if you know where to look).

    Hint—it’s in the comments!

    Let’s look at a few comments from my LinkedIn post.

    • “But my email is a weekly. How do you weave in a welcome sequence for a weekly newsletter without spamming your wonderful subscribers?” → They need help optimizing their email cadence.

    • “This makes sense, but how do I turn it into revenue?” → They need monetization guidance.

    • “Do you have a template for this?” → They want a done-for-you solution.

    Your comment section is free market research.

    Use it.

    Follow up the previous prompt with the one below to extract key pain points from your comments.

    Great, now I want you to act as a market research analyst specializing in opportunity identification.

Please analyze the comments in the LIST to identify key problems and opportunities people mention.

Output your response as follows:

#[number] - [Problem Title]
[2-3 line description of the problem]
- "[Verbatim Quote 1]"
...

Reminder: Focus on the comments.

    This will surface the biggest challenges your audience is struggling with—so you can create new content, products, and offers that solve them.
