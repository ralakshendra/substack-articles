    Want to connect with your reader?

    Find out what problems they want solved in their life. And then educate them on how to solve them.

    There are a bunch of ways you can do this.

    • You can guess.

    • You can run a survey.

    • You can ask ChatGPT to "come up with problems."

    While these “work”…

    The best thing you can do is talk to a real human.

    But most people don’t have the time.

    So the next best option is to listen in on your target audience where they already hang out. And there is no better place than on Reddit—”the internet’s water cooler.” There are over 57 million active users and 100 million subreddits where they hang out. That’s a lot of conversations for you to eavesdrop on for every topic imaginable.

    The beautiful part?

    You’re hearing real people, in their own words, talk about what’s bothering them.

    You can use this for:

    • Writing better hooks

    • Validating product ideas

    • Building high-converting landing pages

    • And anywhere else you need to speak to pain

    So today, I'm going to show you how to use Reddit to find what people are struggling with and then test those problems using “Outlines As Content.”

    Let’s dive in.

    Step 1: Choose Your Keywords

    Start with a specific topic and brainstorm 5–10 search terms your target audience might use when venting or asking for help.

    Keep the language casual, like they’d say it—not how you’d write it.

    Example for freelance writers:

    • "freelance writing problems"

    • "copywriting dead?"

    • "can't land writing clients"

    • "tired of $0.03/word gigs"

    • "how do I find better writing clients?"

    You can use ChatGPT to help generate a list of these based on your topic.

    Here’s a prompt:

    I'm researching problems for the following audience: 

[describe your target audience].

The topic I'm interested in is: [insert general topic, e.g. freelance writing, SaaS onboarding, losing weight, etc.]

Give me a list of 10 casual, emotionally charged phrases this audience might type into Reddit when venting or asking for help about this topic.

Use the style and tone real people use—not polished headlines. 

Focus on pain, frustration, or confusion.

Format the output as a simple list of search keywords I can use to find Reddit threads.

    Step 2: Find High-Engagement Reddit Threads

    Ask an LLM (or search manually) to find 20 Reddit threads where people are discussing this topic.

    This prompt works well on Perplexity:

    Find 20 Reddit threads with 30+ comments where people are discussing problems related to [insert topic]. 

Use these keywords: [insert list]. 

Return the post titles and links.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!EOG7!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd9f20935-7066-4c25-a4f1-d1b31236ce9a_1554x2726.png)

    You’ll get a list of high-signal, high-emotion posts where real people are venting or asking for advice.

    Tip: You can also use Google to find Reddit posts by typing:

    site:reddit.com [your keywords]

    For example:

    site:reddit.com how to write email subject lines

    This shows only Reddit results that match your keywords.

    Step 3: Scrape The Comments From Each Thread

    Use this Reddit Comment Scraper Chrome Extension to copy the full comment section from each thread.

    Yes, it’s a manual step. But it only takes a couple seconds per thread.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!yZl9!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F28472957-8ed8-48a6-a170-d16d765c05bc_2020x1350.png)

    Paste all scraped comments into a Google Doc or Notepad, then save as a .txt file for easy uploading into GPT later.

    Step 4: Extract And Distill The Core Problems

    Upload your .txt file to Claude or ChatGPT and ask it to group similar complaints, summarize them into clear problem categories, and tell you what percentage of all comments fall into each.

    Prompt:

    Based on the attached data, identify the top recurring problems people mention. Include relevant quotes verbatim.

Group similar complaints together, give each group a label, and show what percentage of total comments fall into each category.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!h-GB!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F557c025e-62d2-4e8b-9a1a-d1b76e7c7752_1424x584.png)

    This gives you a ranked list of pain points, based on real conversations.

    Step 5: Test Each Problem With “Outlines As Content”

    Now that you have your ranked list of problems, it's time to test them.

    You need to validate these problems because you don't know if YOUR audience actually cares about them until you create content. So, the next thing you need to do is take each problem from your Reddit research and turn it directly into an "Outline As Content."

    What does this mean?

    Make a list!

    Let’s say "can't land writing clients" was your #1 problem.

    You test the problem by making a list using multiple angles for the same problem.

    For example:

    • The mistakes they're making…to land a writing client

    • The steps…land a writing client

    • The tools that help you to….land a writing client.

    • The questions they should ask to…land a writing client

    Make a list for each one, then publish it as its own short-form content.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Mmy2!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1be00cf0-0d02-45f9-b0e9-62591b541da4_1178x776.png)

    Here's what you're looking for:

    • Which posts get the most engagement?

    • Which problems consistently perform well across different angles?

    The winners are your validated problems.

    Here’s a prompt:

    I am going to train you to write short-form "Outlines As Content" (280 characters or less).

We are going to use the: What / How / Why format.

This format consists of three parts:

- Start with a declarative opening line (this is the “what”)

- Follow with 3–5 bulleted examples, actions, mistakes, steps etc. (this is the “how”)

- End with a one-sentence insight, benefit, or motivating reason (this is the “why”)

Attributes that make this short-form format compelling:

- Opening first sentences (the “What”) that are hooks, deliberately intended to grab the reader’s attention. 6 proven hook styles are a strong declarative statement; a thought-provoking question; a controversial opinion; a vulnerable statement; a weird unique insight, stat, or data point; or a moment-in-time referencing a meaningful event on specific date, day, time, or time period.

- 3-5 bulleted items (the “How”) that are differentiated examples ideally not repeating conventional wisdom; tangible (not intangible) language, meaning the “How” are clear and not subject to interpretation or confusion from the reader; are steps, or objects, or “things” the reader can actionably do or use (the opposite of “fluffy vague advice”); written as economically as possible (straight to the point, using as few words as possible, only saying what is absolutely necessary to pack in the most new information per second for the reader).

- Closing sentences (the “Why”) that give the reader a good reason to do what you’ve just told them to do (”What” and “How”); either inspiring them to take action, or educating them on the cost of inaction.

~
Example Outline As Content:

4 steps to start writing:

1. Get comfortable saying "I don't know"

2. Figure out what is it you want to know

3. Commit to learning about that topic in public

4. Distill everything you're learning and share it with others

Then, repeat this process every day for years.
~

I am going to give you a problem, target audience, and angle and you are going to write a short-form piece based on my input.

Do you understand?

    Run the prompt and feed Claude or ChatGPT your target audience, problem, and desired angle (mistakes, steps, tips, etc.).

    For example:

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!cbO3!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbaeae73a-08d7-46f4-90c6-6b0fc374096a_1562x938.png)

    Create different versions of this content and schedule them a week or two apart from each other to see what works: do readers want Tips or Mistakes? Are they interested in landing clients, or a different topic? And most of all, do they want to hear it from YOU?
