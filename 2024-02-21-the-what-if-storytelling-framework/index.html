    Ahoy, Digital Writers!

    Last week I read 2 different emails that taught me the “What If” method for generating story ideas.

    Both left me wanting more. Because they told me what to do, but now how. And I wanted to know how!

    Has that every happened to you?

    I’m betting yes.

    So today I want to show you how I worked with AI to fill in the missing “how-to” gap for the “what if” storytelling technique. And I’m going to show you how I used AI to build my own step-by-step guide so you can too!

    Let’s dive in!

    The “What If” Storytelling Technique

    Here’s a quick paraphrase from one of the emails:

    There is one question that opens up endless creativity

    And it will change your world. Set your imagination on fire. And give you a never ending supply of interesting stories.

    The question: "What if?"

    Here’s how it works:

    Look around you. Make an observation and ask the “What If” question of any object you see. For example, let’s say you notice the paint on your wall, you would ask something like, “What if paint could talk?” or “What if we only had 1 color to choose from?” or “What if Michelangelo painted the wall?”

    Tell that story.

    You get the idea.

    If you need a story idea, find an object, start asking what if questions, and then write.

    Simple enough.

    But when I tried it for myself, I immediately got stuck searching my brain for a "what if" question. My mind went blank. “What if you get stuck coming up with what if questions?” Then what happens.

    I will tell you.

    You ask yourself, “What am I missing here?” and then you call up the Digital Intern.

    Here’s what happened:

    How I Created The “What If” Storytelling Framework From Scratch: 5 Lessons In Iterative Prompting

    First, before I went to ChatGPT I thought about what I was missing.

    I needed a lens for asking “What if.” A good “What If” question comes down to how you position it. And I wanted some categories to help me.

    What follows is a list of the 5 lessons I learned along the way, so you can avoid my mistakes, learn from them, and see how I iterated my way into creating a framework with AI.

    By the end of this post you’ll have:

    • A prompt to create 6 “What If” questions to help you tell a creative story.

    • A prompt that builds the “What If” Storytelling Framework that you can expand for your own storytelling needs.

    • A prompt and a process to generate any Step-By-Step Guide / Framework you want for yourself to make writing them a breeze.

    • And you’ll learn how to transform a series of iterative prompts into a single mega-prompt that you can use on repeat.

    Let’s go!

    Lesson #1: Be Clear

    This was my first prompt.

    You can tell by the script that it’s not the best.

    What are 10 ways to write a better “what if” question? 

Like ways to think about framing.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!j4KX!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7068908c-5454-4c5f-a75f-c6c1750866c3_1050x1296.png)

    And guess what?

    Surprise! The result wasn’t helpful.

    But, it was my fault. Because it was a poor prompt. “Like ways to think about framing” is extremely vague and frankly makes no sense. And the results showed.

    So, learning from my mistake, I refined my next prompt.

    Lesson #2: Add Context

    In this prompt, I added an example and a little more detail about what I wanted— a framework.

    Hmm. I want to ask better what if questions of things I observe. 

Like I observe paint on a wall. 

Boring until you ask some what if questions about the paint. 

For example, what if paint could talk? 

I want to create a simple actionable framework for asking what if questions of my observations. 

Can you help?

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!em7i!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F73b91b13-8967-4886-85a2-20c2910d2bdf_1034x1318.png)

    The results were better, but I noticed that I made another BIG mistake.

    I failed to give ChatGPT the bigger context. The reason WHY I wanted to do this in the first place was to create a helpful storytelling framework. Notice how I skipped right over that in the last prompt.

    So I tried again (noting what I liked from the previous output).

    Lesson #3: “What kind?”

    Here you can see I’ve shifted my strategy from “prompting” to “conversing “with AI.

    I give it some feedback (Steps 1-3 are good). Then I “try” to explain what I want again and ask for a revision to the previous step.

    Ok, steps 1, 2, and 3 are good. 

But what I'm really trying to do is turn an observation into a story by asking what if questions. 

How does that revise your steps?

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!J6R3!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ffc6f973b-7cc1-4c3e-9a02-c1795f5cedd1_830x1032.png)

    ChatGPT did what I asked, but I learned another valuable lesson.

    I needed to be clear, about what “kind” of story I wanted to tell. ChatGPT assumed I wanted to write a fiction story. This is what happens when you leave the decision making up to your Digital Intern.

    So, I tried again.

    Lesson #3: “Who’s it for?”

    Keeping the conversational tone, I corrected my last mistake and clarified how I was looking for help with non-fiction stories and not fiction. I also added in the concept of “anecdote” which helped. Because it adds context.

    Oops. you went too far.  

And I should have clarified. 

This is for telling a non-fiction story, more like an anecdote. 

Also for a beginner, so take that into consideration when outlining the actionable steps here.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sUHz!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff3791f20-1703-4af4-b4a9-e2cc0c844ee7_1102x1246.png)

    You can see we are getting closer, but I’m still lacking clarity.

    I felt like the output wasn’t hitting the mark. ChatGPT was doing a good job of describing the steps. However, I wanted specific instructions.

    So, I needed to get even clearer on the WHO.

    Lesson #4: “Seriously, WHO!!”

    Ok, I think the beginner needs more help and direction on how to go about asking the What if question.  

It's not enough to just say pose a simple "what if" question related to your observation that introduces a hypothetical scenario or perspective. 

They need help with different ways of framing the question.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HjXv!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9d091042-0c4e-4c3e-af22-06d19cf3c0e5_786x1324.png)

    Aha, I’m starting to like what I see with actions and examples to follow.

    The output is starting to feel actionable. But it still feels too advanced and not helpful.

    So, I get even more specific.

    Ok, headed in the right direction here. 
BUT the basic what if, feels advanced. 
I think a 13 year old needs to be able to understand this. 
I think they can get the idea of obseving something, taking note of what they observe/what's true but then when we move to asking what if questions, we need to give them better categories. 
"Alternate Causes" in your example is a category, but it's too vague. 
I needs to be simple, clear and actionable for my 13 year old reader.  
Like, what if "a property of what you observed was false", etc.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!p1DZ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fae7ee562-16d3-4f3b-9c6b-c13b16837281_744x1100.png)

    Much much better.

    These are tangible and actionable ideas that I can read and immediately know how to act on.

    Now, as a reader I wanted more examples to help me understand. So I asked ChatGPT to give me two more examples. (Plus a little encouragement for doing a good job!)

    Lesson #5: Keep The Good

    I liked the last result, but I wanted more example questions.

    So, I asked for them.

    YES! You did it. 

But I want you to give me 3 examples for each option you gave me in step 3, just so it's crystal clear.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!qtj9!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa96c1170-c9d9-4888-94f7-345ea59ba433_830x952.png)

    ChatGPT, again, did exactly what I asked.

    Unfortunately, it created a whole new set of examples. Which I didn’t want.

    So I tried again.

    This time I made sure it was clear “what I liked” by including it in my prompt.

    In general, this is a wise prompt strategy. ChatGPT is supposed to remember everything in your chat session. But I’ve noticed it can and will hallucinate. A good practice is to bring forward key context anytime you are working on an iterative prompt like this.

    Carrying your context forward will keep both you and ChatGPT on the same page.

    Here’s the revised prompt:

    Oops, I wanted you to add examples to the original you gave me. I will repost it here. So, it's clear. 

Now Please add 2 new examples for each, so there will be 3 examples in total.
Also for each "action," please add a reason why to do the action step and a simple tip.

Step 3: Imagine "What If" by Simple Categories
a. Flip the Truth
Action: Invert a truth about your observation.
Question Framework: "What if [opposite of what you observed]?"
Example: "What if the ants were all working alone instead of together?"
b. Change a Purpose
Action: Assign a new or opposite purpose to your observation.
Question Framework: "What if [your observation] was used for [different purpose]?"
Example: "What if the ant hill was a meeting spot for other insects to trade food?"
c. Add a New Ability
Action: Imagine your observation has a new ability or feature.
Question Framework: "What if [your observation] could [new ability]?"
Example: "What if the ants could communicate with humans?"
d. Alter the Size
Action: Dramatically change the size of what you observed.
Question Framework: "What if [your observation] was [much larger/smaller]?"
Example: "What if the ant hill was as big as a house?"
e. Shift the Time
Action: Place your observation in a different time period.
Question Framework: "What if [your observation] existed in [different time period]?"
Example: "What if ants had the technology of humans in the medieval times?"
f. Mix with Fiction
Action: Combine your observation with an element from your favorite book, movie, or game.
Question Framework: "What if [your observation] was like [fictional element]?"
Example: "What if ants organized their societies like the kingdoms in fantasy novels?"

    FINALLY! Success!

    I got to 80% of what I wanted in 5-steps and about 3 minutes. It still needs formatting and general clean-up, but I’m ok with handling that on my own. And this is how prompting goes. From here I can retrace my steps and build a better prompt correcting my mistakes and taking in all the lessons.

    Let’s see if we can do better with a single prompt.

    How I Turned Iterative Prompts Into 1 Mega-Prompt For The “What If” Storytelling Framework

    To do this, I retraced my steps from each of my previous prompts.

    I looked through each prompt and copy/pasted each command into a single script. Then I refined the script into a cohesive set of instructions, taking into account the lessons I learned in my iterative prompt sequence.

    For example:

    • I specified the kind of story I wanted to tell, “non-fiction.”

    • I made sure I was clear about WHO the output was for, “beginners.”

    • And I leveraged the previous categories and examples to help ChatGPT understand what I was looking for.

    Below is the final prompt after my edits.

    “What If” Story Questions

    I am going to train you to use the "What If" question technique.

Here's how it works:

You will ask me for an object. For example, paint, cup, pencil, etc.

I will tell you what kind of object I want to focus on.

Then you are going to give me a list of 6 "what if" questions that I can use as a prompt for telling a story.

These questions are going to help me tell a non-fiction story—like an anecdote. 

I'm a beginner. So your output needs to be clear for clear for 13 year old.

For example, if I observe paint on a wall. 

Then you might say, "what if paint could talk?" 

Here are 6 "what if" categories of questions to consider:
1. Flip the truth. For example, "What if the ants were all working alone instead of together?"
2. Change a purpose. For example,"What if the ant hill was a meeting spot for other insects to trade food?"
3. Add a new ability. For example, "What if the ants could communicate with humans?"
4. Alter the size. For example,"What if the ant hill was as big as a house?"
5. Shift the time. For example, "What if ants had the technology of humans in the medieval times?"
6. Mix with fiction. For example, "What if ants organized their societies like the kingdoms in fantasy novels?"

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!xZPN!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Faf1e1da0-3373-492c-bba9-71e1449becb9_1462x796.png)

    You can see after running the prompt, ChatGPT gave me a list of good questions that I can now use to generate storytelling ideas, which is helpful, but I want to build a framework.

    So, let’s see if we can create the Step-By-Step Guide.

    The “What If” Storytelling Framework

    Here’s the prompt:

    I want to create a simple step-by-step actionable framework for asking what if questions of my observations. 

I'm a beginner, so take that into consideration when outlining the actionable steps.

Be sure to include the different categories of What If questions to ask.

Each step should include the following:
- The Action to take
- A Reason to take the action
- A Tip for applying it or a mistake to avoid
- A framework or template to follow
- 3 simple examples

The first step should be "Make An Observation"

The last step should be "Write A 5-Sentence Story."

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!aKv_!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F57aee8e7-1d48-4cd6-a8de-9f24fea5420c_1354x1254.png)

    The output was pretty close to where I ended up using the iterative approach.

    In this case, I found the iterative approach got me closer to what I was looking for in the beginning, but in 1 step the single-prompt gets me 70% of the way instead of 80% after 5 steps. Still not bad!

    Now let’s turn our specific framework into a generic one that you can use to build out your own frameworks.

    How To Create A Step-By-Step Framework With AI

    From here, I turned my specific “What If” framework prompt, into a generic one.

    To create this prompt I simply removed the specifics and turned it into a template that anyone can use. For example, I changed “asking what if questions” into the variable {PURPOSE}.

    Then all have to do is fill in each variable with anything you want!

    Here’s the template:

    I want to create a simple step-by-step actionable framework to {PURPOSE}.

I'm a beginner, so take that into consideration when outlining the actionable steps.

Be sure to include {ANYTHING SPECIFIC YOU WANT}.

Each step should include the following:
- The Action to take
- A Reason to take the action
- A Tip for applying it or a mistake to avoid
- A framework or template to follow
- 3 simple examples

The first step should be {FIRST STEP}.

The last step should be {LAST STEP}.

    I tried using the template for our “Endless Idea Generator.”

    I want to create a simple step-by-step actionable framework to generate endless writing ideas.

    I'm a beginner, so take that into consideration when outlining the actionable steps.

    Be sure to include different types of writing like actionable, analytical, anthropological, and aspirational for writing non-fiction.

    Each step should include the following:

    • The action to take

    • A reason to take the action

    • A tip for applying it or a mistake to avoid

    • A framework or template to follow

    • 3 simple examples

    The first step should be "Take out piece of paper."

    The last step should be "Choose one idea and start writing."
