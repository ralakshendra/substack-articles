How To Instantly Write Funny

The Nate Bargatze Method

Author: Nicolas Cole
Date: 2025-04-09

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!oFZP!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbd7088b8-f322-4fe6-a6b2-9709d0943fa7_1260x900.png)

Ever notice how Nate Bargatze never seems like he's trying to be funny?

Yet he has millions of people crying with laughter.

His secret?

He doesn't tell jokes.

He just points out what's weird about normal life:

"Your 20s are great. Your friends call, like, 'Do you want to go?'

You're like, 'I'll go!'

You don't even know where you're going."

And we laugh because it's true.

Here’s the thing:

You don't need to be "naturally funny" to write like Nate.

You just need to train yourself to notice the absurd things we all do but never talk about. And if your writing could use a little more punch, humor, or relatability, then today’s AI prompt is for you.

Let’s dive in.

When was the last time you genuinely laughed?

Chances are it was because someone pointed out a truth you'd experienced but never articulated.

Nate Bargatze didn't become a comedy legend by inventing absurd scenarios. He simply observes everyday life with uncomfortable accuracy:

One of my favorite bits:

“ICED COFFEE WHIPPED CREAM?!”

It’s strangely familiar, right?

Just like when you’re trying to find tahini sauce at the grocery and it becomes an existential crisis.

Funny is familiar.

3 Reasons Why “Familiar” Works

  • "OMG. You do that too?!" When readers recognize their own experience in your writing, you bypass the need to establish trust. They feel like you've been secretly following them around, taking notes. This shared experience builds rapport faster than any credentials could.

  • “Oh, thank God!” There's relief in knowing you’re not alone in your weird little habits. Everyone secretly worries they’re the only ones who have certain thoughts or behaviors.

  • It’s true. Made-up scenarios require suspension of disbelief. But when you nail a real-life observation, readers can't dismiss it. They've lived it. Which is why the emotional reaction to recognizing a previously unarticulated truth is almost always laughter.

3 Steps To Find Humor In Your Day

Let’s break it down.

Step 1: Question the unquestioned

Look for the strange social contracts we all follow without thinking.

For example:

  • “Why do you sell stuff that should be thrown away?”

  • "Why do you pretend you’re not home when the doorbell rings?"

  • "Do you judge people's typing speed during a Zoom screen share?"

  • "Why do you walk on the right side of sidewalk even though there's no law requiring it?"

To practice this step:

Notice your automatic behaviors. Then question why you do them.

Step 2: Get stupidly specific

When you spot something worth pointing out, zoom WAY in.

  • Don't just say "bags of ice" - say "50 bags of ice."

  • Don't just mention "coffee" - name "Starbuck’s iced coffee with cream.

  • Add what you're thinking: You said take your time, and now I've been in this bathroom for 14 minutes afraid to flush.

The more specific you get, the easier it is for someone to see themselves in your content. And once they do? They mentally bookmark you as someone who gets it.

Which is why they'll reply to your email or share your post without thinking twice.

Step 3: Ask: "What does this say about being human?"

After you've described a familiar situation with hyper-specific detail, you need to pull back and reveal the deeper truth.

What's REALLY going on here?

For example:

We pretend we're not home when the doorbell rings unexpectedly...because we believe we have the right to control who enters our personal space AND when. Yet we still feel guilty about it - like we're breaking some ancient social rule about hospitality.

It’s a universal tension.

You feel it, but rarely talk about it. And when someone finally acknowledges these unspoken feelings, it's both hilarious and relieving.

A few tips to find a funny truth:

Look for:

  1. Contradictions: Look for the clash between what you say and what you do Example: You say "Let's get coffee sometime!" but do you really have the intention of ever seeing them again?

  2. Irrational Fears: Identify the irrational fears driving your normal behavior Example: You triple-check that you’ve locked the car even though you just heard the beep and saw the lights flash.

  3. Lies: Notice which emotions we try to hide from others Example: You panic when someone asks "What do you do?" and you hate your job. Or even the lie you tell yourself to feel better Example: "I'll start eating healthy on Monday."

Let’s look at another Nate Bargatze bit about letting the dogs out to see this in action.

Here’s Nate:

"We have one dog. I'd like another. My wife says it's too much.

Because then you have to open the back door and let two dogs out.

Then they're out there… you're inside…

But then they want to come back in. Now you've got to go let ALL these dogs back inside.

You're like: one dog, two dogs—look at all these dogs everywhere."

Using the 3 step process above, Nate’s bit would break down like this:

  • What's weird? The way two dogs suddenly feels like a full-time job.

  • Specifics? Opening the door. Letting them out. Letting them in. The fake drama of "ALL these dogs."

  • Universal? Even simple choices create unexpected burdens. We're all just trying to keep things manageable—and life keeps sneaking more in.

Now, it's obvious, but if you don't share what you find, then nobody gets to experience that "OMG me too!" moment.

And that's the whole point.

The best comedians aren't just observant - they're brave enough to say the quiet part out loud.

They say what we're all thinking but are too afraid to admit.

So once you've found your human truth, you have to actually put it out there.

Want To Practice? Start Here

Now that you understand how to spot those universal human truths, let's make it even easier with an AI prompt.

Copy this prompt into your favorite AI tool:


I want your help to find the funny truth in this everyday situation: 

[DESCRIBE YOUR SITUATION IN DETAIL]

Analyze this situation and identify:

- Any contradictions between what people say vs. what they do
- Irrational fears or anxieties that might be driving behavior
- Emotions people might be hiding or lies they tell themselves
- The unspoken social contract being followed

Then, rewrite this observation as a short, relatable, humorous insight that highlights the deeper human truth. 

Make it conversational and hyper-specific, as if a standup comedian like Nate Bargatze were pointing it out.

Drop a short story or situation from your day.

For example:

I dropped my car off to get the tires rotated. My wife is out of town and it's something she wants done. The maintenance required light is on and it’s important to her that it’s turned off. Unfortunately, Costco didn't turn it off. And now I have to figure it out.

Run the prompt.

And voila!

Here’s where you can use these funny truths:

  • On X (Twitter) Perfect for short, punchy observations. Start with the specific situation, then hit them with the truth bomb in the second half of your tweet.

  • On LinkedIn Yes, even LinkedIn! The platform is DESPERATE for authentic, human content amid all the corporate jargon. Your funny truth will stop the scroll instantly.

  • Email newsletters This is where these observations SHINE. Use them as story openers or subject lines to boost your open rates.

That’s it.

  • Pick one ordinary situation from your day.

  • Describe it in detail.

  • Then ask: "What would I never admit out loud?"

  • Share it wherever you write online

Soon you'll have an endless well of relatable content that makes people think, "How did they get inside my head?!" And if you do that? They'll remember you. They'll share you. And they'll keep coming back.
