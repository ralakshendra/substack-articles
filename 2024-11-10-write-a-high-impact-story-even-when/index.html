    Write A High-Impact Story (Even When Life Is Boring)

    Why Your Dirty Dishes Could Be Your Next Breakthrough Post

    Author: Nicolas Cole
    Date: 2024-11-10

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Eecc!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3dac04a7-6680-47a4-94e4-094c1d440690_1260x900.png)

    Most people think AI’s job is to write for them.

    But AI works best when you write with it.

    And one easy way to start practicing this is by mining your daily life for content.

    Think about your day so far.

    • The dirty dishes you left sitting in the sink? That's a post.

    • The instagram reel you shared (while sitting on the toilet)? That's 3 posts.

    • The time you lost your words, spilled your coffee, and still closed the deal? That's 10 posts.

    Each of these moments contains multiple stories, lessons, and insights.

    You're literally living content. Even boring, mundane experiences can be turned into something worth writing. But most people never pause to reflect because they think “nobody cares.” This is a mistake. The most relatable content comes from the moments we think are too ordinary to share.

    For example, one of my most viral posts on Substack started with a tiny mental “ping.”

    Finding the story in your daily moments takes practice. The trick is connecting them to your audience. But you don't have to figure it out alone.

    AI can help.

    Here's a simple framework to “unboring” your life and turn it into content using AI:

    The Record-Reflect-Reframe Framework

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5U4p!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc87a2af5-1adb-42cb-b1a9-d3a6434fc83a_1339x895.png)

    Every morning when I wake up, the first thing I see are yesterday's dirty dishes in the sink.

    Most people would just see a chore. But I see a story about trade-offs, priorities, and the myth of perfect productivity. That's what this framework is about—finding relevant, relatable ideas in life's ordinary moments.

    Here's how it works:

    Record: Notable Moments During Your Day, Then Distill Them Into 3-Bullets At Night

    Every morning fire up a note-taking app open on your phone.

    The moment you encounter something that sparks your interest—whether it’s an observation, an idea, or even a small moment that stands out—jot it down in one sentence.

    For example, when I was home at home recently, I noticed a book on the bookshelf in my family’s living room: Mental Toughness Training For Sports: Achieving Athletic Excellence. It reminded me of a lesson my dad taught me about success. So, I added it to my notes.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!XyR4!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff3462664-c0a8-4ff3-941b-e6bb1bd82d5e_1319x679.png)

    At the end of the day take 5 minutes to read through all your notes.

    Write 3 bullet points:

    • "Today I learned..."

    • "Today I struggled with..."

    • "Today I wondered about..."

    These are your content seeds.

    Bonus: Tag each note with an emotion (🤔 curious, 😤 frustrated, 💡 insight)

    Relate: Your Personal Experiences Back To The Reader To Make Them Relevant

    Readers care about their own experiences, not yours.

    This prompt helps you to “universalize” your notes and make your story relevant by linking it directly to your reader’s pain, frustrations, or goals.

    Copy/paste the below prompt into a new chat. Insert the 1-3 events from your day and describe who would benefit most from your experience (i.e. your target audience).

    For example:

    Claude will analyze each experience and generate a list of potential angles for you to write about.

    Here are a few examples from my barista note:

    One interaction, four different angles, and 8 different post ideas.

    Still think your everyday is boring?

    Here’s the prompt:

    
# Content Ideation Partner Prompt

Act as my content ideation partner. 

I'm going to share my day with you in bullet points. 

## INPUT SECTION
Here's what happened:
1. [Experience 1]
2. [Experience 2]
3. [Experience 3]

Target Audience: [Describe your primary audience's demographics, pain points, and goals]

## ANALYSIS REQUEST
Based on these experiences, analyze:

1. Universal Elements
- Identify common human struggles
- Extract core life lessons
- Surface key insights

2. Emotional Core
- Primary emotions involved
- Relatable feelings
- Key emotional transformations

3. Audience Value
- Specific benefits for target audience
- Pain points addressed
- Desired outcomes

## CONTENT ANGLES
Generate 4 unique content angles using these perspectives:
- Actionable: Practical steps and solutions
- Anthropological: Human behavior insights
- Analytical: Data-driven observations
- Aspirational: Growth and improvement focus

## HEADLINE FRAMEWORKS
For each content angle, create 2 headlines using these structures:
- [Number] [Items/Steps] to [Desirable Outcome]
- "How to [X] Without [Y]"
- "What Nobody Tells You About [X]"
- "Why [Common Belief] About [X] Is Wrong"
- "The [Time Frame] Guide to [X]"
- "How I [X] by [Unexpected Y]"
- "Is [X] Actually [Unexpected Y]?"

## OUTPUT FORMAT
For each content angle:
1. Angle Description (1-2 sentences)
2. Target Audience Benefit
3. Two Compelling Headlines

    We’re not done yet.

    Reframe: Your “Aha!” Moment As A Shortcut For The Reader

    Since these are personal stories, a good pattern to follow is the "Epiphany Bridge."

    Here’s how it works:

    • Start with your relatable challenge. ”I always thought being 'smart' meant having high IQ..." Everyone who's ever felt "not smart enough" can relate to this.

    • Share the moment it clicked for you."Until I found my dad's old sports psychology book and realized why he called Jerry West 'the smartest player on the court.'" Your personal story creates a bridge to the solution.

    • End with an insight for your reader. "Smart didn't just mean intelligence. It meant mental toughness." Now readers who struggle with feeling "not smart enough" see a new path forward - they can develop mental toughness.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!G_Qj!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fda5d76e6-bd2c-4f26-9edf-b62f8bd11f47_1274x1268.png)

    This structure works because your personal "aha" moment becomes their shortcut to understanding. You struggled with the same limiting belief they have, found a solution, and now they can skip straight to the insight.

    Here’s the prompt to outline your story:

    
Help me structure this story:

[Paste your chosen angle from Step 1]

I want to include:

- Strong first line (11 words or less)
- Curiosity gap (setup)
- Epiphany bridge
- Segue to value promise (here's why/how/what matters to you)
- 3 main points (benefit subhead/tie in example)
- Objection handling
- Close & CTA

Format it as bullet points I can expand on.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Li2R!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F109200e3-e94f-4177-88e3-fca950e80191_1526x1122.png)

    Bonus: Tell Your Story On YouTube, LinkedIn, Or iTunes

    Once you have a working outline, insert it in the below prompt to repurpose the story for different platforms.

    
# I've written about this experience:

[Insert your outline]

# Show me how to adapt this into:

CHOOSE ONE:

- A Twitter Thread (280 chars/tweet)
- A LinkedIn Post (≤3000 chars)
- A Newsletter (500-800 words)
- A YouTube Script (3-7 mins, include B-roll)
- A Podcast Outline (15-30 mins)

# Adjust the format and tone for the platform.

    Boom.

    One experience just became five pieces of content.

    • An X Thread

    • A LinkedIn Post

    • A YouTube Script

    • A Podcast Outline

    • A Newsletter Issue

    Each version automatically adapts to platform requirements - 280 characters for X, B-roll ideas for YouTube, etc.

    Pro tip: The stronger your Epiphany Bridge outline, the less editing you'll need to do later. You're still the one living the experiences. You're still the one choosing what matters. AI just helps you see the patterns and possibilities you might have missed.

    That’s it.

    Give it a try.

    Start with today. What happened? You've got the experiences. Now you've got the framework to document it. Open Claude. Run the prompt and turn your “everyday” into something worth sharing.
