    Ahoy, Digital Writers!

    Have you ever struggled to clarify an idea, even when you thought you understood it?

    You write your idea down on a piece of paper. Then you scratch your head and wonder, “What do I say?” Sometimes it’s a lack of knowledge. But most of the time it’s because you haven’t thought critically about your topic or your reader yet.

    Here’s the good news:

    You can use a simple 4-part method to bring clarity to any idea so it’s easy to understand.

    It’s called SEE-I.

    Let's dive in!

    The SEE-I Method For Clear Writing

    SEE-I stands for State, Elaborate, Example, and Illustrate.

    It’s a step-by-step process for explaining an idea in a concise way. It reduces ambiguity and confusion, giving your readers a clear, easy-to-follow path through your ideas.

    How It Works

    • Step 1: Start with a Statement. Clearly state the concept or idea you're trying to communicate. Make sure it's brief and to the point.

    • Step 2: Next, Elaborate on that statement. Explain it in your own words, as if you're talking to a friend who's unfamiliar with the topic.

    • Step 3: Provide an Example (and counter-example). This is where you bring your statement to life. Use a real-world situation or create a hypothetical scenario that illustrates your point

    • Step 4: Lastly, Illustrate with a metaphor or analogy. This simple comparison can clarify complex ideas and assure your readers truly grasp the concept.

    Simple, right?

    The reason SEE-I works so well is because it matches how our brains learn best. Stating the main idea first helps you focus on what you want to say. Elaborating helps you prove or backup the point you want to make. And giving real examples anchors your point in reality and makes your idea easier to remember.

    Let’s look at an example.

    Example

    Suppose we want to explain the concept of "social proof."

    You start the process by first stating what you want to say. For example, “Today, we’re diving into social proof.” Once you know what you want to talk about, then all you have to do is work your way down the list one section at at a time.

    Like this:

    • State it: “Most people assume that if a crowd is doing something, then it must be ‘proven.’ ”

    • Explain: “When you see a crowd doing something, you assume it must be the right thing to do. It's a natural instinct. Our brains are wired to follow the pack, to trust the masses, and to assume safety in numbers—often unconsciously. This is the power of social proof.”

    • Example: “For example, when a restaurant is full, you assume the food must be good. Or when you see the Stanley Quencher Cup on Amazon has 33,430 positive reviews, you assume it makes your lemonade sweeter. But, (counter-example) just because something is popular doesn't always mean it's good. McDonald’s is popular, but not necessarily good for you.”

    • Illustrate: “Imagine you're walking down a street with two ice cream shops. One has a long line out the door, while the other is empty. Which one are you more likely to choose? The busy one, right? This is how social proof works.”

    Each section expands your thinking—and the reader’s comprehension.

    Tip: For the “State It” part of the framework, use one of the 6 proven single-sentence openers to pull your reader into the section with a hook.

    Now, let’s try this out with AI.

    The SEE-I “Idea Expander” Prompt

    Prompting AI with SEE-I is easy.

    AI is already familiar with the framework, so you don’t have need to spend time training it. Which means you can reference SEE-I by name in your prompt and AI will take care of the rest.

    Now, if you aren’t sure if AI knows about a framework, all you have to do is ask!

    For example, “What do you know about the SEE-I framework?” In less than 1 second you’ll know whether you need to spend time helping AI understand what you want it to do, or if you can kick your feet up on your desk, eat a donut, and celebrate your digital intern—who already knows a lot!

    Let’s take a look.

    Example

    Let’s say you are writing an article about the 5 Biggest Mistakes Notion Users Make That Lead To Information Overload. And the first point you want to make is that trying to use every feature Notion offers is a mistake. But you aren’t quite sure where to start.

    So, you plug your first point into the prompt below.

    Like this:

    I need help expanding the topic: 

Trying To Use Everything Notion Has To Offer Is a Mistake

Use the SEE-I framework to expand the topic. 

Tone of voice: Informative, helpful, blunt, and direct. Human. Colloquial.

Writing style: Clear, concise, organized, with a mix of short and long sentences. Good use of line breaks and a style that makes you want to continue reading from one sentence to the next.

Format:
[Open with a declarative single-sentence hook in 10 words or less]
[Explain the opener like I'm 5 (but still an adult)]
[Share an example and counter-example]
[Illustrate with a relatable everyday analogy or metaphor]
[End with a simple, direct, and poignant takeaway in 10 words or less]

    Then when you are ready, mash the enter key, and like magic, AI expands your point.

    Now you’re in a good spot to start editing.

    Give This Prompt A Try

    Here’s the template.

    I need help expanding the topic: 

{TOPIC}

Use the SEE-I framework to expand the topic. 

Tone of voice: Informative, helpful, blunt, and direct. Human. Colloquial.

Writing style: Clear, concise, organized, with a mix of short and long sentences. Good use of line breaks and a style that makes you want to continue reading from one sentence to the next.

Format:
[Open with a declarative single-sentence hook in 10 words or less]
[Explain the opener like I'm 5 (but still an adult)]
[Share an example and counter-example]
[Illustrate with a relatable everyday analogy or metaphor]
[End with a simple, direct, and poignant takeaway in 10 words or less]

    Prompt Writer Tips

    Before we wrap up, here are a few takeaways from this prompt:

    • Formatting: The prompt includes a “format” section to reinforce the framework. You can remove this section and the prompt will follow the SEE-I framework, but the output will include labels for each section instead of the clean paragraphs above. It’s not hard to remove the labels when editing, but it will save you a little time.

    • Hook In 10 Words Or Less: Give AI a number of words to stick to in the hook and you’ll get a better result. The forced constraint prevents AI from returning a sentence or sentences full of conjunctions that are less likely to grab attention.

    • Explain Like I’m 5: When you instruct AI to “explain like I’m 5” it will simplify it’s output. The problem is the examples will be for a 5 year-old too. Firetrucks and Sesame Street can work, but I’ve found that it’s better to modify the command with “but still for an adult.”

    Prompting is a game of clear instructions and choosing the right words!

    That’s it.

    Next time you need to explain a topic and you’re feeling stuck, remember SEE-I: State, Elaborate, Example, Illustrate. This simple framework will help you write with clarity, no matter the subject or audience.

    Your ideas deserve to be understood, by you and your reader - SEE-I shows you how.
