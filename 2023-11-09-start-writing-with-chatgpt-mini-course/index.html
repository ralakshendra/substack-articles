    Ultimate Guide To Start Writing With ChatGPT

    In Ship 30 for 30, one of the foundational frameworks we teach is the Lean Writing Method.

    This is our proven method for:

    • Generating hundreds of relevant ideas for any niche audience.

    • Compressing & expanding those ideas into Tweets, Twitter Threads, Atomic Essays, LinkedIn Posts, and even long-form blog posts.

    • And then doubling-down on the ones that “work” (resonate most with readers), and rewriting about those topics over and over again.

    This is how we have built combined audiences of 500,000+ followers, 60,000+ email subscribers, and a cohort-based course that has helped 6,000+ people start writing online.

    But there’s a way to pour gasoline on this framework of ours…

    And it’s by leveraging ChatGPT.

    The Lean Writing Framework + ChatGPT

    Think of ChatGPT as your own personal Digital Intern.

    You can train it. You can speak to it like a wide-eyed, ambitious human. You can help it improve. You can critique it and give it feedback as you go along. It is there to assist you with whatever you need—it’s just on you to give it proper instructions.

    Most writers don’t realize how much of their craft is boring, repetitive work (or, they do, but take pride in it). But in an AI-driven future, the Digital Writers who succeed the most will not be the ones “taking pride” in doing as much of the labor-intensive work as possible. The most successful Digital Writers will be the ones who train their own personal AI Interns (or create an army of them) to create, publish, and distribute as many of their ideas as possible—as quickly as possible.

    This is what we want to help you to do.

    And we have a terrific framework for doing so—using ChatGPT.

    Here’s how it works:

    Step 1: The “2-Year Test”

    Before you even open ChatGPT, or start asking AI for help, you need to gain some clarity yourself.

    What do you want to write about?

    The easiest place to start is by doing “The 2-Year Test,” reflecting on the past 2+ years and asking yourself:

    • “What skills have I built?”

    • “What struggles have I faced?”

    • “What hobbies have I picked up?”

    • “What problems have I overcome?”

    • “What life transitions have I made?”

    • “What stories have I experienced?”

    • “What topics have I learned about?”

    • Etc.

    You can also do the 2-Year Test “forwards” by asking yourself what you want to have learned, do, and experienced 2 years from now:

    • “What skills do I want to build?”

    • “What struggles do I want to face head-on?”

    • “What hobbies do I want to pick up?”

    • “What problems do I want to overcome?”

    • “What life transitions do I want to make?”

    • “What stories do I want to experience?”

    • “What topics do I want to learn about?”

    • Etc.

    The 2-Year Test reveals all the different things you want to write about—either from a place of personal credibility (”I experienced this”), or borrowed credibility (”I want to learn this, and here’s who I’m studying”).

    Go through this exercise, and then just pick 1 of these bullets (from either list) to start writing about.

    Step 2: Choose 1 Incentive

    Once you have a “topic” (either something you’ve already learned/done, or something you want to learn/do), next you need to pick an “incentive.”

    An “incentive” is the reason why the reader would be interested.

    Why are you telling them what you’re telling them?

    Here’s a list of proven incentives to get you started:

    • …To Get Started {Skill}

    • …To Learn {Skill}

    • …To Master {Skill}

    • …To Overcome {Problem}

    • …To Unlock {Outcome}

    • …To Streamline {Process}

    • …To Get Your First {Outcome}

    • …To Achieve {Big Outcome}

    • …So You Don’t {Worst Outcome}

    • …To Hire Your First {Employee}

    • …To Buy Your First {Valuable Asset}

    • …To Sell Your First {Valuable Asset}

    • …To Make Your First {$X}

    • …To Build Your First {Product/Company}

    • …To Improve {Task}

    • …To Scale Your {Business}

    • …To Pitch Your {Idea}

    • …To Help You Fix {Problem}

    • …To Teach You {Solution}

    • …To Create The Perfect {Product}

    • …To {Do More} Without {More Work}

    • …To Make Progress On {Goal}

    All you have to do is take your 1 topic, and pair it with 1 incentive, and you now have the foundation to generate hundreds of ideas.

    Step 5: Turn Each Individual Headline/Idea Into A Tweet

    Here’s where your efforts really start to compound.

    Once you go through the above exercises and create 30 headline ideas for 1 Topic & 1 Incentive, you can then create 5 different versions of each headline—by simply changing the format/word count parameters.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!f6Lb!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa495be78-9f74-4d86-8740-5b0beab39bd5_1440x764.png)

    Each headline can be:

    • A Tweet

    • A Twitter Thread

    • A LinkedIn Post

    • An Atomic Essay

    • A Blog Post

    Let’s walk through how to train ChatGPT to create each one—starting with a Tweet.

    Here’s the script for turning your headline into a Tweet:

    Great, I like this headline: {Headline}

Please write a bulleted list tweet based on this headline.

Here are the rules:

1. It must be 280 characters or less.

2. No hashtags

3. Start with 1 clear, declarative sentence.

4. 3-5 bulleted list of actionable tips.

    *Note: You may notice that ChatGPT doesn’t give you exactly what you want. That’s OK. Remember: “interns” are learning! They are far from perfect. And so the idea is to use ChatGPT as a starting point and accelerate the most manual parts of the work—so that you can focus on the tweaking/editing to get it across the finish line.

    Here’s the script for turning that same headline into a Twitter Thread:

    Rewrite this headline as a Twitter Thread:

{Headline}

A few rules:

1. Make each tweet stand alone
2. No hashtags
3. Use this format:

First sentence = name the "tip"

3-5 bulleted list of how to actionably execute that tip

If there's room, 1 final sentence encouraging the reader.

    *Note: It’s important to not just ask ChatGPT to do something (”Write this as a Twitter Thread”) without giving it some constraints/rules. Otherwise, it won’t know how to create exactly what you’re looking for.

    Here’s the script for turning that same headline into a LinkedIn Post:

    Great, now rewrite this headline as an 800-word LinkedIn post

{Headline}

A few rules:

1. Start with a brief introduction of the topic, citing a statistic or data point and crediting the source
2. Divide the post up into sections/a list
3. Include at least 1 actionable tip and 1 mistake the reader should avoid in each section

    *Note: Since LinkedIn is a very business-forward platform, there’s benefit to leveraging one of ChatGPT’s greatest features (research) and including stats wherever you can. It’s also a great way to immediately elevate the quality of your writing.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_qId!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F330b5105-18e2-4be1-bd71-507f8c29eb7b_1472x1142.png)

    Here’s the script for turning that same headline into an Atomic Essay:

    Atomic Essays are the format we use in Ship 30 for 30 to train writers on the fundamentals of Digital Writing.

    Because once you understand the fundamentals, you can write anything online.

    Here’s the script for training ChatGPT to turn your idea into an Atomic Essay:

    Great, now rewrite this headline as an Atomic Essay:

{Headline}

Atomic Essays are short essays 250 words or less.

Here are a few guidelines:

1. The first sentence of the essay must be short, to the point, and clearly tell the reader the "strong opinion" of the essay.

2. The essay must be structured in a list of 3-7 Main Ideas.

3. After each main idea must be a short description of that Main Idea. These descriptions should be some combination of the following: Reasons why this main idea is important, Mistakes people make when trying to implement this main idea, Tips to implement this main idea, Examples of this main idea, Stats that prove this main idea, etc. 

4. Do not use flowery language or vague examples. Be as specific as possible.

    Finally, here’s the script for turning that same headline into the outline of a long-form blog post:

    The best way to use ChatGPT for this last format is to divide the writing up into 2 separate steps:

    • First, ask ChatGPT to create an “outline” for you.”

    • Second, ask ChatGPT to create individual “Atomic Essays” for each of the main points in your outline.

    This is how you can really start leveraging AI technology to accelerate your writing process.

    Here’s the script for training ChatGPT to turn your idea into the outline of a long-form blog post:

    Great, now create an outline for a long-form blog post with this headline:

{Headline}

A few rules:

1. Organize the outline into main points
2. For each main point, list out 1 actionable tip, 1 mistake, and 1 stat with a source
3. And for each main point, also list out 2 relevant case studies or well-known stories to reference

    Just take a look at the outline ChatGPT produced with this prompt!

    This is as-good as any copywriting intern.

    *Note: From this outline, you can then go point-by-point and ask ChatGPT:

    • “Expand on this Actionable Tip. Here are the rules…”

    • “Expand on this Mistake To Avoid. Here are the rules…”

    • “Expand on this Stat. Here are the rules…”

    • “Expand on this Case Study about ‘How MailChimp used data-driven insights to create highly targeted campaigns for their customers.’ Here are the rules…”

    • Etc.

    This is all the work you would otherwise have to spend hours doing as a writer.

    Which can effectively be outsourced to your new “AI Intern.”

    Which means you get to spend the majority of your time thinking of new frameworks to “teach” it, and editing its work.

    This is how you “ascend” out of being a writer who “gets paid to write,” to becoming a Digital Writer who gets paid to “THINK!”
