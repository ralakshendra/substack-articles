    7 questions to create infinite content

    Author: Nicolas Cole
    Date: 2025-11-16

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!tS2F!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc03e248e-ddeb-480f-887c-b1b5eb354d64_2000x1428.png)

    Every copywriting framework on the planet is a remix of the same 7 questions.

    AIDA, PAS, Before-After-Bridge, StoryBrand—they’re all pulling from the same ingredient list, just in a different order. Which means you don’t need to memorize a dozen frameworks.

    You just need to answer 7 questions. And when you do, you can:

    • Write any type of content without second-guessing yourself

    • Reverse-engineer what makes great copy work

    • Feed AI your answers and get drafts that sound like you

    But nobody teaches these questions—they just keep selling you new frameworks with new acronyms.

    I’m about to show you the 7 questions behind every piece of persuasive writing, and how to use them as your own simple writing system.

    Let’s dive in!

    Question #1: WHERE Is Your WHO…Right Now?

    Once you know who you’re writing to, the next step is to play a game called: Answer The Right Questions In The Right Order. And only if and when you understand those questions, do you get to experience the luxury of AI amplifying your work.

    Here’s the game, in a nutshell:

    1. Describe your target reader’s current state (where they actually are)

    2. Identify their dissatisfaction (why this state bothers them)

    3. Paint their desired state (where they want to be)

    4. Name their obstacle (what’s blocking them)

    5. Present the solution path (what would get them there)

    6. Provide proof (why they should believe it works)

    7. Give them the next action (what to do right now)

    That’s it.

    Unfortunately, 99% of writers using AI approach it through the lens of prompt collection. They want the perfect template, on demand. So, what do they do? They ask AI to “write a LinkedIn post about [topic].”

    And it’s the biggest mistake you can make (because AI doesn’t know where your reader is right now, which means it can’t help you connect the reader, which means your content sounds generic, which means it’s going to take you 1000x longer to finish writing.

    Before you ever prompt AI, you need to answer: Where is your reader right now?

    Are they stuck? Overwhelmed? Skeptical? Excited but confused? The more precisely you can describe their current state, the more persuasive your content becomes.

    Question #2: Why Does This Current State Bother Them?

    Most writers skip this question entirely.

    They jump straight to solutions: “Here’s how to make $10K/month with AI!”

    But persuasion doesn’t work that way.

    People don’t take action because of where they COULD be. They take action because of where they ARE and why it hurts.

    This is the dissatisfaction question.

    Before your reader cares about your solution, they need to feel the weight of their current problem.

    The more specific the pain, the more powerful the persuasion.

    Question #3: Where Do They Actually Want To Be?

    This is your desired state.

    Not where YOU think they should want to be. Where THEY actually want to be.

    Most writers make this mistake constantly:

    • The reader wants “consistent $5K months” but you’re selling them “7-figure business strategy”

    • The reader wants “3 clients who pay on time” but you’re selling them “scale to 50 clients”

    • The reader wants “AI that saves them 10 hours a week” but you’re selling them “AI that writes everything for you”

    Misalignment here kills conversion.

    When you understand their desired state precisely, your content resonates. When you don’t, it bounces off.

    Question #4: What’s Stopping Them From Getting There?

    This is the obstacle question.

    And here’s where most content falls apart: writers describe the wrong obstacle.

    They describe implementation obstacles when the real obstacle is belief. They describe tactical obstacles when the real obstacle is understanding. They describe tool obstacles when the real obstacle is strategy.

    For example:

    • Wrong obstacle: “You don’t have good enough prompts”

    • Right obstacle: “You don’t understand what questions good content needs to answer”

    Or:

    • Wrong obstacle: “You need better AI tools”

    • Right obstacle: “You’re using AI as a replacement for thinking instead of an amplifier of thinking”

    When you identify the real obstacle, your content creates breakthrough moments for your reader.

    Question #5: What Path Would Reliably Get Them There?

    This is your solution.

    But notice the word: reliably.

    Not “might work.” Not “worked for someone else once.” Not “sounds good in theory.”

    What would reliably move them from their current state to their desired state?

    This is where understanding this structure becomes your unfair advantage.

    For example, the path for this post is:

    1. Understand the 7 questions

    2. Answer the questions for your audience

    3. Feed those answers to AI as context

    4. Let AI execute the writing faster

    Question #6: What Would Convince Them This New Path Is Real?

    This is the proof question.

    And it’s not about testimonials or case studies (though those help).

    It’s about addressing the specific doubt in their mind right now.

    If their doubt is “this sounds too complex,” your proof is simplicity demonstration. If their doubt is “this takes too long to learn,” your proof is speed of implementation. If their doubt is “I’ve tried frameworks before and they didn’t work,” your proof is showing how this is different.

    Match your proof to their specific skepticism.

    Question #7: What’s The Next Step They Should Take Right Now?

    Most writers either:

    • Make the action too big (leaving readers paralyzed)

    • Make the action unclear (leaving readers confused)

    • Skip the action entirely (leaving readers inspired but motionless)

    The best next action is the smallest believable step.

    For example, the action isn’t “go rewrite all your content using this 7 question framework.”

    The action is: “Next time you sit down to write OR prompt AI, write out answers to these 7 questions first. Just once. Then see what happens.”

    One Structure, Infinite Variations

    Every “framework” you’ve heard of is just a different arrangement of these core questions.

    But depending on how you order and emphasize them, you create completely different types of content.

    For Example:

    • PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution):

      • Q1: Start with current state

      • Q2: Dig into the pain

      • Q5: Deliver the solution

    • BAB (Before-After-Bridge):

      • Q1: Show them where they are

      • Q3: Paint the future they want

      • Q5: Explain how to get there

    • AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action):

      • Hook + Q2/Q4: Highlight the obstacles

      • Q3: Create desire with the outcome

      • Q7: Push to action

    • 4Ps (Picture-Promise-Proof-Push):

      • Q3: Lead with the desired state

      • Q5: Promise the path

      • Q6: Prove it works

      • Q7: Push them to act

    Same questions. Different order.

    How To Use AI Once You’ve Done The Thinking

    This is where AI becomes powerful.

    You can have Claude or ChatGPT walk you through the whole thing, step by step. The prompt below asks you each question one at a time, offers suggested answers if you get stuck, and then moves you straight into the writing once you’ve done the thinking.

    Here’s how it works:

    • Step 1: Pick a topic. Choose one idea you want to write about today (something you’ve been circling, thinking about, or avoiding).

    • Step 2: Copy the prompt below. Paste it into ChatGPT, Claude, or whatever AI assistant you’re using.

    • Step 3: Hit enter and follow along. The prompt will guide you through each of the 7 questions, one at a time.

    • Step 4: Let AI build your content. Once you’ve answered all 7 questions, the prompt automatically transitions into writing the content for you.

    This works if you want to write a quick post or if you need a few suggestions to get your creative juices flowing. And when you answer the questions first the content sounds more like you. Because the AI isn’t thinking for you. Instead, it’s articulating the thinking you’ve already done.

    Pulling It All Together

    Let’s recap the thinking process you’ve learned:

    • Step 1: Identify your audience (who you’re writing for / so that they can achieve X)

    • Step 2: Notice their problems, solutions, and outcomes

    • Step 3: Shape those into obvious and non-obvious combinations

    • Step 4: Answer the 7 questions to write your content

    Once you’ve completed these steps, writing becomes easy. Because you’re not staring at a blank page wondering what to say. You’ve already done the hard work—the thinking.

    Now you just need to arrange it in the right order for the piece you’re creating. The thinking is the same. Only the arrangement changes.

    That’s it!
