Ahoy, Digital Writers!

Clichés are the death of originality.

Readers know they are there—and they skip right over them.

  • They’re obvious

  • They’re predictable

  • And they’re unemotional

Using them makes your writing easy to ignore, it makes you seem lazy, and you risk losing the reader all together.

Avoid them like the plague! 😜

BUT there is a simple way to use a cliché to your advantage that will make readers pause and pay attention. And today we are going to show you how to hook a reader with a cliché (and get ChatGPT to help)!

But first, let’s look at why we use clichés in the first place.

Why Clichés Stick (And Stay Stuck)

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9DoM!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4168086b-0d16-4949-b57a-1c7ffffe9e86_834x408.png)

Clichés are the “fast food” of language.

Which is why they catch fire—because we are lazy, energy conserving humans!

  • They help you fit in.

  • They shortcut deep thinking.

  • They help you simplify communication.

Convenient? Absolutely.

But not always the most enriching option.

There is an upside.

If it’s cliché, it’s overused. If it’s overused, it’s ignored. But to be ignored means it’s recognized. And as a writer you can capitalize on the familiar.

Here’s why:

Readers want to feel smart—like they are part of the “in crowd.” It’s why niching down works so well. But they also want to be surprised. Nobody loves a mystery they can predict.

So, how do you get a reader to sit up and take notice?

How To Hook A Reader With A Worn Out Cliché

Use a “Persuasive Twist.”

Take a cliché and flip it on its head.

  • Cliché: “try before you buy”

  • Twist: “buy before you fly”

The reader expects the opposite: “try before you fly.”

And when you flip it on its head, the reader can’t help but give it a double take!

4 Steps To Write A Persuasive Twist Hook With ChatGPT

This prompt is inspired by Todd Brison’s article: “Are Clichés Actually a Writer's Best Friend?”

And we are going to break it down into 4 steps.

The first step is yours!

Step 1: Choose a broad “umbrella” topic.

Most clichés are tied to big, broad topics because they're trying to capture universal truths or experiences that many people can relate to.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9tZ5!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7744a9bf-57ba-4045-ad9b-cfd829243272_1126x370.png)

Specific niche topics work too, but we recommend starting with the umbrella topic your niche sits within to capture the most common clichés.

Once you have your topic, it’s time to call in ChatGPT for the next 2 steps.

Step 2: List all the clichés you can think for a topic.

Common wisdom IS ChatGPT’s wheelhouse.

20 overused sayings about money? No problem!

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vlQO!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Faab2b123-59c5-4a57-b623-62dffcbc5820_1088x1040.png)

Have fun with this one.

Now, it’s time to flip it!

Step 3: Add/remove one word to change the clichés meaning.

Once you have the list of clichés for your topic, we ask ChatGPT to swap out 1 word in each phrase to give it a completely different meaning—so it lands and emotional punch (more effectively than the cliche itself).

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sH6j!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fddfcab24-6fbb-41d9-9c43-721fb4373211_1086x1032.png)

Not all of the rewrites are going to land, but I see a few that look promising!

“Money is a necessary sequel.”

This is a great line you could use if you talk about free work as a growth strategy.

Pick out the ones that catch your attention, made you think, or create a new connection in your brain. If one of the phrases got you to notice, someone else will too.

On to the last step!

Step 4: Use them in your headlines, emails, and ads.

Or as a single sentence opener like this:

Clichés are cliché for a season—until you add a persuasive twist!

Here’s the prompt:

I am going to train you to write a “Persuasive Twist.”

A Persuasive Twist starts the reader down a “usual path” and then changes the path so it so grabs the reader’s attention. A Persuasive Twist gives you enough meaning to understand, but also create a burning need to figure out the rest.

Here’s what we are going to do to create Persuasive Twists:

I will give you a topic and you will give me a list of 10 well-known cliches related to the topic.

Then you will take each well-known cliche and swap out or add a word to give the phrase a completely different tone so it lands and emotional punch (more effectively than the cliche itself).

Each phrase should be instantly catchy and memorable.

For example:
CLICHE: What doesn’t kill you makes you stronger.
PERSUASIVE TWIST: What doesn’t kill you makes you money.

Another example:
CLICHE: 0 to 60
PERSUASIVE TWIST: 0 to 60… eventually.

Another example:
CLICHE: The best things in life are free.
PERSUASIVE TWIST: The best things in life are dairy-free.

Do you understand?

Let me know when you are ready for the topic.

Boom!

That’s it for today!
