The 4-Step Email Storyselling Formula

This is how you can turn almost any experience from your personal life or business into a story-driven email that sells your offer in 15 minutes or less.

All you have to do is follow these 4 steps:

STEP 1: Story

→ “What do you see?”

Most people are sleepwalking through life. They don’t pay attention to the world around them. So become an avid observer. Take note of what you see happening in front of you at the gym, grocery store, online, with clients, etc.

STEP 2: Lesson

→ “What does it mean?”

Most people struggle to understand why they haven’t succeeded. If your story was an essay, this is the conclusion. Explain what it reveals about the world or human nature.

STEP 3: Pivot

→ “How does it also apply to your audience?”

Most people are stimulus rich, but context poor. Show them how the lesson also applies to getting the result they want. This is the bridge to your offer.

STEP 4: Call to action

→ “What should they do next?”

Once you’ve connected the dots, all you have to do is give them a link to buy. Keep it casual. Let the story do the selling FOR you instead.

As you can see, this lets you deliver value (and sell) through storytelling instead of step-by-step guides or in-depth tutorials.

Save those for buyers.

(unless you get the chance to do a guest post for Write With AI ;)

As for why this works, the answer is simple:

“Show, don’t tell” is the golden rule in writing. And storytelling is the ultimate expression of it. This is how you’ll showcase your values, beliefs, experience, and personality to attract clients & customers.

Remember: people buy coaches, not coaching.

Now let’s get to work.

What You’ll Need to Write Your Story-Driven Email With AI

Claude is the undisputed champion of AI copywriting.

So we’re going to use Sonnet 3.5 and ‘Normal’ style on a Pro account to write our email.

On top of that, you’ll need to fill in placeholders for:

  • Your offer

  • Your target audience

  • How you developed your unique method for getting results

Now, these don’t need to be super long or fleshed out. But the last one is important because it’s the story we’re going to use to sell our offer.

You’ll see how I did this below.

Let’s dive into the first prompt.

Prompt 1: Create Your Outline

First, we’re going to get Claude to create an outline for us.

This gives us the chance to tweak it before it writes the full email.

In this first prompt, we’re going to:

  • Assign a role

  • Explain the framework

  • Show it a good example (outline + full email)

SIDE NOTE: I’ve used an example of my writing to train it. Once you’ve written some story-driven emails of your own, swap one in so the voice and tone sound more like you. My book can teach you how to do this.

Now, here’s the full prompt:

You are a world class direct response copywriter with an expertise in writing story-driven email emails that sell products and services. These always follow the same 4-step formula: 

STEP 1: Story (what do you see?)
STEP 2: Lesson (what does it mean?)
STEP 3: Pivot (how does it also apply or serve as an analogy or metaphor for the audience’s goal?)
STEP 4: Call to action (what should the audience do next?)

Prompt 2: Write Your Email

Next, we’re going to turn our outline into a fully-written email.

We’ll include some additional guidelines to make sure our email hits hard.

Here’s the full prompt:

Excellent! Now you’re going to turn this outline into a story-driven email that sells in the exact same style as the example I showed you. Remember, you’re a world-class direct response copywriter.

Here are some additional instructions for the email:

- Strong declarative opener
- Story includes specifics (numbers and details)
- Conversational language
- Includes one simile, analogy, or metaphor
- Good visual flow (lots of variance in sentence length)
- Smooth transitions
- Bolding and all caps for emphasis in select areas
- Casual pitch at the end (using ‘if/then’ framework)
- 250-450 words long
- Grade 4 reading level

Be specific, creative, and emotional.

Prompt 3: Pick a High-Curiosity Subject Line

As Gary Halbert once said:

The only thing more important than self-interest in copy is curiosity.

So the last step is to make sure we’ve got a strong high-curiosity subject line that forces people to open our email.

Now, Claude will typically include a suggested subject line when it writes the email.

Sometimes this first attempt is good, but sometimes it’s crap.

So we’re going to generate a bunch more subject lines to choose from, based on 6 proven frameworks with examples from myself and other people in my inbox.

Here’s the full prompt:

Amazing! This is pretty good. Now suggest 5 high-curiosity subject lines for this email from each of the following categories:

***

1) Create curiosity by teasing an insight or takeaway without revealing it

Examples:

- how a Utah man built a 1.1 million person email list without spending a dime

- The productivity secret of Hollywood’s most prolific screenwriter

- he acquired a 53,000 person Facebook group for free (here's how)

- this g-doc sold thousands of low-mid ticket products

- this one 5-min video filled 15 sales rep calendars last month…

2) Create contrast by juxtaposing two opposite ideas

Examples:

- the 4-page “anti salesletter” that’s crushing

- why you should ignore 95% of the people on your list

- horrible email with typos & repeat sentences brings in 200+ buyers a day

- How to do discounts without turning on bums & cheapskates

- she couldn’t speak English… now she’s having 5-figure months as a copywriter

3) Create controversy by challenging mainstream wisdom

Examples:

- When NOT to use story in copy, messaging and marketing

- Why A-Player sales reps don’t need scripts

- Why most high-ticket biz owners don’t need closers

- forget challenges & low-ticket funnels (do this instead)

- From 15k/m to 90k/m with ZERO ad spend? (here's how)

4) Highlight drama, gossip, or newsworthy events

Examples:

- Ben Settle banned by Amazon?

- Alex Hormozi didn't get rich selling "hard work"

- GONE ROGUE? New closer breaks the rules (screenshot inside)

- Addressing Justin Welsh's recent comments on email frequency

- reader accuses me of using "fake scarcity" (here's my response)

5) Focus on specificity by including numbers and results

Examples:

- I made $6887 an hour while lying on the beach in Cancun (here's how)

- how to turn a tiny list into a 5-figure email promo (case study)

- 7-min loom video generates $43,200? (here's how)

- This one hire will make me 1.8 million this year…

- 683 buyers in a day with a low-priced "pocket change" offer (here's how)

6) Use short keywords & phrases to signal relevance to the audience

Examples:

- recurring rev

- fire your leads

- newsletter math

- high earnings per subscriber

- 7-figure newsletter playbook

***

Important: The subject line should NOT give away the key lesson or insight.

Remember, you're a world class direct response copywriter, so you know these must generate maximum curiosity when subscribers see them in the inbox. Use power words and emotionally resonant language. Be creative!

Similar to the first prompt, you can customize this with your own examples if you like.
