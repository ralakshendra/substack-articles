    One of the best ways to grow your list is to tap into other creators' audiences.

    That’s where sponsorships come into play.

    The concept is simple: you pay another creator a small fee to get featured in their newsletter.

    I’ve been using this technique for the past 2 years. And it has been a significant catalyst in filling my email list with subscribers who not only read my newsletter…

    But who also buy my digital products.

    So in this article, I’m going to walk you through the exact process to grow your list with sponsorships.

    Step 1: Find 50 newsletters to sponsor

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!_Cua!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F14c59698-fefc-493b-8689-76bb45778979_1436x916.png)

    The first step is to find the right newsletters to sponsor.

    You want creators in your niche with engaged audiences who are also open to sponsorships.

    AI tools (with Research mode) can significantly speed up your research process that used to take me HOURS to do manually.

    You could use:

    • Grok

    • Perplexity

    • ChatGPT

    Make sure you turn on Research mode (or even better: Deep Research mode.)

    Here's the exact prompt I use:

    #ROLE: 
Expert research assistant specialized in newsletter discovery.

#TASK: 
Identify suitable creators for potential sponsorship opportunities.

#INSTRUCTIONS: 
Provide a list of 50 newsletter creators who meet ALL of the following criteria:

1. Niche: Health, fitness, or wellbeing
2. Target Audience: Men aged 35 and older
3. Frequency: Send at least 1 email per week
4. Monetization: Accept sponsorships (or partnerships)
5. Size Constraint: Have under 150,000 subscribers (to avoid high sponsorship fees)

#OUTPUT FORMAT: 
List each creator with the following details:

- Creator name
- Newsletter name (if different)
- Creator big ideas (core philosophies)
- Website or signup link
- Estimated subscriber count
- Sponsorship details (or link to sponsorship page)
- Posting frequency
- Target demographic (if available)

#ADDITIONAL NOTES: 
Avoid newsletters that are too broad or general. Prioritize creators with a personal or niche angle who are actively growing but not yet saturated with sponsors.

    Now, take this prompt and replace the niche and target audience with whatever your specific data. Marketing, productivity, AI writing, personal finance – whatever.

    I suggest running the same prompt across different LLMs to get more options.

    My preference goes for creators with 3,000-50,000 subscribers. They're big enough to have an engaged audience but small enough to actually respond to your emails, and offer a good deal.

    The mega-creators? They're either booked solid or charge stupid money. The micro-creators (under 1,000) often don't have enough reach to justify the effort.

    What to look for in the results:

    • Newsletters that regularly feature guest content or sponsors

    • Creators are active and engaged with their audience

    • People in your exact niche (don't go too broad)

    • Contact information that's actually accessible

    Once you have your list, spend 10 minutes researching each creator. Read their recent newsletters. Check out their social media. You want to understand their vibe before you reach out to make sure it fits your brand.

    Step 2: Create an irresistible lead magnet

    There’s one thing people get wrong about sponsorships.

    They try to pitch their newsletter directly. "Hey, join my newsletter for marketing tips!"

    The best way to get people onto your list through sponsorships isn't to sell your newsletter. It's to give away something valuable that solves a real problem.

    That's where the good ol’ lead magnet jumps in.

    I know that you're already familiar with lead magnets, but I see so many people getting this wrong.

    There are two mistakes that you need to avoid:

    First, generic PDFs like"10 Tips for Better Productivity". The problem? They're not solving a specific, urgent problem.

    The second is trying to “bruteforce” the problem?

    What do I mean by bruteforcing?

    Depending on the newsletter that you're sponsoring, you might slightly need to tweak your lead magnet.

    Please read that again.

    To give you an example, when I'm targeting newsletters that mainly target bloggers, my lead magnet talks to bloggers. When I'm sponsoring an email list that talks about email marketing, I’m tweaking my lead magnet to speak to email marketers.

    And if you don't want to spend all your day creating lead magnets, you need a way to do it fast.

    Different types of lead magnets that convert:

    Okay, before showing you how to create your lead magnet, or to at least find lead magnet ideas, let's just think about the format.

    Here are some formats that worked well for me:

    • Cheat sheets: Quick reference guides for complex processes. Think "Retirement Planning Checklist for Your 40s" or “First-Time Home Buyer's Timeline & Checklist"

    • Case studies: Behind-the-scenes breakdowns of how you (or someone else) achieved a specific result. People love seeing the actual process.

    • Free email courses: My personal favorite. A 5-7 day sequence that teaches one skill step-by-step (and generates automated income.)

    Which one should you choose?

    It depends on your audience and what you're comfortable creating. I found that the format doesn't matter as long as you nail the problem.

    But here's a shortcut – use AI to figure out what your audience actually wants.

    Use this prompt:

    #Role: 
Expert marketer and audience insight extractor.

#Task: 
Help me identify the core pain points of my audience and turn them into compelling lead magnet ideas.

# Instructions:

I want to attract [INSERT YOUR AUDIENCE – e.g., “coaches over 40 who want to grow online” or “busy professionals trying to lose weight”].

They know they’re stuck, but they often can’t articulate exactly what the real problem is.

Start by identifying the top 3 root problems this audience is actually struggling with (not just surface-level frustrations).

Then, for each problem, give me:

- A brief description of the pain, in the words the audience might use

- 1–2 specific lead magnet ideas that:
  - Solve an immediate pain
  - Deliver a small but meaningful win
  - Are easy to consume (checklists, cheat sheets, templates, mini-guides, etc.)

For each lead magnet idea, include:

- Title/Format
- Why it’s valuable (what pain it solves)
- Outcome (what result they’ll get or feel)

# Constraints:

- Avoid generic “ebooks” or “ultimate guides”
- Avoid anything that takes more than 10 minutes to use
- Think tactical, specific, and emotionally compelling

Example problems to use as a reference (don’t copy):

- “I don’t know what kind of content to post”
- “I feel like I’m shouting into the void”
- “I want to grow but I don’t want to spend all day online”

    This prompt saves you from guessing what people want. Instead of creating what you think they need, you're creating what they're actually searching for.

    Step 3: Create your opt-in page in 5 minutes with AI

    Once you have your lead magnet, you need an opt-in page.

    Don't overthink this. You're not building the next Amazon. You just need a page that clearly explains what people get in exchange for their email.

    I used to spend hours tweaking opt-in pages. Moving buttons around. Testing different colors. Waste. Of. Time.

    The formula is simple: One big promise + three clear benefits + an email capture form.

    That's it.

    Here's the exact structure that works:

    • Headline: The main benefit or outcome they'll get

    • 3 bullet points: Specific things they'll learn or achieve

    • Email form: Keep it simple - just email address

    • Call-to-action button: "Get My Free [LEAD MAGNET NAME]"

    • Nice to have: a visual representation of what they’ll get

    Instead of spending hours writing copy, let AI do the heavy lifting:

    Use this opt-in page creation prompt:

    # Role: 
Expert conversion copywriter specializing in high-converting opt-in pages.

# Task: 
Create a compelling opt-in page that maximizes conversions for my lead magnet.

# Context:

- Lead magnet: [LEAD MAGNET NAME]
- Target audience: [SPECIFIC AUDIENCE]
- Core problem: [MAIN PROBLEM THEY'RE STRUGGLING WITH]
- Desired outcome: [WHAT THEY WANT TO ACHIEVE]

Structure the page with:

1. Headline (focus on the transformation/outcome, not the product)
  - Use "How [Audience] [Achieve Desired Result] Without [Main Obstacle]" format
  - Include emotional trigger words
  - Keep under 15 words

2. Subheadline (amplify the promise with specificity)
  - Add credibility element or method preview
  - Mention the format/delivery method

3. Three benefit bullets (focus on outcomes, not features)
  - Include the "even if" objection handler
  - Start each with the emotional benefit
  - Add curiosity
  - Use specific numbers/timeframes when possible

4. CTA button (action-oriented, benefit-focused)
  - Use first-person language ("YES, I WANT...")
  - Include the main benefit
  - Create urgency without being pushy

# Tone: 
Conversational, empathetic, results-focused. Write like you're talking to a friend who's frustrated and needs hope.

# Constraints:
- No hype or exaggerated claims
- Address their skepticism directly
- Focus on immediate, tangible benefits
- Use their language, not marketing speak

    This gives you professional copy in under 30 seconds. No need to stare at a blank page wondering what to write.

    Step 4: Write your sponsorship ad blurb copy

    Okay, now you have a lead magnet, you also have an opt-in page, and you also have newsletters to sponsor.

    Now, it’s time to write your sponsorship ad.

    Some creators will do it for you and for some you will need to do it yourself.

    Different newsletters have different formats.

    Here's what I usually do:

    I spent 15 minutes reading their last 10 newsletters. I look for:

    • How long are the sponsorships?

    • Do they use stories or just bullet points?

    • What's the tone - casual or professional?

    • Where do they place the sponsors in the newsletter?

    Then I ask the creator directly: "What format works best for your audience?"

    Most creators appreciate that you're thinking about their readers, not just your own goals.

    Then, you want to model what worked in the past, and simply feed this to AI with examples.

    Head over to ChatGPT and use these 2 prompts:

    SHORT COPY PROMPT:

    Write a short classified ad (50-75 words) for promoting my lead magnet: 

How to lose belly fat for busy dads in just 11 minute a day 

Focus on the main benefit and include a clear call-to-action. 

Keep it punchy and direct.

Audience = dads, 38+, with belly fat

Write like the audience talks
