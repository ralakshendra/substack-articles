    Step 1: Stop Guessing Who You're Talking To (Get Specific About Your ICP)

    Most writers think they know their audience.

    • "I help entrepreneurs." (Which entrepreneurs?)

    • "I work with busy professionals." (Busy doing what? Brain surgery or data entry?)

    • "I serve online business owners." (Cool story. So does everyone else on online)

    These aren't audiences.

    These are demographics having an identity crisis.

    Here's the problem:

    Most writers start by trying to figure out their audience, then wonder what to sell them.

    That's backwards.

    This is why I teach my students to start with their offer first.

    When you get clear on your offer, everything else falls into place - your positioning, your niche, your ICP, your entire revenue model.

    The offer-first framework works like this:

    • WHO you help (your specific person, not a broad category)

    • WHAT you help them achieve (the specific transformation or outcome)

    • HOW you help them achieve it (your unique method or approach)

    • WHY you're different/novel compared to what's already out there

    When you nail these four elements, you don't have to guess who your audience is anymore. Your offer tells you exactly who to target and what they need to hear.

    Here's the reality:

    The riches are in the niches, and the niches are revealed through your offer.

    This isn't just an exercise for your vision board.

    This is business intelligence that separates successful writers from broke influencers.

    When you start with your offer instead of your audience, your content stops being background noise and starts being a magnet for people who actually need what you're selling.

    That's when followers become clients instead of just dopamine hits.

    Step 2: Extract The PROMISE From Your Offer (This Changes Everything)

    Once you have your offer foundation, you need to extract the PROMISE.

    The PROMISE is your WHAT + HOW combined into one irresistible statement.

    It's not just what you deliver - it's the specific transformation your clients can expect.

    This is crucial because your PROMISE gives readers a reason to lean in.

    It's the foundation and positioning from where all your content is going to be born.

    Without a clear PROMISE, your content has no direction.

    (ReAd ThAt AgAiN)

    You're just creating random helpful tips instead of strategic content that moves people toward your specific solution.

    But with a clear PROMISE, every piece of content becomes intentional.

    You're not just sharing productivity tips - you're sharing productivity insights that lead to your specific productivity transformation.

    You're not just giving business advice - you're giving business advice that points toward your unique business outcome.

    Here's how to extract your PROMISE:

    "I help [WHO] achieve [WHAT] through [HOW] because [WHY]."

    Example:

    "I help mid-level marketing professionals land dream job interviews and negotiate 30%+ salary increases through executive-level interview prep and psychology-based salary negotiation strategies."

    That PROMISE becomes your content North Star.

    Every post should either reinforce why this transformation matters, address obstacles to achieving it, or demonstrate what life looks like after you've achieved it.

    That's how you move from random content creation to strategic content that builds your business.

    Step 3: Uncover The Fears, Desires, And Frustrations That Actually Drive Purchases

    Now that you have your ICP and your PROMISE, it's time to dig into the psychology of purchasing.

    Here's what most writers miss while they're busy being "authentic": People don't buy because of what you offer.

    They buy because of what they're desperately trying to escape or achieve.

    Your ideal client has specific fears that wake them up at 3 AM in a cold sweat.

    Specific desires they'd sell their firstborn to achieve.

    Specific frustrations that make them want to throw their computer out the window.

    When your content speaks directly to these emotions, magic happens.

    This is where you use your ICP and PROMISE together to extract the deep psychological drivers that make people buy.

    Here is the prompt:

    My target audience is < your target ICP>

My offer helps these people a <The PROMISE from your One Page Offer>

I want you to make 4 lists with 50 bullet points for each:

- 1 list of their most painful and common problems.

- 1 list of their most common and annoying frustrations.

- 1 list of their most sought after desires and wants

- 1 list of their top objections to not buy my offer.

    This gives you 200 content ideas that are psychologically designed to resonate with your exact audience.

    Step 4: Turn One Insight Into Targeted Content That Converts

    Now comes the fun part: turning those insights into content that pays your bills.

    Most writers treat content creation like some mystical art form.

    They light candles, burn sage, and wait for the content gods to bless them with inspiration.

    Instead, pick one item from your fears, frustrations, or desires list and create targeted content around it.

    Here is the prompt:

    I want the topic to be about [OUTCOME FROM OFFER] and one of the biggest frustrations/obstacles they face in achieving that is [FRUSTRATION]. I want to talk about how to solve it.

    For example:

    I want the topic to be about [why understanding your market value] is paramount to the overall success and prosperity of your career. This is especially important considering [salary stagnation in your current role]. Most of the time you can remedy underpayment because you don't know how to articulate your true worth. let's illustrate how to remedy this.

    See how specific that is?

    You're not writing generic content about knowing your worth. You're writing targeted content that speaks directly to a specific frustration (salary stagnation) for a specific audience (mid-level marketing professionals) with a specific solution (understanding and articulating market value).

    That's how you move from content that gets likes to content that gets clients.

< Start of Original Content >

< End of Original Content >

STEP 2: You must ask me for the new topic before you write the new post.

    The "topic” that you're supposed to be using in step two is what we just created in the last section.

    Step 6: The Secret Sauce - Making Every Piece Of Content Lead Somewhere (Besides More Scrolling)

    Here's what separates successful writers from digital busybodies:

    Every piece of content has a job to do.

    It's not just there to get likes from other writers who are also trying to get likes from other writers in an endless circle of mutual validation.

    Your content should move the right people closer to a buying decision.

    It should make qualified prospects think "I need to know more about working with this person."

    And that is done by your call to action.

    If you’re a [DREAM CLIENT/ICP] who’s looking for [OUTCOME]

I’m looking to work with [X DREAM CLIENTS] to help them get [PROMISE IN TIMEFRAME]

DM me “KEYWORD” and I’ll get you the details.

    Which for our purposes would look something like this:

    If you're a [mid-level marketing professional] who's looking for [your next career breakthrough]

    I'm looking to work with [5 ambitious professionals] to help them [land dream interviews and negotiate 30%+ salary increases in the next 90 days]

    DM me "CAREER" and I'll get you the details.

    This is the difference between content that gets you invited to podcasts and content that gets you invited to wire transfer money.

    When someone reads your post and thinks "This person has clearly been reading my diary," they don't just double-tap - they slide into your DMs asking how much it costs to solve their exact problem.

    And that, my friends, is when content becomes currency.
