Curate stories with Claude! Speed up your audience growth

Author: Nicolas Cole
Date: 2026-01-11

When it comes to building an audience & getting into a content rhythm, there is a simple way you can “speed up the process.”

Let’s say:

  • You are starting with 100 followers or less...

  • Your library of content is just getting started...

  • You don’t feel comfortable sharing your own ideas yet...

  • Or you want to do everything you can to accelerate your audience growth...!

Curation is a powerful way for you to “borrow” other people’s credibility and experience while you build your own.

And the reason Curated Content is so effective is because basically what the reader is “paying” you to do (paying with their attention) is to take the time to do the homework, go sift through all the possible material, and emerge with only the best stuff.

For example, there are over 7,000 TED Talks available online. And if someone is interested in a specific topic like leadership, productivity, or creativity, they would most likely want to know:

  • Which 10 TED Talks on leadership are most actionable

  • Which 10 talks best explain productivity techniques for remote workers

  • Which 10 creativity talks are most inspiring for writers specifically

  • And so on.

To someone exploring a new topic, you “curating” the best content is extremely valuable and helpful. In fact, it’s most likely the sort of thing this target reader would Bookmark, save for later, and refer back to multiple times.

Which means your goal is to always be thinking: “What can I do to save my target reader a ton of time? What can I organize for them? What can I get my hands dirty exploring, so they don’t have to? How can I make my target reader’s life as easy as possible?”

Here’s how to get started:

Create a list of things you can curate that are relevant to your niche.

The most effective way to use Curation in your “content strategy” is to consider things that lots of people would be interested in (how can you make it universal?) without abandoning your chosen topic area.

Here’s a quick list of 10 things that are universal (everyone likes this stuff) that can be easily curated:

  • Lessons worth learning

  • Mistakes worth avoiding

  • Tips worth using

  • Frameworks worth using/knowing about

  • Stories worth hearing

  • People worth following on social media

  • Books worth reading

  • YouTube videos worth watching

  • TedTalks worth listening to

  • Podcasts worth listening to

You can see how all these things are universal—and could be applied to anything.

Focus on stories.

A compelling story from 5 or 10 years ago is still just as interesting today.

This means you have a huge library of material to draw from.

For example, using our Writing Advice topic:

  • This crazy story about Hunter S. Thompson worth keeping in mind if you are an aspiring novelist

  • The little-known story of the book that influenced how Charlie Munger ran his annual meetings

  • The surprising story of how Stephen King’s wife rescued Carrie from the trash

  • The unconventional writing ritual Maya Angelou used to write all her books

  • How Toni Morrison started her writing career at 39 while working full-time

See? Easy!

By using Curation, you can leverage other people’s expertise, experience, credibility, and social standing—and tap into wider and wider audiences (without ever abandoning your niche).

And with story, before your reader can even think to themselves, ‘Do I want to read this?’ they’re already off and running.

Now create your own list of stories you can curate related to your topic!

Start with 5 stories related to your topic that taught you something valuable, surprised you, or changed how you think about your niche.

Use this simple formula to setup your story:

The [adjective] story of [person/company/event] worth [knowing/remembering/hearing] if you [target reader’s situation]

For example, if your niche is productivity:

  • The unexpected story of how Elon Musk structures his day worth studying if you manage multiple projects

  • The counterintuitive approach Cal Newport used to become a professor and bestselling author simultaneously—proof you don’t need to wake up at 5am

  • How a Fortune 500 CEO eliminated all meetings for one month worth trying if you feel overwhelmed by your calendar

You can curate stories for infinite topics.

For example:

  • LA Lakers

  • Home gardening

  • A-list copywriters

  • Formula one racing

  • Apocalypse prepping

  • Specialty coffee roasting

  • Books famous people recommend

You’re only limited by your imagination.

You could use this exact same story in multiple ways depending on your goal:

  • Option 1: Tell it straight Just share the story as-is with minimal commentary. Sometimes a great story needs no explanation. Your readers will extract their own meaning, and you’ve provided value simply by surfacing something interesting they wouldn’t have found on their own.

  • Option 2: Extract a universal lesson “This story perfectly illustrates the power of genuine enthusiasm. Portman didn’t write a review or create a formal recommendation. She just kept handing the book to people for three years. That’s the kind of authentic advocacy that changes careers—and creates $100 million film projects.

  • Option 3: Tie it to your niche For example, if you’re in marketing: This is word-of-mouth marketing at its finest. No ads—just one passionate advocate who wouldn’t stop talking about something she loved.

  • Option 4: Use it as a hook for a larger point Start your next piece with this story, then pivot: “Now, you might be thinking you need Natalie Portman’s platform to make recommendations that matter. You don’t. Here’s how unknown creators are using the same principle...”

See how versatile one curated story can be?

You’re not locked into one interpretation or one use case. How you frame it depends entirely on what point you’re trying to make.

Now you might be thinking, “But I don’t know any stories. Where do I find them?”

Good news. I’ve created a prompt that finds them for you.

Here’s how it works:

  1. Copy the prompt below

  2. Head to Claude.ai (you can use the free version)

  3. CRITICAL: Make sure Web Search is enabled in your settings

  4. Paste the prompt and replace {TOPIC} with your niche

  5. Watch as Claude finds 10 fascinating true stories for you

Here’s the prompt:

**CRITICAL RULE:** You must use web_search to find REAL stories. Do not generate or make up examples.

**Your task:** 

Find **10 small, fascinating human-interest stories** from the world of {TOPIC}.

Stories must be unusual, provocative, titillating, or simply intriguing.

**TARGET STYLE:**

Your hooks MUST follow this exact pattern:
**— One sentence**
**— Cinematic, specific moment in time**
**— Starts in the middle of an event (“in medias res”)**
**— No summary, no generalization, no explanation**
**— Feels like the opening line of a documentary or crime story**

**Reference hooks (imitate EXACT structure):**
• *In 2008, Anthony Curcio needed twenty-five men to help him on a job in Monroe, Washington. So he advertised on Craigslist.*
• *In 1503, a Florentine artist took on a commission for a local businessman. It wasn’t considered anything special.*
• *When Steve Jobs returned to Apple, the smartest thing he did was spot Jonathan Ive.*
• *California has more car crashes than any state in the USA, yet 95% of people say organ donation is a good thing.*

OUTPUT FORMAT:

For each story, output:

**[story-engine hook]**
One sentence in the cinematic hook style above.

**[story]**
3–6 sentences telling the true story concisely, with concrete details.

**[source]**
Cite the exact page(s) pulled from web search.

**TOPIC:** {insert topic}

Prompting Tip: The prompt requires Claude to cite sources. By forcing the AI to link back to real web pages, you ensure every story is not a hallucination. However, I recommend you double-check the details of the story to be safe.

If you to take this prompt further, you can not only curate stories but also extract lessons and connect them to your offer.

Simply add these three lines to the OUTPUT FORMAT section and fill in you offer:

**[simple lesson. appeal to the reader's self interest]**

**[smooth segue and correlate to benefit of my offer]**

**[if/then cta]**

**OFFER**{Insert your offer}

Now Claude will automatically:

  • Extract a key lesson from each story

  • Connect it to YOUR specific offer

  • Give you a ready-to-use call-to-action

This transforms curated stories into a complete content marketing system.

To wrap up:

The whole point here is to show you that you don’t need to be the world’s foremost expert to build an audience online. Indeed, you might have started out thinking you needed years of experience and dozens of original insights before you could publish anything worth reading. But by now, I hope you’ve realized that curation is a legitimate—and powerful—strategy for growing your audience while you’re still developing your own voice and expertise.

I don’t necessarily expect you to abandon writing original content altogether. Rather, it would be helpful if you spent your first 30-90 days mixing curated content with your own emerging insights. And stories are a great way to go.

Give it a try.

You may find that curated stories get more engagement than anything else you’ve written, giving you valuable data about what your audience wants from you!
