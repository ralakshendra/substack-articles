    Yesterday I showed you how to use AI to analyze a brain dump and get your creative juices flowing.

    But the hardest part of writing is starting.

    “So, what do you do when your mind’s blank?”

    Good question.

    Here’s what I do:

    I pull out my journal and write at the top of the page:

    “Recently I’ve...”

    Then I list 10 things I’ve recently done, experienced, tried, explored, learned, or noticed. Then I pick one. And write 250 words on it.

    Each of these are incredibly unique & authentic experiences that AI cannot replicate

    But even with this method, I still get stuck.

    Which is why I trained Claude to pull ideas out of me.

    Turn Claude Into Your “Recently I’ve” Co-writing Partner

    When you’re trying to figure out what to write on your own, it’s easy to get stuck inside your own head.

    You overlook what’s interesting because you’re too close to it. Everything feels obvious or boring.

    But when someone asks you questions:

    • “What did you work on yesterday?”

    • “Why did you do it that way?”

    • “What made you decide that?”

    Suddenly you’re explaining.

    And when you explain, interesting things come bubbling out of your brain.

    Questions give you direction when you’re stuck. They help you dig deeper than you would on your own. And they help you see what’s worth writing about before you waste time writing about the wrong thing.

    This prompt helps you reverse engineer educational “thought-leadership” style content from your everyday life so you never run out of things to say.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!y6iY!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9723b062-1230-4c72-97a9-ce084059a722_1628x1258.png)

    Here’s how it works:

    1. Claude asks you what you worked on yesterday

    2. Then it drills down with “why” and “how” questions to extract insights

    3. Then it shows you who else would benefit from your experience

    4. Then it generates 3-5 headline options for you

    5. And finally, it helps you write the content in whatever form you choose

    The beauty of this approach is you start with YOUR personal credibility.

    And because you’re having a conversation instead of staring at a blank page, you bypass the “I don’t know what to write” problem completely.

    Let me show you how to set this up for yourself in 4 easy steps.

    Step 1: Create a new Claude Project

    In case you’re not familiar, Claude Projects is a feature inside the Claude AI platform that lets you create a dedicated workspace (or “project”) for a specific task, topic, or workflow.

    Inside your project, you can:

    • Upload documents, files, references that matter for your topic.

    • Set up custom instructions or “how you want Claude to behave”

    • Maintain your chat history, context, and knowledge base specific for the project.

    This helps you keep everything relevant to one topic together (instead of starting fresh every time).

    To do this, go to claude.ai and click “Projects” in the left sidebar. Create a new project and name it something like “Daily Writing Coach.”

    Step 2: Copy the “Recently I’ve” prompt into your Claude project instructions

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!xEJn!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc8e749c4-ce21-4245-9866-6493a422fc3c_2730x1356.png)

    I’ve put together a complete prompt that does all of this for you (link below.)

    Copy the prompt from that page and paste it into your project’s “Custom Instructions” section.

    Step 3: Power-level your Claude project with artifacts

    Once you have the basic project set up, you can supercharge it by adding three additional tools to your project artifacts:

    1. The Outliner: Helps you take your story and turn it into a structured outline

    2. The Section Expander: Takes one section and expands it into full paragraphs

    3. The Question Bank: 50+ questions Claude can pull from to dig deeper into your stories

    All three of these are available on the same Notion page as the main prompt.

    👉 Click here to grab the prompts.

    Just copy each one into your project’s knowledge base, and Claude will automatically know how to use them.

    Step 4: Start your first daily writing conversation

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!LaC2!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5b018573-299e-45e2-8745-97d87a39e84e_1720x772.png)

    Open a new chat in your project and say “Hello!”

    Claude will immediately start asking you questions about your day.

    After 15-30 minutes of conversation, you’ll have:

    • A story you can share

    • A lesson worth sharing

    • A clarifying topic statement

    • 3-5 headline options

    • A polished outline

    • And a publish ready post entirely in your voice.

    From there, you can ask Claude to expand it using different angles: tips, steps, mistakes, examples, reasons.

    Each angle gives you a different piece of content from the same story.

    Here’s why AI co-writing works so well:

    • Your voice is already in your answers. If you can talk, you have a voice. When you answer questions naturally, your voice is there—AI just organizes it. Compare that to asking AI to mimic someone else’s style, which still comes out sounding like AI with fake profundity layered on top. Starting from your own voice means fewer AI quirks to clean up.

    • Your experience is already differentiated. AI pulls from content that already exists online—averaged into formulas. But your specific experience? No one else has said what you have to say exactly the way you’d say it. Questions unlock that insight, and AI organizes it into something clear.

    • You stay in control at every step. When you walk through content creation with AI, you control the direction in real-time. If you don’t like where it’s going, you redirect it. Steering in real-time beats editing a 1,000-word draft after the fact.

    • It feels like conversation, not writing. Answering questions is like texting a smart friend who’s a great listener. And because it’s conversational, you can be messy. AI handles the grammar, punctuation, and polish while you focus on getting ideas out.

    • You unlock your thinking. Writing is thinking. When you answer questions, you’re forced to work through your ideas instead of guessing what sounds smart. You spend less time performing and more time discovering what you actually believe—and that’s where real insights come from.

    That’s it.
