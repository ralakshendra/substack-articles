    Brutal truth:

    People don’t pay for writing.

    They pay for what the writing does.

    • People opening your emails.

    • More booked calls.

    • Sales on auto-pilot.

    Which means if you want to land high-paying $3K-$5K clients, you need to position yourself as a problem solver and not a writer.

    Here’s how you can turn what you know into a clear, outcome-focused offer your potential client’s instantly understand and want:

    Step 1: Choose one high-value deliverable

    By now you should have a super-specific list of people who you could write for.

    But what services do they need?

    Well first, you need to figure out what potential problems they might be facing.

    Here’s a prompt to help you do exactly that (just replace TARGET CLIENT TYPE with a specific client you would like to work for from yesterday’s prompt).

    Help me identify the 5 biggest problems that [TARGET CLIENT TYPE] face in their work and life that impact their time, money, or business. 

Keep each problem description to 10 words or less.

    This will give you a list of 5 problems.

    So, what services can you use to solve one of these problems?

    Use this prompt:

    Based on the problem '[SELECTED PROBLEM]' that [TARGET CLIENT TYPE] face, suggest 3 specific writing services I could offer to solve this challenge. 

Focus on high-paying options like thought leadership articles, email courses, social media content, landing pages, pitch decks, video scripts, or other premium content. 

For each service, include:

1. A clear name/description
2. How it specifically addresses their problem
3. Why this client would see it as valuable enough to pay premium rates

    Boom, now you know:

    • Who to target

    • What problem they have

    • And how to solve that problem with ghostwriting

    Let’s say you understand the fitness space.

    Here are a few services that easily go for $2-5K:

    • Email sequences that convert trial gym members into monthly subscribers

    • Social posts that build authority for trainers and drive DMs

    • Landing pages that sell coaching programs to a specific type of buyer

    Those are specific. Tangible. Easy to understand.

    (And they solve real problems.)

    Step 2: Create your high-ticket service package

    Now that you know which writing service to focus on, it's time to package it.

    A "package" is simply what your client gets for their money. It’s the full offer—what you’ll deliver, how you’ll deliver it, and what result it leads to. It's like when you buy a "meal deal" at a restaurant. You’re giving them everything they need to get a specific outcome.

    Here’s the formula to follow:

    Specific problem → Specific deliverable → Clear result

    For example:

    • Problem: SaaS trial users don’t understand the product and churn

    • Deliverable: A 5-day onboarding email sequence

    • Result: Activates new users, reduces drop-off, and boosts paid conversions

    That outcome is worth thousands to the right client.

    A few ways to tighten up your package:

    • Give it a real name. “The Conversion Welcome Series” works better than “Email project.”

    • Define what’s included. Be clear about the number of emails, pages, assets, or revisions. Add things like research, kickoff calls, or subject line A/B suggestions.

    • Explain the impact. You’re not locking in numbers, but you are pointing to what this is meant to improve—so clients can see how it connects to their priorities

    Clients need to know what they’re buying—and what it helps them achieve.

    Step 3: Set a price that reflects the value

    Writers often guess at pricing by looking sideways at what others charge.

    A better way is to anchor your price to the upside your work creates.

    Let’s say your email sequence helps a client convert five more customers per month, at $1,000 each. That’s $5,000 in extra monthly revenue.

    Charging $2,000 is reasonable. And sustainable.

    Here’s a simple rule:

    • Aim to price at 10% of the value you create

    • Start at the higher end of your range (most writers aim too low)

    • Offer 2–3 tiered options to give clients flexibility

    This approach gives clients context—and gives you room to grow.

    Here’s the prompt:

    You are a Writing Service Strategist, and your primary function and skill is to create premium writing packages that are clearly scoped, outcome-aware, and designed for high-paying clients.

I am going to give you my niche expertise and ideal client profile, and I would like you to create a clear writing offer that solves a real business problem and is easy for clients to say yes to.

However, there are very specific styles and formats that work best for creating premium writing packages. So I would like you to create your output with the following training data in mind:

~

There are 5 fundamental variables that make for a great writing service package:

- Pain Point: The urgent business problem your ideal client already wants solved

- Deliverable: A specific writing project that directly addresses the pain point

- Scope & Process: What’s included in the offer and how it gets delivered

- Result Framing: What this project is built to improve (without overpromising)

- Price Strategy: A 3-tier pricing structure that reflects value and gives clients flexibility

I am going to walk through each piece and give you clear instructions on what makes each variable work well, along with examples to follow.

~

Variable #1: Pain Point
Why it matters:
Start with a business problem your client already knows they have—and actively wants to solve.

Framework:
[Who] is struggling with [specific problem] that’s costing them [money/time/growth].

Examples:

SaaS founders losing trial users during onboarding

Course creators struggling to convert free subscribers

Coaches getting ghosted after discovery calls

~

Variable #2: Deliverable
Why it matters:
Clients need to know exactly what they’re buying—and how it solves their problem.

Framework:
Choose a single, outcome-driven format (emails, landing pages, case studies, etc.)

Examples:

A 5-part onboarding email sequence

A high-converting landing page

A month of lead-gen content for LinkedIn

~

Variable #3: Scope & Process
Why it matters:
The more clearly you define what’s included, the more confidently your client can say yes.

Framework:
List each step of the process: kickoff call, research, draft, revision, delivery.

Examples:

60-minute kickoff call

Voice-of-customer research

Writing + 1–2 rounds of edits

Final delivery in Google Docs or Notion

~

Variable #4: Result Framing
Why it matters:
Clients buy the outcome they believe your work will help them achieve—not the task.

Framework:
“This gives your client everything they need to [reach a specific goal or solve a real pain].”

Examples:

Everything you need to turn free trials into paid users

Everything you need to follow up with leads and book more calls

Everything you need to help new clients follow through on Day 1

Note: You’re not promising results. You’re showing what the work is built to support.

~

Variable #5: Price Strategy
Why it matters:
Tiered pricing gives clients options and helps you anchor your value across different budgets.

Framework: Use a 3-Tier Structure
Offer three levels of support or depth—each with clear deliverables and outcomes.

Base Tier – “Essentials”
The core writing deliverable, tightly scoped and efficient.

Example:

3 emails

30-minute call

1 round of edits
Price: $1,000–$1,500

Middle Tier – “Growth”
Adds strategy, research, or more comprehensive assets.

Example:

5 emails

60-minute call

Research + messaging brief

2 rounds of edits
Price: $2,000–$2,500

Top Tier – “All-In”
Full-service option with support, testing, and polish.

Example:

5 emails + 2 bonus re-engagement emails

A/B test subject lines

Swipe copy for social or SMS

Slack access for feedback
Price: $3,000–$4,000

Guidelines:

Make sure each tier feels complete on its own

Highlight the extra value in your top tier

Use price gaps that feel logical, not random

~

I am now going to give you my niche and ideal client so you can create a full writing package based on these 5 variables.

Please provide the output in the following format:

Package Name: [Name of the offer]
Target Client: [Who this is for]
Pain Point: [Specific business problem]
Deliverable: [Exactly what they’ll get]

Price Tiers:
• Essentials: [$X] – [Summary of what’s included]
• Growth: [$Y] – [Summary of what’s included]
• All-In: [$Z] – [Summary of what’s included]

Are you ready for my niche and client details?
