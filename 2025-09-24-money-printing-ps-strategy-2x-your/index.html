    Most people know that the PS (postscript) is one of the most read sections of every email you send.

    ​But, we've never challenged this assumption.

    So last year on Prime Day, Daniel (our CMO) decided to put this belief to the test.

    Can a PS drive more sales or not?

    The results were mind blowing.

    Here's what Daniel did:

    • He wrote a PS at the end of every email in a 6-part sequence.

    • Each PS had a CTA (because, again, he wanted to see if they could actually generate results).

    • He set up some lightweight tracking so he could see how many clicks the main CTA of the email got vs how many clicks the PS CTA got.

    The result?

    Double the conversions.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3Jr1!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5a91fc8e-6315-41f6-8eb0-c6a36ff70d39_1200x900.jpeg)

    Notice how the PSs drive almost as many clicks as the main CTA of the email.

    Crazy, right?

    Here’s the lesson:

    Do NOT half-ass your PSs.

    Invest some time in making them really good. Because if do, you might make some extra cash the next time you do a launch.

    For context, we've made over $15,000,000 selling digital products. And if we had ignored using PSs, a large chunk (potentially 50%) of that revenue would never have happened.

    Now, the beauty of this strategy is that it is extremely simple and you can use AI to help you write the 5 most common types.

    But first, let’s look at how a PS works.

    Here’s how a PS works:

    At the end of any email, all you have to do is say:

    • “By the way, if you liked this email, you might also like XYZ.”

    • “By the way, we have this other thing that can help you do XYZ.”

    • “By the way, only 2 more days until we do XYZ. Would you like to be part of it?”

    The people who take advantage of it—take advantage of it. And everyone else just sort of glazes over it. No harm, no foul.

    It’s extremely effective at giving your readers a second chance to engage with your offer, more content, or any objections they might have. Chances are, if you are reading this right now, you probably at some point have read or clicked on a PS inside one of our email sequences.

    Now, let’s do this with AI.

    How to write a PS using AI

    This prompt will take any email and suggest 5 different PSs.

    Copy/paste the prompt below into your favorite LLM along with your email and a description of what you want to promote to your audience.

    Here’s the prompt:

    You are an expert email copywriter.

I will provide you with:

- A draft email (main body copy)
- The offer being promoted
- The target audience

Your task is to brainstorm 5 different PS (postscript) sections to append to the email.

Each PS should follow one of these formats:

1. Question / Answer: Surface a common doubt or hesitation, then address it clearly.

2. Direct Objection: Anticipate a buyer objection and resolve it persuasively.

3. Testimonial: Reinforce credibility with a customer or peer success quote.

4. Sneak Peek: Offer a preview of a resource, feature, or bonus inside the offer.

5. Waitlist CTA: Create urgency by pointing to a waitlist, deadline, or limited access.

Rules:
- Each PS must be congruent with / a natural continuation of the email.
- Keep each PS short (3–6 sentences max).
- Use the draft email’s tone and flow.
- Each PS must tie directly to the offer and audience.
- Include specific details, numbers, or outcomes (not vague promises).
- End each PS with a clear next step (link or call-to-action).

Output format:
Label each section clearly (PS #1, PS #2, etc.), then provide the copy.

    What to do next:

    • Run the prompt with a draft email

    • Review the 5 outputs (pick a PS that fits)

    • Edit, drop the PS into your email, and hit send

    Watch your click-through rate double!
