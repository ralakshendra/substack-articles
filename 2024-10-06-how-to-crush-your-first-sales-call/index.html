3 Steps to Prepare For (And Overcome) Any Sales Objection

Most people hate getting rejected.

They either freeze at the first sign of resistance, scramble for a quick comeback, or fail to understand why their prospect isn’t interested. But here’s the thing: objections don’t have to catch you off guard if you plan for them ahead of time.

Every "no" is actually a signal.

It’s a chance to understand your customer better, refine your offer, and ultimately close more deals. When you walk into a call already prepared for the most common objections, you’re always one step ahead.

And with this simple 3-step pre-call strategy, you’ll out-prepare your competition, anticipate objections, and effortlessly turn your prospects’ “no” into a “yes.”

Step 1: Create Your Irresistible Offer

Before any prospective customer can raise an objection to your product or service, you have to make an offer.

The problem is most people think an offer is about the solution. But nobody cares about the solution until they understand the problem. It doesn’t matter what product or service you’re trying to provide. You have to frame the problem & solutions properly, or it’s going to be hard to get a “Yes.”

Here’s how to create an irresistible offer:

  • Step 1: Identify a specific problem your customer is facing.

  • Step 2: Explain why that problem exists.

  • Step 3: Highlight the pain caused by the problem.

  • Step 4: Describe the ultimate negative outcome if they don’t act.

  • Step 5: THEN offer your solution that leads them to their desired outcome.

Step 2: Conduct An Objection Audit

List every single reason your prospect might say no to your offer.

Why? Because as soon as they hear your offer, they’ll come up with every single reason to say no (before they say yes). It’s human nature—we’re all skeptical. Which is why you want to think ahead and preemptively address as many objections as you can.

For example:

  • Price: "I can't afford this right now.”

  • ROI: "Is this worth the investment?”

  • Need: "This doesn’t apply to my situation.”

  • Credibility: "Has this worked for others like me?”

  • Risk: "What if it doesn't solve my problem?”

  • Complexity: "It seems too complicated for me.”

  • Time: "How long will it take to see results?”

  • Support: "Will I get help if I need it?”

If you can answer these questions, you’ll be able to command higher prices, land more high-quality customers, and build a lucrative business.

Because your skills aren’t what’s holding you back from growing your business. Not knowing how to communicate with your customers is what’s holding you back.

Step 3: Overcome Objections In Your “Sales Copy”

Any place your customer is interacting with your offer, you need to address and overcome objections.

  • On a sales call

  • On your landing page

  • In your marketing emails

Each touchpoint is an opportunity for you to help your potential customer to see the value of your solution relative to the value, cost, or urgency of the problem.

The best way to do this is by reframing every objection into an “Overcoming Script.” An Overcoming Script is simply an objection reframed as a benefit for the customer. For example, let’s say you offer to write 800-word thought leadership articles for startup founders who want to raise capital.

For example, here’s a common objection you might come across:

"Investors don’t care about articles; they care about numbers.”

You can overcome this objection by turning it into a benefit:

"Numbers are important, but they’re only part of the story. A strong online presence builds the trust and credibility that gets investors to look at the numbers in the first place. For example, the founder of X company secured their largest round of seed money after an investor read the founder’s article on Inc. The two had never met. And this happens all the time. I’ve got 100 more stories just like this one.”

The secret to overcoming these objections is being prepared.

And that takes research, time, and exposure.

The good news is you can shortcut the process, so you’re ready to hit the ground running by using AI to elicit the most common objections for your specific niche offer.

Here’s how:

The Objection Killer Checklist Prompt

This prompt will take any offer or sales copy and turn it into a list of possible objections.

You can use this list to kill objections in your marketing emails, or add them as FAQs on your landing page, or as checklist to prepare for a sales call.

Here’s the prompt:

You are an expert sales consultant and copywriter specializing in identifying and addressing common objections in sales processes. Your task is to analyze a given offer or sales page and generate a comprehensive "Objection Killer Checklist" tailored to that specific offer.

Input: The user will provide either a description of an offer or the content of a sales page.

Output: Generate an "Objection Killer Checklist" in a table format with the following columns:
1. Objection
2. Why it arises
3. Eliciting Question
4. Preemptive strategy
5. Overcoming script
6. Follow-up tactic

Format your response as follows:

The Objection Killer Checklist for [Product/Service Name]

| Objection | Why it arises | Eliciting Question | Preemptive strategy | Overcoming script | Follow-up tactic |
|-----------|---------------|---------------------|---------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| [Objection 1] | [Brief explanation] | [Question 1] | [Strategy] | [Script] | [Follow-up] |
| [Objection 2] | [Brief explanation] | [Question 2] | [Strategy] | [Script] | [Follow-up] |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Guidelines:
- Analyze the offer or sales page thoroughly to identify 5-7 objections specific to the product or service.
- For each objection, provide a brief explanation of why it arises.
- Create a subtle, non-confrontational, caring, and empathetic question that could elicit the objection naturally in conversation.
- Use a conversational tone similar to the EXAMPLE provided.
- Provide practical, actionable advice for each objection.
- Ensure strategies and scripts are ethical and focus on educating the prospect rather than pressuring them.
- Include a mix of objections related to interest, budget, timing, and perceived value.
- Tailor your language and examples to the industry or niche of the offer.

After presenting the table, ask the user: "Would you like me to convert this table into a CSV format for easier importing into spreadsheet software?"

Now, please provide the offer description or sales page content, and I will generate the Objection Killer Checklist based on this prompt.

How to use this prompt:

  1. Copy the prompt into ChatGPT/Claude.

  2. Run the prompt and respond with your offer script or sales copy.

  3. The AI will respond with a list of objections.

The “Sales Call Arc” Prompt

If you’re new to sales, engaging in a sales conversation can feel intimidating.

  • What’s the first thing you say?

  • How do you transition into your offer?

  • What do you do when they “reject” your offer?

It’s easy to get wrapped around the axle before you even pick up the phone.

So, what do you do?

First, the easiest way to overcome the fear of selling is to not think of sales as selling.

Instead think of it as education. Once you stop thinking of sales as “I need to sell my product or service” and adopt the mindset that you're “helping your prospect solve their problems,” sales become much easier. It's no different from working with a friend who wants to start a newsletter and teaching them how to use Substack or Beehiiv. You're simply being helpful.

Second, prepare for your call.

That means reviewing your list of objections and having a plan for your sales conversation. The good news is all sales conversations follow a very simple arc:

  1. Introduction & rapport building

  2. Transition to your prospects problems

  3. Educating the prospect on your irresistible offer

  4. Closing

  5. Addressing objections

Every good sales call breaks down into those 5 areas.

Now, there’s loads of nuance in each part. But when you’re first getting started, the most important thing is to have a plan and educate your prospect on your offer during the call. That’s it.

So, to help you prepare we built a prompt that creates a sales script for you.

The Sales Call Arc Prompt:

You are an expert sales consultant tasked with creating a detailed sales script based on a specific call arc. Your goal is to generate a script that follows the provided structure and allows for effective communication and note-taking during sales calls.

Input: The user has provided the following desired "Sales" Call Arc:

1. "Where are you tuning in from?"
2. "Cool so thanks for taking the time. just so I know, why don't you share with me a bit about your biz. I know we were bouncing DMs/emails, but what's the biggest problem you've been running into lately?"
3. No matter what they say. Give the OFFER SCRIPT.
   a. First, the client needs to be educated on which problem they need to solve, why that problem specifically, what the consequences of NOT solving that problem are, and what ultimate negative outcome they will have to live with if they don't. 
   b. Second, once the client understands the problem (and the consequences of NOT solving it), the client then needs to be educated on the new & different way you are suggesting to solve that problem, why that solution specifically, the benefits of using that solution, and the ultimate positive outcome that solution unlocks for them.
4. Rest of the call = logistics & objection handling.

Output: Generate a detailed sales script in a 2-column table format. The left column should contain the script, and the right column should be left blank for note-taking. Format your response as follows:

Sales Script for [Product/Service Name]

| Script | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| [Opening line] | |
| [Question or statement] | |
| ... | |

Guidelines:
- Follow the provided call arc structure closely.
- Include specific questions and statements for each step of the arc.
- For the offer script section, ensure you include points that:
  1. Educate the client on the problem, its consequences, and the ultimate negative outcome of not solving it.
  2. Educate the client on your solution, its benefits, and the ultimate positive outcome it provides.
- Include prompts for objection handling and logistics discussion.
- Use a conversational, professional tone throughout the script.
- Ensure the script flows naturally from one section to the next.

After presenting the table, ask the user: "Would you like me to convert this table into a CSV format for easier importing into spreadsheet software like Excel or Notion?"

Here’s how it works:

  1. Run the prompt using ChatGPT/Claude

  2. The AI will ask you for your offer.

  3. Respond with your offer.

  4. The AI will generate a sales script for your to follow.

Print the output or pull it up on your computer during your sales call to keep your call on track.
