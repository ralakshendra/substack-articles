    Effortless Expansion: How To Instantly Turn Short-Form Content Into Long-Form Content With ChatGPT

    Accomplish in 5 minutes what used to take 2 hours.

    Author: Dickie Bush
    Date: 2023-05-03

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Qw3o!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F866f399c-909a-4cef-a33a-f02d3b077d78_1260x900.png)

    Ahoy, Digital Writers!

    Today I’m going to walk you through a framework for using ChatGPT for “effortless expansion.”

    Anyone familiar with Ship 30 for 30 is familiar with our Lean Writing framework.

    From a high level, we emphasize the importance of validating long-form content with short-form content.

    Just like a startup should validate its product before hiring a massive team and deploying millions of dollars of resources, writers should test their ideas and make sure the market is interested in them before investing time & effort expanding them.

    Now, here’s a quick “a-ha” for you.

    The only difference between short-form and long-form content is “more.”

    • If you wrote a tweet listing 3 books, your long-form will list 10 books

    • If you wrote an Atomic Essay with 3 mistakes, your long-form will be 10 mistakes

    • If you gave a breakdown with 3 stats, your long-form will be 10 stats

    Simple enough, right?

    Now, before ChatGPT, it was on you to rack your brain for additional “expansion” ideas whenever you saw something that worked.

    Luckily, this type of task is perfect for our Digital Writing Intern.

    And so the following prompt is going to show you how to instantly expand your short-form lists into long-form lists (so you can churn out more content in less time, without banging your head against a wall trying to come up with more ideas).

    Let’s dive in.

    Alrighty, so before I show you the prompt for this one, we’re going to walk through 2 separate examples to show you the “thinking” behind the prompt and how I came up with it.

    That way, you can ingrain this “see something that works, try to expand it” Lean Writing Habit into your everyday writing.

    From a high level, this is how the Lean Writing process works:

    1. When you come up with an idea, tweet it out or save it somewhere

      • This could be as simple as hearing a nugget in a podcast or someone asking you a question

    2. After coming up with the initial idea, try to expand it into a list of 3 for short-form content

      • We’ve found “3” to be the perfect number of things to list inside tweets or Atomic Essays

    3. Once you’ve validated people are interested in the topic of the list, start trying to expand it

      • For long-form posts like Threads, Newsletter, or YouTube videos, we’ve found lists of 7-10 work best.

    Now, here are 2 examples of how I used this exact process over the last week.

    Example 1: Expanding the idea that “it has never been easier to win”

    I was listening to a podcast episode with Alex Hormozi and Chris Williamson, and Chris mentioned a fascinating statistic.

    “99% of podcasts have fewer than 21 episodes. So if you can create a weekly show for 5 months, you are in the top 1%.”

    And Alex responded:

    “That is proof that it has literally never been easier to win. All you need is a little bit of consistency.”

    Right away, I put on my “Lean Writing” thinking hat.

    Alex and Chris just wrote the hook for me: “X stats that show it has literally never been easier to win.”

    And Chris gave me the first item on the list!

    So I scribbled that note into my phone and set it aside for a day or so.

    When I sat down to write later in the week, I pulled up my note of ideas and started thinking about how to expand this.

    I looked at the single idea I had, and my brain started making connections.

    If most podcasts fail, that means most blogs, YouTube channels, and businesses fail after a certain time too. What are all the other stats that I could find that showed people are relatively inconsistent?

    Enter, my Digital Writing Intern, ChatGPT.

    Rather than try to research the internet and come up with these stats myself, I wrote a simple “expansion” prompt (that I will give you as a template at the end of this post).

    I am writing a post with 10 reminders showing people that it has literally never been easier to win (as long as you have a bit of patience and consistency).

I want to gather stats illustrating that most people are neither of those things. 

Here are a few I started with: 

- 99% of podcasts have fewer than 21 episodes

I think we could find more stats for:
- blogs
- youtube channels
- businesses
- diets
- meditation habits
- other general wellness habits

To start, come up with 10 statistics that to add to my list. I will want the sources later, so make sure they are verified.

    And here’s what it came back with:

    Boom, right away I had several more ideas. I started asking for specific numbers about how long it takes people to give up for each of these (so the stats were relatively similar to only 1% of podcasts making it past 21 episodes).

    And that is scheduled to go live in about 2 weeks, which is perfect.

    Because here’s what is likely going to happen:

    1. In two weeks, that tweet will go live. And people will respond to it.

    2. I have a hunch that it will receive a lot of positive comments because it is motivational & inspiring

    3. That will be the validation I need to turn it into a long-form post, like a thread

    And this is Lean Writing in action, aided by the help of ChatGPT.

    I have this thread already outlined, listing 10 (expanding on the 5 above) examples that show it has literally never been easier to win.

    And it took me a fraction of the time it would have taken me to try and expand it on my own.

    Example 2: Expanding the idea of the difference between A-players and C-players

    Here’s another example of Lean Writing in action (aided by ChatGPT).

    I was in Cabo for a mastermind, and one of the presentations was on hiring & retaining talent.

    Off the cuff, the presenter made a comment that stuck with me:

    “And that’s the difference between A-players and C-players. A-players default to end-of-day, C-players default to end of week.”

    BOOM, another hook written for me with the first item on the list outlined.

    “X differences between A-players and C-players (after years of working with each).”

    So once again, I scribbled the idea down and stored it in my notes so I would stumble upon it the next time I sat down to write.

    And by doing so, I let the idea marinate in the background of my brain, slowly gathering a list of other differences between A-players and C-players.

    Later that week when I sat down to write, I pulled up that note and filled in 2 more ideas to turn it into a list of 3.

    • C-players are reactive, A-players are proactive

    • C-players blame circumstances, A-players take extreme ownership

    • C-players default to end-of-wee, A-players default to end of day

    And, you guessed it, I turned that into a short-form piece of content.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!X4n7!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fed2a2807-e058-4361-8183-3ee508e93139_1120x1288.png)

    And just like the example above, that is set to go live in 2 weeks.

    Between now and then, I’ll start to expand that list of 3 into 10, using the following ChatGPT prompt.

    I am writing a post with 10 differences betewen A-player employees and C-player employees. I only build my team with A-players as they are what help build world-class organizations.

I want to gather a list of differences.

Here are a few I started with:

- C-players are reactive, A-players are proactive
- C-players blame circumstances, A-players take extreme ownership
- C-players default to end-of-week, A-players default to end of day

I think we could find more differences with:
- their mindset
- how they take feedback
- how they present themselves
- how they bring energy

To start, come up with 10 more differences to add to my list.

    And here is what it came up with:

    Now, I find these to be a bit generic. And more than likely, ChatGPT will generate some generic ones like this, so you don’t want to copy and paste.

    Instead, you want to use the idea ChatGPT generates as a springboard.

    For example:

    • I like number 4, but I would rename it to “C-players are me-first, A-players are team-first.”

    • I like number 6, but I would rename it to “C-players get Cs, A-players get As”

    • I like number 8, but I would rename it to “C-players are energy takers, A-players are energy-givers.”

    You get the point.

    But just like that, I’m able to take a small nugget I heard during a presentation and turn it into:

    1. A single tweet

    2. An Atomic Essay (expanded by me)

    3. A Thread (expanded by ChatGPT)

    All of that in under an hour.

    That is the power of writing with AI.

    Turning the Lean Writing process into a ChatGPT prompt

    We’re going to keep today’s prompt short & surgical: focused only on helping you expand a list of 1-3 items into a list of 10.

    If you want help generating topics, subtopics, and potential lists to write about, make sure you check out our 1 Topic, 100 Ideas post for a more in-depth idea generation prompt.

    Here’s the ChatGPT “template” you can use to expand anything:

    I am writing a post with [X] [topic of the list]

[1 sentence about why I am writing this]

I want to gather a list of [topic of the list]

Here are a few I started with:

- [List item 1]
- [List item 2]
- [List item 3]

I think we could find more differences with:
- [Potential list item]
- [Potenital list item]
- [Potential list item]

To start, come up with 10 more to add to my list.

    Now, here are 4 steps to give this prompt a try:

    1. Listen carefully for when you come across a small nugget of an idea or someone asks you a question

    2. Put on your Lean Writing hat to recognize you were just given the “hook” of a post with the first item outlined

    3. Validate that idea with short-form content (tweet → Atomic Essay)

    4. Use ChatGPT to expand that idea into long-form content with the prompt above

    And the best part about this process?

    You can accomplish all of this, literally in the next 10 minutes!
