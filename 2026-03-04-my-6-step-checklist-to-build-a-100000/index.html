Hey there!

I’ve built two paid newsletters on Substack to multiple six figures in annual recurring revenue—Category Pirates to $200K+ per year and Write with AI to $400K+ per year.

After doing this not once but twice, I finally feel like I have the framework for launching new paid newsletters and scaling them to six figures over and over again.

Here are the six steps.

Step 1: Pick a topic attached to a traffic engine

This is one of those ideas that’s so simple but people over-complicate it.

You launch a paid newsletter. How do you get people to find out about it? You have to write about the same topics for free somewhere else.

If I could summarize a paid newsletter business, that’s pretty much it. You write online for free about a topic, you tell people “I also write about this on my paid newsletter over here,” and then you do that every day, multiple times per day, for years.

People want to believe there’s something more. There isn’t. The boring work is the moat. The secret is consistency.

Step 2: Make your newsletter tangible

This is probably the hardest one for new writers to wrap their heads around.

As a general rule, readers value tangible ideas more than intangible ones.

Just notice how differently these two sentences feel:

  • “I’ll explain how to build a paid newsletter”

  • “Here’s my six-step checklist to build a $100,000+ paid newsletter on Substack.”

Same topic.

But the first is intangible—you can’t hold me explaining in your hands. The second is the digital equivalent of handing you a physical object.

For this newsletter, we give you ChatGPT and Claude prompts.

What is a prompt?

A tangible asset. I’m not explaining things to you—I’m giving you a digital physical object. When someone pays to subscribe, they’re aware of the trade in their mind: I give you $20 per month and you give me prompts.

That decision is probably 90% of the reason Write With AI has grown so quickly.

[

](https://writewithai.substack.com/p/the-write-with-ai-prompt-library)

Step 3: The one free, one paid strategy

Substack is becoming a platform in and of itself—not just a publishing tool, but an ecosystem of readers and writers.

Almost 20% of our paid subscribers have come from inside the Substack network.

So here’s how you get those readers: write one free newsletter per week and one paid newsletter per week. Consistently. Forever.

Every time you hit publish, you’re tapping into Substack’s explore algorithm. Your free content is the appetizer. Your paid content is the main course.

Step 4: Send paid snippets to your entire free list

On Substack, your free readers and paid readers are automatically segmented.

There’s a feature called the paywall preview—you choose where to insert the paywall, which means you can make the first 20-50% of a paid post free to anyone.

So you send the paid post to everybody. Paying subscribers get the whole thing. Free subscribers only get the preview. It’s a great way to soft sell and remind your free list: hey, if you want to keep reading, upgrade to paid.

Step 5: Put the paywall at a cliffhanger

Most writers don’t do this, and it is causing them to leave so much money on the table.

Don’t throw the paywall just anywhere in your paid newsletter. Put it right before you give the reader the tangible thing you’re promising them. Give the free education away in the preview — and then right as you say “here’s your Kindle Unlimited heat map for the week,” that’s where you drop the paywall.

Now the free subscriber has to make a choice: abandon the newsletter right as it’s starting to get good, or upgrade to paid.

Now, below is a prompt you can use to optimize your newsletter content for maximum free-to-paid conversion. (I just used it to help me insert the paywall you see above.)

Here’s a simple prompt you can use:

I'm writing a newsletter about [TOPIC]. 

I want to create a free teaser version that converts to paid subscriptions. 

Here's my full article:

[PASTE FULL ARTICLE HERE]

Please help me:

1. Identify the most compelling 25-30% of content to use as a teaser
2. Suggest the optimal paywall placement for maximum tension
3. Rewrite the section before the paywall to build anticipation
4. Create 3 versions of teaser endings that encourage subscriptions
5. Format this as a complete newsletter with:
   - An engaging opening hook
   - The teaser content
   - A compelling paywall message
   - A strategic CTA

Please maintain my writing style while optimizing for conversions.

How to use this prompt:

  1. Replace [TOPIC] with your newsletter’s focus

  2. Paste your draft newsletter content

  3. Use the AI’s suggestions to restructure your content

  4. Test different paywall placements with your audience

Pro tip: Run this prompt 2-3 times to get different versions, then combine the best elements from each response.

Step 6: Republish your short-form content on Substack Notes

Substack Notes are basically Twitter/X but on Substack.

In addition to the one free, one paid strategy, just republish all your short-form content there. It’s a very easy double dip.

Once per day, we take one of my tweets from X and repost it on Substack. Don’t over-complicate this one. Substack has a growing ecosystem and now it has an algorithm. Republish your content and introduce more people to your writing.

That’s it.
