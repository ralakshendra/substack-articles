    Hey there!

    There has never been a better time to leverage visual content on LinkedIn.

    The platform's algorithm is heavily rewarding infographics and carousel posts. Smart creators are capitalizing on this to build massive engagement.

    If you're looking at people like Chris Donnelly, Eric Partaker or Matt Gray and thinking, "They must have a secret design team creating all those viral infographics..."

    Then I'm here to tell you it's not that complicated.

    You don't need:

    • A design degree

    • Expensive software

    • A team of freelancers

    All you need is this simple 10-step system for turning any article into a professional infographic using Figma Make. And you can master it in the time it takes to drink your morning coffee.

    Before we break it down, here’s an example to show you what’s possible when you follow this process:

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!LwKi!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7ac8ad3f-4f47-481d-99c2-a39abc5a0cc9_1120x1353.png)

    Pretty cool, right?

    This is 1,000X better than what you can do with ChatGPT or Claude (for now).

    Here’s how I did it:

    Step 1: Setup A Free Figma Account

    In case you’re not familiar, Figma is a design tool originally built for web and app interface.

    But you can also use it to make social media graphics, presentations, and even illustrations. It’s like Canva—only more powerful and built for professional designers.

    You can get started with Figma's free plan, but you'll burn through your free credits after about 5 AI prompts

    So if you want to really test the workflow without limits, I suggest grabbing the Pro plan for $20/month.

    Step 1: Choose A Proven Long-Form Post To Visualize

    Not every article makes a good infographic.

    Look for articles with these characteristics:

    • Numbered lists or steps (like "7 ways to..." or "5 mistakes...")

    • Clear, actionable advice that can be summarized in bullet points

    • Visual concepts that benefit from diagrams or illustrations

    • Reference material people would want to save and share

    I chose "3 Simple Ways To Structure Ghostwriting Deals" because it had numbered points and I think it would make a nice quick reference guide for our PGA audience.

    Perfect infographic material.

    Step 2: Launch Figma Make

    Navigate to the Make feature within your Figma workspace.

    Start a new chat.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!k2Dc!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F1010c8b7-a39c-47f6-8f3e-2761d78f5cae_2670x1044.png)

    Figma Make is a feature within Figma that allows you to create an interactive prototype using a text prompt or by building on your existing Figma design.

    Step 3: Write Your Figma Make Infographic Prompt

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!EIKn!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9ae6d99d-ae69-4eb3-81ea-02ae68451366_1518x1110.png)

    You can chat with Figma Make just like you do with ChatGPT.

    Here’s the simple prompt I used:

    I want to turn my article into an information-dense infographic.  

For context, I’ve attached an example infographic.  

[attach image]

My brand colors are: 

[list hex codes or any color preferences].  

Here’s the article: 

[paste text].

    Drop in your article text, your brand colors, and give Figma an example to follow. (I used a screenshot from Chris Donnelly’s LinkedIn feed.)

    Run your prompt.

    Step 4: Watch Figma Build A First-Draft Of Your Infographic

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!TBlv!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff3488ec0-e9f2-4b86-bc1e-bb90c381f31f_3080x1616.png)

    Step 5: Use Inline Prompts And The Code Editor To Refine Your Design

    Take notes on what you like and what needs changing.

    Make targeted improvements using simple prompts:

    • "Change the background color to navy"

    • "Increase the size of the main header to 38"

    • "Add more spacing between sections 2 and 3"

    • "Make the numbered points bold"

    If you know CSS, you can dive into the code editor for precise control.

    But even if you’re beginner you can make changes using natural language prompts by clicking on any element in the design.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9cVE!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb30c00eb-e84c-403c-89fb-22929260ca7c_2372x640.png)

    Step 6: Publish Your Infographic To The Web

    Once you're happy with your edits, publish it using Figma's publish feature.

    Unfortunately you can’t export your design to a pdf from Figma Make which is why you need to follow this step.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!pLXr!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa71377a4-44f9-4285-b2c3-78ad2eefb0ac_1546x1130.png)

    After you publish your file, Figma will generate a public URL.

    Copy it.

    Step 8: Import Your Design Into A New Figma Project Using The “html.to.design” Plugin

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!nsVz!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6c32d49a-c5c3-493f-a565-f11f2f161fbf_2812x1550.png)

    Here’s how:

    1. Create a new Figma project

    2. Launch html.to.design

    3. Paste your URL from Step 7

    4. Run the import

    Now you have a fully editable design file that you can resize, recolor, and customize using Figma’s design tools.

    Step 9: Make Final Edits And Export Your Infographic

    Adjust spacing for visual balance, make any text edits, and add your logo or other custom images.

    Export as a PDF.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HVhQ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc5a00fa1-f39c-430e-bd1b-61577e3d3d69_3314x1910.png)

    Step 10: Share On LinkedIn!

    That’s the whole process.

    Give these steps a try. I’d love to see what you create.
