The Newsletter Name Generator

3 Step Formula To Attract Your ICP

Author: Nicolas Cole
Date: 2024-10-23

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!KbBk!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff2054ccd-0a04-44eb-925b-83f3abb87a21_1260x900.png)

For the vast majority of Digital Writers, starting a newsletter feels like “a big deal.”

And the biggest reason why is: the name.

This is where even the smartest Digital Writers fall back into the bad habit of trying to be clever, not clear.

But don’t overthink it!

For example: if you are writing 15-minute yoga tips for busy B2B professionals, literally title your newsletter: “15-Minute Yoga Tips For B2B Professionals.” This is 10x better than naming it something clever like: “The Stretchy” or “Flexible Forever.”

If your newsletter promises to help people “build a healthy, wealthy life” then nobody is going to read.

Why?

Because those outcomes are incredibly intangible and subjective. Ask 10 different people how they define “healthy” or “wealthy” and you will get 10 different answers.

That’s a problem.

The way you get people to read for your newsletter is by promising Tangible, Objective outcomes.

What are tangible objects?

  • Reports

  • Prompts

  • Templates

  • Case studies

  • Cheat codes

  • Etc.

You can literally “hold” these kinds of things in your hands, making them tangible.

What does objective mean?

Clear, specific, and measurable. There’s no room for interpretation—it’s either achieved or it’s not.

For example:

“Become happier in your relationship” = intangible and subjective.

But “Scripts of exactly what to say when you start dating again as a divorced man” = Tangible (”scripts” are tangible, you can write them down in your phone) and Objective (you’re either a divorced man or you’re not.)

When naming a newsletter, you want something that sticks in your audience's mind, is easy to remember, and clearly conveys the value or biggest benefit of reading in tangible, objective terms.

The FOR WHO / SO THAT Framework To Write A Clear, Tangible, & Objective Newsletter Name

Here’s a simple 3-step framework to help you name your newsletter:

For Who / So That.

Step 1: Who Is Your Newsletter For?

The first thing you can do to write a clear, tangible, and objective newsletter name is call out your target reader—WHO is it for?

  • Newbie Investors?

  • 3rd Base Peewee Coaches?

  • Fashionistas?

By calling out your audience directly, you’re being objective. Either a reader fits the description, or they don’t.

For example, if your newsletter is "Kitchen Gadgets for Busy Sous Chefs," you’re going to attract Sous Chefs who need to save time, not weekend BBQ enthusiasts or home cooks.

Now, look at these examples:

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!3PSR!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3f020110-4158-4ed9-a6b5-4a4f5109b479_592x702.png)

These newsletters might be amazing, but I have no clue if they’re for me—so I skip right over them.

Don’t make this naming mistake.

Unless you have tons of brand equity tied up in your name like Michael Jordan or Taylor Swift, avoid using your name or your company’s name in your newsletter title. Focus on your audience instead. Steer clear of names like “Nick’s Newsletter” or “The Wine Stop Newsletter.”

Let’s keep going.

The next part of the framework is SO THAT.

Step 2: Name What Your Readers Gain After Subscribing

After naming your audience you want to tell them what they’ll get for reading.

For example:

  • Growth Stock Deep Dives

  • Base Stealing Signals

  • Wardrobe Teardowns

Every reader wants to know the benefit—busy Sous Chefs will read your newsletter SO THAT they can find time-saving kitchen gadgets.

The more tangible the better.

So, ask yourself WHO, specifically, you want to write for—SO THAT they can do or get…what? Give the reader a reason to subscribe.

Step 3: Combine Your For Who And So That

Put your WHO and SO THAT together and you’ve got your name:

  • 15-Minute Yoga Tips For B2B Professionals

  • Growth Stock Deep Dives For Newbie Investors

  • Wardrobe Teardowns For Fashionistas

Aim for less than 5 words.

This keeps your newsletter name quick to scan and easy to read in a crowded inbox. Spend some time iterating on a few different versions. Often, a tangible name will naturally remove the need to include an audience name. But for your first iteration, it’s a good idea to include both.

If you follow this simple framework, your newsletter name will be clearer than 99% of the competition and as a result attract 10X more of the right kind of subscribers.

How To Name Your Newsletter With AI

This prompt helps you brainstorm 10 different names for your newsletter using the For Who / So That framework.

  • Copy the below script into Claude. (You can also try it with ChatGPT, but from my experience, Claude generated better results.)

  • Fill in the section at the end of prompt with your topic and target audience.

  • Hit run and voila—you’ve got 10 names to play with.

Here’s the prompt:

I want to create a clear, straightforward name for my newsletter.

Your task is to generate 10 newsletter names to attract SPECIFIC NICHE.

The newsletter name must meet the the following criteria:

1. Promise: It must imply the biggest benefit for the [SPECIFIC NICHE]. Make a bold promise.
2. Tangible: Tangible assets like templates, reports, scripts, prompts, case studies, etc. The reader must get "some thing" in return for reading.
3. Objective: Clear, specific, and measurable. It's either true or it's false. You have it or you don't.
4. Aim for 7 words or less.
5. Who it's' for: The audience must be specific (named or implied).
6. Format: Use natural flowing phrases. Avoid colons, dashes, or "edition" subtitles.

Consider the following questions when generating names:
- What tangible assets are most valuable to [SPECIFIC NICHE]?
- What measurable outcomes do people in [SPECIFIC NICHE] strive for?
- What action words resonate with [SPECIFIC NICHE] professionals?
- How can you concisely convey the newsletter's value proposition?
- Who specifically is in this [SPECIFIC NICHE]?

Based on these criteria, suggest 10 potential newsletter names and desrciption.

Provide a brief explanation (1-2 sentences) for each name, highlighting how it meets the criteria and why it would appeal to the target audience.

Output format:
## 1. [Newsletter Name]
### How to {avoid problem} {get benefit}, and {ultimate desirable outcome}
[Explanation]

<SPECIFIC_NICHE>
Topic = {Insert your topic or industry
Target Audience = {Insert your target audience}
</SPECIFIC_NICHE>

Remember: It's imperative the audience is clear and the language is tangible + objective

And here’s the follow up prompt:

I like number {Insert your favorite newsletter name.} 

Can you come up with 5 different variations of this idea?

Vary the name using these templates:

1. The {Tangible Promise}
2. The {Aspirational Audience Persona}
3. {Tangible Promise} for {Type of Audience}
4. {Tangible Activity}
5. {Action Verb} Your {Desired Result}

Please use the same output format:
## 1. [Newsletter Name]
### How to {avoid problem} {get benefit}, and {ultimate desirable outcome}
[Explanation]

That’s it.

Now you have plenty of ideas to name your newsletter.

Remember the golden rule when it comes to Digital Writing & Digital Building:

This isn’t a marriage decision.

  • You can change the name of your newsletter later.

  • If you decide to change your niche, you can download all the emails from your existing list, launch a new newsletter, and upload all your subscribers into your new newsletter (with a new name) later—if you really want to.

Don’t let the name of your newsletter keep you from STARTING.

(Because, chances are, your first newsletter—just like your first digital product, your first eBook or book, your first online course, etc.—won’t be your last.)
