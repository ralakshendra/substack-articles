The 4 Components of Your Writing Voice

To understand how to isolate your writing voice, it's important to realize that your voice is made up of four key elements:

  1. Voice

  2. Tone

  3. Style

  4. Structure

Your unique writing personality exists at the intersection of these four factors.

But, for the purposes of AI voice cloning, we can simplify this down to just two core components: Voice and Style.

Structure is dependent on what you’re writing, like an opinion piece or a listicle. Tone is subtle, and unless you’re consistently snarky, then it’s not worth training AI to replicate this attribute of your writing.

Step-by-Step Process

Here's the simple process to clone your voice:

  1. Gather 3 samples of your best email writing

  2. Run the samples through the voice/style isolation prompts

  3. Compile your consolidated Voice and Style attribute lists in a document

Once you have your Voice and Style keys, you'll be ready to start generating highly personalized email content with AI!

Lesson 3: The Unique Promise

In this lesson, we dive into a crucial aspect of creating a powerful welcome sequence:

Your unique promise.

This is the core idea that will motivate people to not only opt-in to your list, but also to open, read, click, and ultimately buy from your emails.

[

](https://go.mattgiaro.com/asm/watch/)

The Psychology of Pain and Gain

At the heart of any compelling promise are two core human motivators:

  1. Pain: Humans are hardwired to avoid problems and alleviate pain points.

  2. Gain: We're also driven by the desire to achieve positive outcomes and experiences.

While both are powerful, studies show that pain is often the stronger motivator. Keep this in mind as you frame your promise.

Anatomy of a Powerful Promise

A promise is essentially a problem paired with an intriguing solution.

It has two key components:

  1. The Problem: What painful issue or frustrating challenge is your target audience facing?

  2. The Unique Solution: What's an interesting, novel way you can help them solve that problem?

The key is to not just offer a generic solution, but to put a unique spin on it.

That unique angle is called the "Magnetic Mind Shift."

The Magnetic Mind Shift

The Magnetic Mind Shift is all about getting your audience to see their problem, and the path to solving it, in a different light.

We all see the world through our own lens. Your job is to shift your readers' perspective to align with yours. Get them to view the problem and solution the way you do.

That's how you build connection and credibility.

Uncovering Your Unique Angle

To zero in on your Magnetic Mind Shift, start by deeply understanding your audience and the current narratives in your space.

Look at what other experts in your niche are saying.

  • What do you disagree with?

  • How can you reframe the conversation?

The goal is to uncover those assumptions or myths you can flip on their head with your unique methodology or philosophy.

Step-by-Step Process

Open a new doc and:

  1. State a specific problem your welcome sequence will solve.

  2. Brainstorm interesting ways to solve that problem.

  3. Complete this statement: "At the end of this sequence, I want readers to believe _________."

  4. Use the "Magnetic Mind Shift" prompts below.

Focus on the "why" behind your approach, not just the "how."

Once you've nailed this core promise and unique angle, you'll be ready to craft email content that grabs attention and drives real results. That's what we'll cover in the next lesson.

Lesson 4: Outline Your Sequence

In this lesson, we're diving into how to outline your email sequence and generate a wealth of compelling ideas to craft your series around.

Why Outlines Matter

Think of your outline as a compass. It keeps you oriented and on track, ensuring that your welcome sequence flows logically and delivers on the promise you made to your subscribers.

Benefits of outlining:

  • Organizes ideas in a coherent structure

  • Prevents irrelevant tangents

  • Ensures you fulfill your promise to readers

  • Builds authority and credibility

Without an outline, it's easy to get lost or distracted. But with a clear roadmap, you can create an email series that engages readers and drives them toward your desired action.

Key Elements of Your Outline

To create a strong outline, you need a few key ingredients:

  1. Audience - Who are you writing for? Get specific about your target reader.

  2. Current Belief - What does your audience currently believe about the problem or topic? This is often the status quo you're arguing against.

  3. Desired End Belief - What new perspective do you want readers to adopt by the end of your sequence? This should be the counterpoint to the current belief.

  4. Desire/Problem - What does your audience want to achieve or solve? Articulate the burning pain point or aspirational goal.

  5. Unique Positioning - What's distinct about your approach or solution? How do you solve the problem in a fresh way?

Having clarity on these elements provides the foundation for your outline and ideas.

The 1=1 Rule

As you brainstorm email topics, keep the "1=1 Rule" in mind.

This means:

1 email = 1 core idea

Dedicating each email to a single focus helps you avoid overwhelming readers. It's the same principle as atomic note-taking - isolating each concept for maximum clarity and flexibility.

You can always write more emails. Better to have multiple messages reinforcing one key idea than cramming too many ideas into a single email.

With that, let's explore different angles you can use to generate email topics.

6 Angles for Email Ideas

  1. Chain of Beliefs - Articulate a logical sequence of ideas that shift readers from the current belief to your desired end belief. Each step in the chain can be an email.

  2. Problems - Explore the different facets and implications of your audience's core problem, showing how it manifests in their lives. Each problem dimension can be an email.

  3. Objections - Anticipate and respond to common objections your readers may have about your approach. Each objection can be an email.

  4. Wrong Ways to Think - Highlight flawed assumptions or missteps in how people typically try to solve the problem. Each myth can be an email.

  5. Competitors - Differentiate yourself by critiquing competing approaches in the market. Each competitor weakness can contrast with your unique strength in an email.

  6. Unique Features - Dive into the distinctive elements of your product or methodology. Each feature can showcase your unique value in an email.

You can mix and match these angles to come up with dozens of relevant email topics. Aim for at least 5, and up to 14+ if you have a lot to say!

Step-by-Step Process

Once you have a list of potential email ideas, it's time to organize them into a cohesive outline.

The key is to arrange the topics in a logical flow, leading readers through a compelling sequence of ideas. Each email should build on the last, moving readers toward your ultimate belief and solution.

  1. Gather your key elements: audience, current belief, end belief, desire/problem, and unique positioning.

  2. Use the 6 email angles to brainstorm at least 5 potential email topics. Aim for 1 key idea per email.

  3. Organize your email topics into a logical outline, using the AI prompt.

  4. Refine your outline until you have a clear flow that shifts readers from the current belief to your new paradigm.

With your outline in hand, you'll be ready to write emails that engage readers and guide them toward your unique solution.

The Power of 3 Prompts

Thanks to all the heavy lifting we did in previous lessons - clarifying your unique promise, outlining your sequence, brainstorming email ideas - the writing process is now streamlined.

In fact, you can write virtually unlimited emails with just three core prompts:

  1. Problem Email Prompt

  2. Objection Email Prompt

  3. Wrong Ways to Think Email Prompt

These prompts, combined with the key variables you've already defined, give you a flexible framework to craft compelling messages for any scenario.

Key Email Variables

To make the most of the email prompts, you'll plug in specific variables, such as:

  • Audience

  • Problem/Desire

  • Topic

  • Story

  • Insight

  • Unique Positioning

  • False Belief

  • Correct Belief

Some variables, like your Audience and Unique Positioning, will likely stay constant throughout your sequence. Others, like the specific Problem, Story, or Beliefs, may change from email to email.

The goal is to mix and match these elements to create emails that are relevant to your overall promise while also feeling fresh and engaging.

Email Prompt #1: Problem Email

When should you use this prompt?

  • For the very first email in your sequence

  • For any email that addresses a specific facet of your audience's core problem

The first email is unique because it's your initial touchpoint with new subscribers. In addition to articulating the problem, it should also include:

  • A personal story that builds relatability

  • A quick explanation of what readers can expect from your emails (frequency, value, etc.)

Subsequent problem emails can dive deeper into different dimensions of the overarching issue, showing your nuanced understanding.

Email Prompt #2: Objection Email

This prompt is ideal for emails that:

  • Address common objections to your approach or solution

  • Reinforce your "magnetic mind shift" by reframing objections

  • Proactively answer questions about your unique methodology or product

Tackling reader objections head-on is a powerful way to build trust and open minds to your perspective.

Email Prompt #3: Wrong Ways to Think Email

Use this prompt for emails that:

  • Debunk myths or missteps in how people typically approach the problem

  • Differentiate you from competitors by critiquing flawed solutions

  • Set up a contrast to highlight the benefits of your unique approach

By addressing misguided ways of thinking, you create an opportunity to position your solution as the superior path forward.

Customizing Your Email Voice

One additional step before running the prompts: consider adding instructions to tailor the email voice to your unique style.

You can modify the "Instructions" section of each prompt to specify your desired tone, personality, or writing quirks. This will help ensure the generated emails align with your authentic voice.

Putting It into Practice

With your prompts and variables ready, it's time to generate some emails!

Here's the process:

  1. Pick the appropriate prompt for the email purpose (Problem, Objection, or Wrong Ways to Think).

  2. Plug in your specific variables for that email topic.

  3. Customize the instructions for your unique voice, if desired.

  4. Run the prompt and review the generated email draft.

  5. Edit and refine as needed to ensure the email flows well and sounds like you. Don't be afraid to put your own spin on things!

Repeat this process for each email in your sequence, and watch your welcome series come to life before your eyes.
