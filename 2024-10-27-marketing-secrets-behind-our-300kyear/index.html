The 3-Step Newsletter Supply Chain

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!CYYR!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F87b87dd9-35a0-4fa8-ad0d-3ed237b9b5e5_1391x579.png)

To market a successful paid newsletter, you need to first understand the structure of the business model.

Think of it as a supply chain. It all starts with generating free content.

Here’s the flow:

  1. Free Content Creation: You need to be writing somewhere for free—whether it’s X, LinkedIn, Instagram, or any other platform. The goal here is to build traffic.

  2. Convert to Free Newsletter Subscribers: From your social platforms, you drive traffic to your free newsletter. The goal is twofold: (1) to show that you can provide more value than what’s available on social, and (2) to get people onto your email list so you can eventually upsell them to paid content.

  3. Free-to-Paid Conversion: Once someone is subscribed to your free newsletter, your job is to convince them to upgrade to the paid version by offering exclusive content or expert insights that they can’t get anywhere else.

Most paid newsletters offer one of two types of content: exclusive or expert.

Either you’re giving subscribers access to insider information, research, or behind-the-scenes insights that aren’t shared publicly, or you’re positioning yourself as an expert with decades of experience, delivering knowledge they couldn’t get elsewhere.

How To Choose The Right Newsletter Platform (Substack vs ConvertKit vs Beehiiv)

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!AIVr!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe8e028b2-3717-4fad-9a4d-d277b45d883a_1134x642.png)

The next most common question to building a paid newsletter is, “Which platform should I use?”

Let me break it down:

  • Substack: This is where we host Write With AI. We chose Substack because it acts as a unified feed for both free and paid issues, allowing free subscribers to see what they’re missing behind the paywall. Additionally, Substack has a built-in community element and a recommendation engine that drives new subscribers. In fact, 20% of our paid subscribers come directly from Substack’s internal recommendation engine.

  • ConvertKit: If you’re planning to build a full digital products business with upsells, email courses, and funnels, ConvertKit is a better option. It has more advanced features for digital product marketing—think FOMO sequences, abandoned cart reminders, etc. We use ConvertKit for other projects like Ship 30 for 30 and PGA, but for a straightforward paid newsletter, you don’t need all of these advanced features.

  • Beehiiv: If you’re leaning toward building a full-fledged publication rather than just a newsletter, Beehiiv is worth considering. Beehiiv is particularly strong for landing pages and publication-style newsletters, as evidenced by the success of Milk Road (a crypto newsletter built on Beehiiv).

So, in short: Substack for simple paid newsletters, ConvertKit for advanced digital product businesses, and Beehiiv for full publications.

Your Newsletter Publishing Cadence

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!DGeY!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0225b204-13b1-487e-8a4c-ab201801da9b_1295x471.png)

A simple cadence that works for most paid newsletters is:

  • One free newsletter per week for marketing and growth.

  • One paid newsletter per week as the core product.

The free newsletter serves as your marketing engine, introducing potential subscribers to your paid content. Meanwhile, the paid newsletter is the actual product they’re buying into.

The publishing cadence depends on the value you're promising.

For example, if you're delivering short, actionable stock tips, you might want to send daily newsletters. If you're writing long, detailed insights (like Category Pirates, which I co-founded), a bi-weekly cadence might make more sense.

One key benefit of the paid newsletter model is that the longer you keep producing content, the more valuable your archive becomes. Each new paying subscriber gets access to not just the next newsletter you send but all of the previous ones behind the paywall.

Over time, this compounds the value of your offering and makes it easier to sell.

How to Upsell Free Readers To Paid Subscribers (3 Simple Techniques You Can Start From Day 1)

This is where we get tactical.

The most effective way to convert free subscribers to paying customers is to send snippets of your paid content to your free list. Substack has a feature where you can insert a paywall 25-30% of the way into a newsletter.

Here’s the 3-part strategy:

  1. Send Snippets: Every time we send a paid issue, we intentionally place the paywall about 25-30% down, then send the newsletter to both the paid and free lists. Paid subscribers get the full issue, and free subscribers see a teaser with a paywall. This reminds them that you have something for sale.

  2. Cliffhanger Paywalls: Instead of arbitrarily placing the paywall, insert it at a moment of high tension—right before revealing a valuable insight, framework, or checklist. This creates a "FOMO" effect, making the free subscriber feel like they're missing out on something crucial. When we do this intentionally, we see much higher conversion rates.

Want to know exactly how to implement these strategies? I've created an AI prompt that will help you transform any newsletter into a conversion machine.

🔓 For paid subscribers only:

  1. Engagement CTAs: At the end of every newsletter, we include simple calls-to-action (CTAs), such as:

  2. “PS: Can you refer this issue to a friend?”

  3. “PS: Would you like to gift this paid newsletter to someone?”

  4. “PS: Please comment with ideas for future issues.”

Now, below is a prompt you can use to optimize your newsletter content for maximum free-to-paid conversion. (I just used it to help me insert the paywall you see above.)

Here’s the Conversion Optimizer Prompt:

I'm writing a newsletter about [TOPIC]. 

I want to create a free teaser version that converts to paid subscriptions. 

Here's my full article:

[PASTE FULL ARTICLE HERE]

Please help me:

1. Identify the most compelling 25-30% of content to use as a teaser
2. Suggest the optimal paywall placement for maximum tension
3. Rewrite the section before the paywall to build anticipation
4. Create 3 versions of teaser endings that encourage subscriptions
5. Format this as a complete newsletter with:
   - An engaging opening hook
   - The teaser content
   - A compelling paywall message
   - A strategic CTA

Please maintain my writing style while optimizing for conversions.

How to use this prompt:

  1. Replace [TOPIC] with your newsletter's focus

  2. Paste your draft newsletter content

  3. Use the AI's suggestions to restructure your content

  4. Test different paywall placements with your audience

Pro tip: Run this prompt 2-3 times to get different versions, then combine the best elements from each response.

These small engagement tactics add up over time and help drive growth through word of mouth.

How To Drive Traffic To Your Newsletter Without Running Ads

Lastly, all of our traffic comes from writing short-form content on social media.

Once a day, I write a post on X about writing, AI, or topics related to our newsletter, and link to either the newsletter or our educational email course. The email course gives away free prompts, and once someone subscribes, we tell them about the paid newsletter.

  • We don’t run ads.

  • We don’t have an affiliate marketing army.

  • We don’t partner with influencers.

It’s just consistent, short-form content on social platforms and a clear funnel to the paid product.

That’s it.

That’s the entire marketing system we used to grow Write with AI to $300,000 in annual recurring revenue.

The key takeaway is that you don’t need to overcomplicate things. Focus on writing about a topic you know well, driving traffic from social platforms to your free newsletter, and consistently reminding your free subscribers that there’s a paid version available. Make your paid content tangible and focused on solving specific problems, and you’ll build a successful paid newsletter.

Now, it's your turn to execute. Keep it simple, stay consistent, and don’t get distracted by shiny marketing tactics that don't move the needle.
