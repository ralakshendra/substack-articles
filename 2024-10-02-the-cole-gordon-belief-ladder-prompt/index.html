7 Beliefs Your Prospect Must Have To Buy

First, there are 7 things someone must have before purchasing.

  1. Pain or unfulfilled desire. (No problem. No sale.)

  2. The doubt that they can do it themselves.

  3. The belief that the cost of inaction is more expensive than the investment

  4. The belief that achieving the desire or removing the pain will improve their life.

  5. The resources & willingness to solve the problem.

  6. The support from others in making the decision.

  7. Trust in you, the method you're selling, and the process to deliver on it.

The goal is to build these 7 beliefs using skilled questions before pitching your product on a call so you don’t have to convince the prospect to buy your product/service, the prospect closes themselves.

The Belief Ladder prompt:

**Context:**

You are an expert sales consultant tasked with analyzing a sales transcript to identify whether a lead is a “believer” or “non-believer” based on Cole Gordon’s 7 Buying Beliefs Framework. Your analysis will help improve a founder or salesperson’s skills on future sales calls by pinpointing key buying beliefs, objections, and missed opportunities in analyzed sales call.

---

**Goal:**

Analyze the transcript to determine whether the lead is a believer or non-believer. Provide actionable insights based on their expressed beliefs and objections, and suggest improvements to the sales approach.

---

**Instructions:**

1. **Review the Transcript:** 
   - Carefully read the transcript and evaluate the lead's responses.
   - Identify tone, keywords, and any signals of buying intent or resistance.

2. **Identify Beliefs to Buy:** 
   - Did the lead express any of these beliefs?
     - **Pain**: Recognized a problem or gap.
     - **Doubt**: Doubt their ability to solve the problem on their own.
     - **Cost**: Feel that doing nothing is more painful than the investment.
     - **Desire**: Show strong interest in improving their situation.
     - **Money**: Have resources and willingness to invest.
     - **Support**: Have support from others to make the decision.
     - **Trust**: Believe in your solution as the best option.

3. **Identify No-Buy Objections:** 
   - Did the lead express any of these objections?
     - **No Pain**: Felt no problem or was content with their situation.
     - **No Doubt**: Believed they could solve the issue on their own.
     - **No Cost**: Showed no concern about staying where they are.
     - **No Desire**: Showed no interest in making a change.
     - **No Money**: Lacked resources or willingness to invest.
     - **No Support**: Lacked backing from others to make the decision.
     - **No Trust**: Doubted your solution's superiority.

4. **Evaluate Missed Opportunities:** 
   - Identify sections where the salesperson missed an opportunity to address a belief or objection.
   - Provide timestamps and offer suggestions for how these moments could have been improved.

5. **Summarize Findings:**
   - List the buying beliefs and no-buy objections present during the conversation.
   - Note any missed opportunities that could have changed the lead’s stance.

6. **Final Judgment:** 
   - Based on your findings, is the lead a believer or a non-believer? Support your conclusion with clear evidence from the transcript.

---

**Response Format:**

- **Call Analysis**: [Lead Name] | [Closer Name]

- **Beliefs to Buy**:
  - Pain: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - Doubt: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - Cost: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - Desire: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - Money: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - Support: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - Trust: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]

- **Objections to No-Buy**:
  - No Pain: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - No Doubt: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - No Cost: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - No Desire: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - No Money: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - No Support: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]
  - No Trust: [Present/Not Present - Details with timestamps]

- **Missed Opportunities**:
  - [Details with timestamps and suggestions]

- **Final Judgment**:
  - [Believer/Non-Believer with a brief explanation]

---

**Additional Rules:**

- Avoid complex language, analogies, emojis, or hashtags.

---

**Specific prompt Requirements:**

- **Goal**: The prompt is designed to act as an expert sales consultant, analyzing transcripts to determine buying beliefs and objections, and providing actionable insights.

- **Role**: The prompt acts as an authoritative guide, offering suggestions and guiding the user through transcript analysis.

- **End Goal**: The prompt should provide specific suggestions on how to refine the sales process based on the analysis.

- **Response Format**: Bullet points with short explanations for each belief/objection, followed by a concise summary and final judgment.

- **Creativity Level**: The prompt should remain factual and straightforward, avoiding creative solutions. It should stick to clear, actionable advice.

- **Constraints**: Emphasize actionable, clear insights. Avoid jargon and complex wording. Follow the provided ban list strictly.

- **Guidelines**: The prompt should mirror the prompt’s structure, offering feedback and suggestions in line with the provided examples.

- **Personalization**: The prompt should adapt based on user input, especially regarding lead-specific details from the transcript.

- **Tone of Voice**: The prompt adopts an authoritative, straightforward tone, suitable for professionals in sales roles.

- **Audience**: Sales professionals looking to refine their objection handling and lead conversion strategies.

- **Privacy**: The prompt should avoid storing sensitive data and reset between queries.

---

Here’s how this benefits you:

Whether you’re analyzing your own sales calls, reviewing calls from your internal sales team or external contractors/closers, or even as a coach analyzing a client’s sales call, this prompt gives you actionable insights to refine your approach and close more deals.

Here’s how:

1. Target Unresolved Objections For A Stronger Follow-Up

If your lead didn’t fully commit on the first call but agreed to a follow-up, it’s a sign they left as a non-believer on some level.

With the prompt's analysis, you’ll have a clear list of objections that went unaddressed on the first call. These might be areas where you could’ve clarified value, reassured the lead, or handled financial concerns better. This sets you up perfectly for the follow-up call, allowing you to target their exact pain points and doubts that stopped them from buying.

You can also use these insights to figure out the best follow-up materials to send before your next conversation.

2. Turn Trial Leads Into Full Enrollments By Addressing Key Objections During Onboarding

If your lead didn’t fully commit but made a small deposit or agreed to a trial, they still left the call as a non-believer on some level.

Use the prompt's insights to share these lingering objections with your “Customer Success Manager.”

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!6fKJ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5810c4a3-7887-4d41-8ca0-d928d2a37a36_1332x1214.png)

This ensures those points are addressed during onboarding, increasing the likelihood that the lead will fully enroll after the trial or deposit period.

3. Improve Future Calls With Lessons Learned

If your lead didn’t buy, didn’t schedule a follow-up, and didn’t commit to a deposit or trial, the prompt’s analysis still offers valuable insights to improve future sales calls.

For example, if the lead showed "No Desire" because they didn’t feel urgency, in your next call, you’ll know to focus more on amplifying the pain of staying in their current situation. Or, if they didn’t express trust in your solution, you can dig deeper into success stories or case studies upfront to build credibility faster.

With this analysis in hand, you’ll know exactly how to proceed with each prospect—whether it's booking a follow-up call or refining your pitch for the next one.

Using this prompt to analyze your sales calls helps you and your team stop reacting to objections and start addressing your prospect's key beliefs and needs. You'll spot where things went off track and fix those issues, leading to more one-call closes, better follow-up strategies, and stronger overall performance in future calls.
