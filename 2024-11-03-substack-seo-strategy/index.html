“Wait, newsletters are an email thing, right?

Yes, but…

Your newsletter posts don't just live in people's inboxes. They live on your Substack page. And that page? It's a website Google can find, index, and send traffic to.

It’s a powerful loop.

Someone searches "how to write a newsletter with AI." They find your detailed guide on Substack. Love it. And then they subscribe for more. Why? Because they were actively looking for the solution you provide.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ZcxM!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fdedb67c1-bcd4-4853-9972-2551d434d817_1562x1444.png)

Without SEO, this doesn't happen.

Unfortunately, most Substack writers ignore this newsletter growth channel. If your newsletter isn’t optimized for search, you're invisible on Google. Which means you’re leaving potential subscribers on the table.

For context, 1% of our Write With AI traffic comes from Google and converts subscribers at 4X the rate of any other source.

Here's a quick gut check to see if a little SEO effort might be worth your time:

SEO Makes Sense If:

  • You write how-to content or tutorials

  • You're building a library of content in your niche

  • You create timeless content that solves specific problems

  • Your ideal readers actively search for solutions

Skip SEO If:

  • Your newsletter is more diary than directory

  • You write personal essays or narrative pieces

For example, if you're writing "The Art of Sourdough Baking," SEO can help curious bakers find your expertise. But if you're writing "Letters from my London Life," focus on delighting your subscribers instead.

Not sure where you fall?

Ask yourself: "Would someone search Google for what I write about?"

If yes, keep reading.

If no, stick to what's already working for you.

Let’s dive in.

“Doesn’t Google ignore content from Substack?”

Not exactly.

Google does treat Substack domains (like yourname.substack.com) differently from custom domains, which can sometimes make ranking a bit tougher. However, with Substack’s strong domain authority and custom SEO features, you still have a solid chance of ranking well—if you use a strategic approach.

This guide will show you how.

Quick aside: Optimizing your titles, post descriptions, and content can also improve your visibility within Substack. The internal search helps users discover your newsletter, but it’s not as SEO-driven as Google.

The Biggest Mistake Substack Writers Make That Impacts Their Newsletter SEO

Most people who want to optimize their newsletter using SEO make the same big mistake.

They target broad, popular topics like “indoor gardening.” Because they want a lot of people to see their post. The problem is broad keywords put you up against massive sites like Better Homes & Gardens, Home Depot, and a decade’s worth of established gardening blogs.

It’s a losing battle.

But when you target specific niche search phrases you have a much better chance of ranking your newsletter. So, instead of “indoor gardening tips,” go for something like “indoor gardening for low-light apartments” or “best indoor herbs for winter months in Chicago.”

Specificity is the secret.

And it’s the difference between getting buried on page 100 of Google’s search results and showing up right where people need you. This is the game of SEO. 2 years ago, finding these perfect search terms meant hours of tedious research, spreadsheets, and guesswork.

Now with AI, you can quickly uncover what your readers are searching for in minutes without getting buried in a mound of data

Here’s how to do it:

How To Find Niche Keywords Using AI

Let’s walk through this step-by-step.

Step 1: Start With Seed Keywords

All SEO begins with understanding your “seed” keywords.

Seed keywords are the topics and subtopics your audience searches for in your niche. For example, let's say you are in the indoor gardening niche. Then seed keywords for your topic might be, “indoor plants,” or “indoor herb garden,” or “how to tell if a succulent needs water.” These are all potential content ideas for your indoor gardening newsletter that you could rank for and have Google direct traffic to your Substack.

Here’s how you can find them:

Ask AI to create keyword suggestions around your core topic.

Generate 20 seed keywords for [TOPIC] that include:

- Basic terms
- Subtopics
- Common questions
- User problems
- Purchase intent terms
- How-to searches

Sort them by likely search volume.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!twPw!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F11b5463c-1228-4fa7-9ffd-4fa03aa1915e_1341x1207.png)

From your seed list, you want to pick the most promising keywords to expand on. In our next step we'll get more specific and build out related content around these seed keywords. This strategy shows Google you're a valuable source, helping your content rise in the rankings over time. We don't want to use our seed keywords at first because they are too big to compete for.

AI can help identify keywords, but YOU need to:

  • Verify search volumes using a tool like Google Keyword Planner

  • Check actual search results to understand who’s ranking for these terms

AI can suggest topics but can't tell you how popular they are.

Try this prompt for a quick list:

I want to compete for the keyword(s): [Keyword(s)]. 

Who are my top 10 competitors?

Name them. And tell me what they offer in 1 sentence.

How competitive is the keyword? 

Estimate search volume and competition level.

Pro-tip: If you have a ChatGPT+ subscription, turn on the search function. It’s not as accurate as Google, but you can get a quick idea.

Let’s move to the next step.

Step 2: Niche Down On Your Seed Keywords

Now that you’ve got a list of keywords, you need to narrow it down.

Pick 1-3 keywords with decent volume and manageable competition. Let’s say “indoor herb garden” has a strong search volume. From here, you want to expand your seed keyword into long-tail variations like “how to start an indoor herb garden” or “best containers for indoor herbs” to capture specific user interests.

This helps you create content that attracts and aligns with your audience’s needs.

Here’s a prompt to generate 50 different long-tail “specific” keywords:

Generate 50 long-tail SEO keyword phrases for indoor herb garden. 

Focus on these categories:

1. Problem-solving phrases ("how to fix", "ways to solve", "dealing with")
2. Question-based searches (what, why, how, where, when, who)
3. Comparison phrases (versus, vs, compared to, or, alternative to)
4. Qualifier phrases (best, top, essential, complete, ultimate)
5. Demographic-specific phrases (for beginners, for professionals, for [specific user])
6. Addition phrases (with, without, for)
7. Extension phrases (e.g. extend primary word with words that start with a, b, c, etc.)
8. Location-specific variations (if relevant)
9. Emotional phrases (frustrated, confused, worried, excited)
10. Time-based phrases (2024, quick, fast, instant)

For each keyword phrase:
- Focus on natural language patterns people actually type
- Keep phrases between 3-7 words

The prompt is the equivalent of running a check in Google. For example looking at autocomplete suggestions as you type, the "People also ask" box, and "related searches" at the bottom.

Tip: If you want more keywords for a specific category, follow up with a prompt like, “Please generate 20 more long-tail question-based searches.”

Step 3: Evaluate Your Long-Tail Keywords

When AI gives you these long-tail keywords, you need to filter them through three questions:

  1. Does this keyword match what your newsletter offers? For example, if AI suggests "best hydroponic systems for industrial farming" but you write about small-space gardening for beginners, that's completely wrong for your audience.

  2. Would your reader type this into Google? For example, "indoor herb garden layout ideas for small apartments" might be perfect if your readers are urban gardeners.

  3. Is this keyword too competitive? Google the keyword. If you see mostly big brands or comprehensive guides from established sites, keep looking. You want to see smaller blogs, forums, or Reddit threads in the results.

With your refined keyword list in hand, run each of them through the below prompt to see exactly how to start building your SEO content strategy:

For the following SEO keyword phrases:

{Insert list of target keywords}

Identify:

- search intent (informational, commercial, navigational, transactional)
- Suggested content type
- Target audience segment

Then Output In Format:

## Keyword {Num}: keyword
Recommended for {Audience Segment} who have {Plain language intent}. Best delivered as {Suggested Content Type}. 
For example:
   - {Example Title 1}
   - {Example Title 2}
   - {Example Title 3}

For example:

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!LFxg!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe883b332-8b21-4c8d-94ca-4fbd1caa7860_1496x1004.png)

This analysis helps you see how to turn each keyword into content your readers will love because it reveals:

  • The specific problem they're trying to solve ("limited window access")

  • Their exact situation ("apartment dwellers")

  • The best way to help them ("step-by-step guide")

Instead of just targeting a keyword, you're matching your content to your reader's situation. This is what helps you create content that both ranks AND converts readers into subscribers.

Ready to see how to turn your keywords into a complete content roadmap?

How To Turn One Keyword Into A Complete Newsletter Roadmap

Now we are going to turn your keywords into a roadmap that builds your newsletter's SEO authority over time.

Remember: The goal is to build a content strategy that starts with easier wins (long-tail, specific terms) and eventually helps you rank for bigger terms.

Think of it like a game strategy:

  • Your main keyword "indoor herbs" is like a boss level

  • Each long-tail keyword is a smaller quest

  • Each piece of content you rank for gives you more "domain authority"

  • More authority = ability to tackle harder keywords

Here's how to build your roadmap:

Create a 1-month content strategy for {Target Keyword} including:

Identify:

1. Main pillar content
2. Supporting keyword
3. Content hierarchy

Then output in format:

# Content Hierarchy

## Pillar Content: {Pillar Title} 
*Keyword: {keyword}*
│
│{Theme} (Week 1)
│── Day 1: {SEO Optimized Title 60 characters or less}
│── Day 3: {SEO Optimized Title 60 characters or less}
│── Day 5 :{SEO Optimized Title 60 characters or less}
│── {Target long-tail keywords for week 1}
│

# Linking Strategy
- Title 1 --> Title 2

For example, if I wanted to target the keyword “fast herbs for fresh cooking,” I would insert it where you “{Target Keyword}” in the prompt above before running the prompt. After you hit enter, AI will generate a 1-month content plan for you which includes:

  • 4 weekly themes

  • 3 content pieces per week (including target keywords)

  • A strategy for linking each piece to build up your search authority

Sample output:

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!XYng!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F89009a0f-f449-4b55-aea8-f5b14b775696_1441x3177.png)

You can do this for as many keywords as you want. I recommend you start with 1 key word and 1 month, then expand so you don’t get overwhelmed. You can adapt as you learn.

This focused approach is better than trying to generate long-tail variations for every seed keyword at once. It helps you:

  • Build topic clusters around strong main keywords

  • Create a relevant content hierarchy

  • Target related searches

The more your posts connect and support each other, the more Google sees you as an expert worth ranking. When each post connects to others, it creates a strong network that shows both Google and readers you know your stuff. Which is why you should plan your content in connected clusters instead of random, isolated posts to build search traffic

Let’s do some writing!

How To Optimize Your Newsletter For Google

Now that you have your content plan, let's make sure each post is set up to rank.

Here's the process broken down into three parts:

Step 1: Generate Your Core SEO Assets

Insert a title from your SEO content roadmap into the below prompt.

For the newsletter post [TITLE]:

Create SEO elements including:

1. SEO title (under 60 characters)
2. Meta description (under 155 characters)
3. URL slug
4. Focus keyword and related keywords

This prompt helps you create the technical SEO elements Google looks for.

  • Title & URL

  • Post Description

  • Relevant search terms

Think of it like setting up your store's sign and window display before opening—it tells both Google and readers exactly what's inside.

Set these aside, because we are going to add them to your post once it’s time to publish.

Step 2: Write Your SEO Optimized Outline

With your SEO elements ready, it’s time to create your post structure.

Add the same title you used above and ]your list of target keywords. Again, referencing your content roadmap.


Create a detailed SEO-optimized outline for [TITLE] that naturally includes target keywords {list keywords} and links to {Related Title(s)}

The outline must:
- Answers the primary reader question for the post
- Maintain a clear scannable hierarchy that delivers on the promise of the title
- Make the title feel achievable through logical concrete subheads
- Include placeholders for any related title links
- Limit to max 5 H2's

Output Format As:
# Subject Line Ideas
- 3-5 potential curiosity-driving newsletter subject lines (< 55 characters)
# [Main SEO Title]
[Suggested quick hook]
### H2: [Section Title] (target word count)
- 3 supporting points
# Recommended Add-Ons
- {Tangible Asset}

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!00dy!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F422c2a1c-a51f-41da-b472-cec7e042cadd_1413x1247.png)

This prompt weaves in keywords into your post naturally.

This matters because Google can spot forced keyword placement. Natural integration shows you're writing for humans first, which is exactly what Google wants to see.

❌ Forced (Google hates this):

"If you want to start an indoor herb garden, your indoor herb garden needs good light. Indoor herb gardens need at least 6 hours of light for your indoor herbs to grow."

✅ Natural (Google loves this):

"Ready to start your herb garden? Choose a sunny windowsill – most herbs need about 6 hours of light. Basil, for instance, thrives in bright spots like kitchen windows."

The natural version:

  • Flows like a real conversation

  • Uses keywords where they make sense

  • Varies similar terms (herbs, plants, garden)

  • Includes specific examples

You wouldn't say "indoor herb garden" in every sentence when talking to a friend, so don't do it in your writing.

We use this same strategy in the next step too.

Step 3: Write Your SEO Optimized Newsletter

Now comes the fun part—writing your post.

In the same chat session, run the below prompt one section at a time using each subhead from your outline as input.

Write [Section Name]:

The section must:

- Flows naturally from previous section
- Incorporates [keywords] in a conversational way
- Uses real examples and specific details
- Maintains reader engagement
- Set up next the section

Section Output format:

[1 sentence definition]
2 line feeds
[3 sentences to give the principles behind the concept backed up by a psychological trigger]
2 line feeds
[2 sentence method that’s clear and easy for a beginner to follow. Keep it so simple that no one can fail. Include an example if it makes it more relatable or actionable.]
2 line feeds
[1 sentence end with the single most compelling benefit—what the reader gains in this section.]

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!nCrh!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe21fe759-4dfc-4be5-97b4-8c93c16569e1_1421x993.png)

Don’t forget to add in your thinking.

To do this, change the “Section Output” to include any key points or desired flow.

Repeat the process until you’ve fished all the sections in your outline.

"Wait, won't Google penalize me for using AI?"

Google doesn't penalize AI-assisted content.

They penalize low-quality, unhelpful content—whether it's written by humans or AI.

What matters is:

  • Your content solves real reader problems

  • Your writing sounds natural

  • Your posts link together logically

That's exactly what our process helps you create. We're using AI as a research and organization tool to help you write better content, not to mass-produce generic articles.

Almost done.

We’ve got 2 more steps.

Step 4: Review Your Full Post

Once you have your post written and before you hit publish, you need to do one final optimization check.

This will help you spot any gaps you might have missed.

Here's how to review your post with SEO in mind:

Please analyze the following text for SEO optimization. 

Check for these elements:

1. Identify the primary and secondary keywords used in the post. Check if they appear naturally and without overuse, keeping density around 1-2% for primary keywords.
2. Review the title and subheadings (H1, H2, H3, etc.) for SEO-friendly structure. Check if the primary keyword is in the title and main headings.
3. Look for paragraph length, bulleted lists, or sections that improve readability, like short paragraphs, bold text for important points, or lists where appropriate.
4. Point out if there are any relevant internal links or external authoritative links included. Suggest one or two, if missing.
5. Analyze the tone and readability level, recommending any adjustments to keep readers engaged (aiming for a Grade 8 level).
6. Note any other ways to improve SEO, such as adding FAQ sections, enhancing E-A-T (Expertise, Authority, and Trust), or providing more specific data or examples.

Now, act like a competitor and identify:

- Missing topics
- Unique angles to outperform
- Depth of coverage
- User questions not answered

Output Format:
# Category
- Feedback

# TEXT = 

{Insert Your Post}

Insert the full version of your Newsletter into the prompt and hit run.

Based on what the AI flags, make quick edits to strengthen your post's SEO value without sacrificing readability.

Pro Tip: If you want to outrank your competition, run a competitor’s post through the prompt above and look for ways you can create a more valuable post.

Bonus Step: Make Your Images Work for SEO

Google can't "see" your images.

But it can read their descriptions. Good alt text helps your posts rank better and makes them accessible to everyone.

❌ Bad alt text:

"Screenshot of herbs"

✅ Good alt text:

"Image showing six potted herbs, including basil, thyme, and chives, growing indoors by a window in terracotta pots with fresh green leaves."

Use ChatGPT Dall-E to help you write alt text.

Every image you describe is another chance to help readers find you.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!CERb!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F720a8063-4163-43ad-ab71-a65cf3bffaab_1742x1208.png)

Here’s the prompt:

Create SEO-friendly alt text for [attached image]:

Requirements:

- Be specific and descriptive
- Include relevant keywords naturally
- Keep under 125 characters
- Describe what the image shows, not what it is
- Format: "Image showing [description]"

No keyword stuffing. No technical headaches.

Here's your action plan recap:

  1. Generate your seed and long-tail keywords

  2. Build your 1-month SEO Newsletter roadmap

  3. Generate your SEO elements for each post

  4. Write your newsletter, focus on your reader, and let the keywords flow

  5. Add your SEO meta elements in Substack

  6. Run a final SEO check

  7. Hit publish

Remember: You're not writing for Google. You're writing for readers who use Google to find solutions. Keep that in mind, and the rest falls into place.
