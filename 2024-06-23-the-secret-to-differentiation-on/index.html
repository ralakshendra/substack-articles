The Secret To Differentiation On The Internet

Author: Nicolas Cole
Date: 2024-06-23

Ahoy, Digital Writers!

Does it seem like everybody is getting rich on the internet except you?

Every idea they come up with works, but everything you try just doesn't.

Here’s why:

They understand the secret to differentiation and you don't...yet.

The secret?

Solve 1 Specific Problem For 1 Specific Person In 1 Specific Way

This formula has the power to turn any idea, no matter how small, into a product or service that will immediately grab people's attention.

All those entrepreneurs you see online - creators, influencers - they aren't any smarter than you. They're just using this simple strategy to separate themselves from any and all competition, which is why it seems like the internet loves them more than it loves you.

Last year, I paid $68,000 to join a mastermind with some of the biggest creators in the world.

And they all, in some way or another, used this simple 1-1-1 framework to build their seven and eight figure businesses. Now, you can either join some fancy schmancy mastermind like I did and pay $68,000, or you can sit back while I give you the secret to differentiation.

And at the end, I'm going to give you an AI prompt to help you start differentiating yourself on the internet.

But first, let's start with the problem.

1 Specific Problem

Most people think the key to getting customers or even going viral is to appeal to the widest number of people possible.

Translation: “I solve lots of different types of problems.” But let me tell you a little story that shows how, as a customer, this isn't actually what you want.

Let's say you have a stained glass window above your garage. And one day, your neighbor's idiot teenager accidentally throws a rock through it. So you go on TaskRabbit to find someone to help you fix it. And what you find are hundreds and hundreds of people who all call themselves "window repair specialists". And they all have five-star reviews, and they all have tons of testimonials.

But this isn't any old window, right? This is a stained glass window

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5XA0!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fcb1e2af7-d4a3-4c1f-909e-c04626105123_1792x1024.png)

.And you want to make sure that you hire the right person for the job. So after a lot of scrolling, you eventually find one person who doesn't have that many reviews and doesn't have a ton of testimonials. But in their bio, they call themselves a "stained glass window repair specialist".

So out of the hundreds and hundreds of TaskRabbits, which person are you going to call?

Are you going to call the generalist who says they can do anything for anyone? Or are you going to call the specialist who says all I do, all day long, is solve the exact problem that you have

You're going to call the specialist every single time.

Now, you might be sitting there going, "Cole, if I say I only solve one specific problem, that's going to cut out 99% of people." And you're right, that's a good thing. Because when you try to be something for everyone, you end up being something for no one.

So the secret to differentiation is you can't say you solve every problem.

You have to say you solve one specific problem.

1 Specific Person

The next question is: for who?

For everyone who has that problem? No. For one specific person who has that problem. So watch what happens when we keep the problem the same, but we swap out different target audiences:

  • Stained glass window repair for royal families

  • Stained glass window repair for antique dealers

  • Stained glass window repair for churches

Same problem, but three completely different target buyers.

And again, you might be saying, "Cole, but if I say I only work with one specific type of person, that's going to cut out 99% of people." Yes, it will. And that's a good thing. Because each one of these buyers has a completely different set of sub-problems, wants, needs, and expectations.

Do you really think having the royal family as a client is going to be the same as having a church as a client?

No, of course not. Which means if all you do is work with one type of person, you're going to know how to help that type of person better than anyone else, which differentiates you even more.

But the real magic happens when you solve one specific problem for one specific person in one specific way.

1 Specific Way

Coming up with a different way to help people is always the hardest part, because every industry has solutions that are already popular, right?

Struggling to lose weight? You hire a personal trainer or a nutritionist. Struggling to sell your house? You hire a real estate agent. These are already the popular solutions to these problems. Your job, and the secret to differentiating yourself and any product or service, is to come up with a different way to solve that one specific problem for one specific person.

Because if the way you help these people is different than what everyone else provides, guess what? You officially have zero competition.

“But will this work for me?”

I know the 1-1-1 framework works because this is how we built our beginner writing program, Ship 30 for 30, to millions of dollars in revenue.

We solved one specific problem for one specific person in one specific way.

  • Problem: People can't write on a consistent basis.

  • Person: Someone who wants to start writing on the internet, not all the people who have already been writing on the internet for a decade.

  • Way: Creating a 30-day writing challenge that holds them accountable to a daily writing habit.

It's so simple.

And this is how Ship 30 became the fastest growing writing program on the internet. You might be thinking, "Okay, well Cole, sure that worked for you. But can this really work for people like me?"

Yes, it absolutely can.

And I also know because I've coached hundreds of ghostwriters in our premium ghostwriting academy on how to implement this 1-1-1 framework, differentiate themselves, and start pitching high-ticket clients with a new and unique service.

And we now have people in our ghostwriting program doing anywhere from $5,000 a month as a side-hustle to $50,000 a month running a small one-person agency.

So yes, this 1-1-1 framework can work for any product or any service.

Now, I promised you I would show you how to do this AI. .

So, let’s get to it.

How To Create Your Differentiation Strategy With AI

This series of 5 sequential prompts will help you work through the 1-1-1 framework even if you are starting from scratch.

The first step: who is your who?

Step 1: Name Your WHO

I like starting with Who because a problem without a person is like a shoe without a foot - it's just not a good fit!

Include demographics, psychographics, etc. Niche down with as much detail as you want.

For example:

"I want to help tech startup founders in their 30s, working 60+ hours a week to grow their SaaS business. They're passionate about their product but struggle to find time for strategic planning and self-care. They value efficiency, innovation, and making a positive impact."

Tip: If you’re tuck, run the 2-Year Test on yourself.

Step 2: List The Top 10 Problems For Your Target WHO

Once you know your specific Who, the next step is to generate a list of problems

For example:

Imagine you are a tech startup founder in their 30s, working 60+ hours a week. Provide a list of 10 tangible and quantifiable examples of specific, concrete, and real-world problems they might face in their work and life that negatively impact their time, money, or relationships. Use 10 words or less per example.

Use 10 words or less per problem.

Output:

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2Nks!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F759264dc-638e-464a-aacd-816a4a73d980_1074x708.png)

In this prompt, I ask Claude to focus on tangible and specific examples instead of generic problems. “20% of monthly expenses going to office rent” is 10X clearer than “high operational costs.” This is how your target audience will identify with the problem.

Choose 1 problem and move to the next step.

Step 3: List 10 Reasons Why Your WHO Has This Problem

There are many reasons a problem can exist.

And choosing a specific reason will help you refine your solution.

For tech startup founder in their 30s, working 60+ hours a week and have the problem "Last vacation was 18 months ago, lasted 3 days".  

Brainstorm 10 potential reasons they might be experiencing this issue. 

Use 10 words or less per reason.

Check out these 10 different reasons a SaaS founder could be experiencing the problem “I haven’t had a vacation in 18 months.”

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!d8NM!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F68133a8a-15c6-40c2-b9a4-65c794724eb0_1220x682.png)

Again, pick one. Then move to the next step to create your 1 specific way.

Step 4: List 10 Different Ways To Solve The 1 Specific Problem

Now that you have your problem and person, let’s use AI to up with different ways to solve the problem.

For example:

List 10 ideas for a signature service, program, or product that solves tech startup founder in their 30s, working 60+ hours a week whose last vacation was 18 months ago, lasted 3 days when it stems from a fears of losing competitive edge by taking extended time off. 

Use 1 sentence for each.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!c3mV!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F04be1944-585f-4692-985b-77917391f30a_1188x1084.png)

Look at the ideas and pick the one you like best. If you don't find any you like, you can either:

  1. Ask for more ideas, or

  2. Start over with a different problem, person, or reason.

Each set of ideas provides various unique options to consider.

Let’s wrap up with one last step.

Step 5: Assemble Your 1-1-1 Differentiation Strategy

Finally, all you have to do is ask AI to assemble your choices into the 1-1-1 framework for you.

For example:

Ok, I liked this solution: 

"A temporary executive placement service that provides experienced leaders to oversee operations during founder vacations."

Please assemble all my choices in the folowing format:

- Problem: 1 sentence
- Person: 1 sentence
- Way: 1 sentence

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WwcR!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa9af07e9-7328-42f0-b297-3b1a2899a96f_1204x250.png)

Now, I would tighten it up to something like “Temporary executive placement service for tech founders who want to take a real vacation.”

A quick Google search shows no ads for this service. This could mean either:

  1. Nobody wants it (bad), or

  2. There's no competition (good).

You'll need to do more research, but this shows how combining the person, problem, and way helps you create a unique offering that stands out.

Give the prompts a try.

Run each of the prompt blocks separately.

List the top 10 problems {SPECIFIC PERSON} face in their work and life that keeps impacts their time, money, or relationships.

Use 10 words or less per reason.

I liked this solution: 

{SPECIFIC WAY}

Please assemble all my choices in the folowing format:

- Problem: 1 sentence

That’s it.

And here's the big takeaway.

The faster you can make these three things clear in your bio, on your website, in your cold outreach, the more likely you will be to succeed on the internet. Remember, the internet wants to make you rich. Social algorithms are literally engineered to help you reach the right customers and for the right customers to be able to discover you. All you have to do is tell the internet what makes you different, and the algorithm takes care of the rest.
