    Hey there, Digital Writers!

    To get the most out of AI writing, there are 3 key skills you need to master:

    • Input

    • Form

    • Frame

    Input is what you tell AI, so it knows what to write. Form is how you use signs and styles AI understands, so it can follow your lead. Frame is how you set up your prompt, so it's easy for AI to grasp.

    When you use all three, you can get great AI output that fits your needs.

    Last week, we covered Input with “The W.R.I.T.E.R.S Compass.” Today, we'll look at prompt form—the language or syntax to "talk" with AI for clear, on-point text that will help you get even better results from your AI writing tools.

    At the end of this post, I'll leave you with a prompt template that includes all the key form elements, which you can use immediately.

    Let’s dive in.

    What Is Prompt Form?

    Prompt form is how you use signs and styles AI knows.

    Just like a base coach uses hand signals in baseball to tell a runner when to bunt, steal or stay on the bag, you can use special characters and formatting to guide AI. Baseball players know the game they are playing (the input), but they need the signals (the form) to help them play to win.

    In the same way, prompt form helps AI understand your instructions better.

    • Use #tags to show themes or topics, like a coach might tug on his ear to indicate a hit-and-run

    • Add [tips] or (notes) for extra guidance, like a coach might tap his head to tell the runner to think and make a smart play

    By using these "signs," you help AI "run the bases" of your prompt more effectively.

    When you mix the Writers Compass (Input), Form, and Frame, you give AI a full game plan. This helps AI create text that fits your needs like a glove, just as a base coach's signs help a runner make the right moves. If you skip any of these parts, your prompt may not do the job, like a runner who misses a sign and gets thrown out.

    The Two Types Of Prompt Form

    When using form, there are two main types of "signs" you can use:

    • Critical

    • Optional

    Let’s take a look at how to use them in your prompts starting with Critical signs.

    Form Type #1: Critical

    Critical signs tell AI what info is a must.

    These special characters draw attention to the most important parts of your prompt.

    For example:

    • Hashtags for key points

    • for specific instructions

    • [Square brackets] for placeholders

    • CAPITAL LETTERS for crucial elements

    Simple enough.

    Let’s look at the differences, so you know how to put these to work.

    Angle Brackets <>

    Angle brackets are used to specify exact instructions or variables that should be replaced with specific information. They indicate that the enclosed text is a placeholder for something that needs to be defined or provided by the user.

    Example:

    In this example, the angle brackets indicate that "Content Type" and "Word Count" are variables that need to be filled in with specific information. The square brackets within the angle brackets provide examples or instructions for what should be included.

    Square Brackets []

    Square brackets are used as placeholders for information you want the AI to fill in based on your specific topic or requirements. They indicate that the enclosed text should be replaced by the AI with content relevant to the given context.

    Example:

    • [Introduction paragraph that captures the reader's attention and introduces the main topic]

    • [Subheading 1: Discuss the first key point or benefit]

    • [Subheading 2: Discuss the second key point or benefit]

    • [Conclusion paragraph that summarizes the main points and provides a call to action]

    In this example, the square brackets contain instructions for the AI to generate specific sections of the content, such as the introduction, subheadings, and conclusion. The AI will replace these placeholders with relevant text based on the provided topic and context.

    Square brackets allow you to outline the key components of the content without having to write out the full text, saving time and effort while still maintaining control over the overall structure and focus.

    Hashtags

    The hashtag (#) is used to highlight the key points or main ideas you want the AI to focus on when generating the content. The hashtag serves as a visual cue to emphasize the importance of these points and ensure that the AI doesn't miss them when processing the prompt.

    For example:

    • [Key Point 1]

    • [Key Point 2]

    • [Key Point 3]

    The hashtag is placed before the square brackets, which serve as placeholders for the actual key points you want to include in your prompt.

    When creating your prompt, you would replace the square brackets and their contents with your specific key points, like this:

    • The benefits of regular exercise for physical health

    • The mental health benefits of exercise

    • How to create a sustainable exercise routine

    Using hashtags in this manner helps to visually separate and highlight the key points, making it easier for the AI to identify and understand the main ideas you want to be addressed in the generated content.

    Double Hashtags+

    The double or triple hashtags (##) are used in the prompt template to create headings or section titles, making the structure of the prompt more organized and easier to read. In markdown formatting, which is commonly used in documentation and online content, the double hashtag is used to denote a second-level heading.

    For example:

    • Main Heading (First Level)

    • Subheading (Second Level)

    • Sub-subheading (Third Level)

    In the Master Prompt template below, the double hashtags are used to separate and identify different sections of the prompt, such as:

    • Key Points

    • Target Audience

    • Style and tone

    By using these headings, you can clearly distinguish between the various components of the prompt, making it easier for both you and the AI to understand the structure and content requirements.

    Capital Letters

    Using capital letters is a way to emphasize the most important aspects, requirements, or guidelines that you want AI to follow.

    By capitalizing certain words or phrases, you draw attention to these elements, making it clear to the AI that they are non-negotiable and should be prioritized in the output.

    Benefits of using capital letters:

    1. Emphasis: Capital letters stand out visually, making it easier for the AI to identify and focus on the most critical parts of your prompt. This ensures that the AI doesn't overlook or misinterpret these elements.

    2. Clarity: By highlighting crucial elements with capital letters, you make your expectations and requirements more explicit and unambiguous. This clarity helps the AI better understand your needs and generate content that aligns with your goals.

    3. Prioritization: When you capitalize certain elements, you signal to the AI that these aspects are of the utmost importance and should be given priority in the generated content. This helps the AI make decisions about what to include or exclude when there are competing requirements or limited space.

    However, you should use capital letters sparingly for the most crucial elements to maintain their impact and avoid undermining the effectiveness of the emphasis.

    Now, let’s move on to Optional Signs.

    Form Type #2: Optional

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Et_R!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7aa3c7ee-057a-425d-a96a-37213ed187f8_1024x1024.webp)

    Optional signs give AI more context about what you want it to think about when generating text.

    They help AI read “between the lines” and generate output that fits your intended subtext.

    For example:

    • {Curly braces} for optional sections

    • (Parentheses) for notes or explanations

    • |Pipes| for alternate options

    • Asterisks for emphasis

    • +Plus signs+ for additional elements

    Let’s take a look at each one.

    Curly Braces {}

    Curly braces are used to indicate optional sections within a prompt. These sections can be included or omitted based on the specific task or desired outcome. By using curly braces, you give the AI the flexibility to generate content with or without the enclosed information, depending on what is most relevant or appropriate for the given context.

    Example:

    • [Topic: The benefits of yoga for physical and mental health]

    • {Target Audience: Beginners interested in starting a yoga practice}

    • {Tone: Informative and encouraging}

    In this example, the "Target Audience" and "Tone" sections are enclosed in curly braces, indicating that they are optional. The AI can generate content based on the content type and topic, and it may or may not incorporate the target audience and tone information, depending on what it deems most suitable.

    Optional sections help keep the main prompt structure clean and focused, while still providing the AI with room for creativity.

    Parentheses ()

    Parentheses are used to provide additional notes, context, or explanations that don't fit within the main prompt structure. These notes serve as supplementary information to help guide the AI in generating more accurate and relevant content.

    Example:

    • [Topic: The impact of social media on mental health]

    • (Note: Focus on the positive and negative aspects, and provide actionable tips for maintaining a healthy relationship with social media)

    In this example, the note enclosed in parentheses gives the AI additional context and direction for generating the article. It specifies that the article should cover both positive and negative aspects of social media's impact on mental health and include practical advice for readers.

    Parentheses enable you to add context that may not be directly related to the main prompt but can still influence the generated content.

    Pipes

    Pipes are used to provide the AI with alternative words, phrases, or instructions to choose from when generating content. This gives the AI some flexibility in how it expresses ideas while still maintaining the overall intent of your prompt.

    For example:

    Suppose you want the AI to choose between three snacks: apples, bananas, or carrots. You would write it like this:

    [apples | bananas | carrots]

    Asterisks

    Asterisks are used to emphasize specific words, phrases, or instructions within your prompt. They draw attention to important elements that the AI should focus on or prioritize when generating content.

    For example:

    Must include a strong call-to-action

    For extra emphasis, use asterisks and capitals to communicate importance to the AI.

    Plus Signs

    Plus signs are used to indicate optional or supplementary elements that the AI can include in the generated content if relevant or appropriate. These elements are not essential but can add value, context, or depth to the output.

    By marking certain elements as optional, you allow the AI to adapt the content to different contexts or requirements while still maintaining the core focus of your prompt.

    For example:

    • Relevant quotes or statistics to support the main points +

    This prompt suggests that the AI can include relevant quotes or statistics to reinforce the main points, but it is not a strict requirement.

    By incorporating these advanced techniques into your prompts, you can create crystal-clear instructions that are easy for AI to understand and follow. The more specific and well-structured your prompts are, the better the output you'll receive.

    So, next time you're crafting a prompt, remember to:

    • Highlight key points

    • Denote priority with CAPITALS

    • and [provide a clear structure] with angles and brackets to create templates that are easy to understand, customize, and use.

    • And use {optional sections}, (explanations), |alternatives|, emphasis, and +additional elements+

    With these tools in your prompting toolkit, you'll be on your way to mastering the art of AI prompting and creating top-notch content more efficiently than ever before.

    To help you remember all these techniques, here’s a template that includes all the key elements and special characters discussed in the post:

    <Content Type: [Blog post, article, social media post, product description]>
[Topic: Your main topic or subject goes here]

#[Key Point 1: Most important takeaway or benefit]
#[Key Point 2: Second most important takeaway or benefit] 
#[Key Point 3: Third most important takeaway or benefit]

{Target Audience: [Specify your intended audience]}

{Tone and Style: [Desired tone and writing style, e.g., informative, persuasive, conversational]}

(Additional Notes: Any extra context or requirements go here)

|Optional Element 1: [Alternative word, phrase, or idea]|

|Optional Element 2: [Another alternative word, phrase, or idea]|

*EMPHASIZE IMPORTANT POINTS OR REQUIREMENTS*

+Supplementary elements that add value but are not essential+

    That’s it.
