Ahoy, Digital Writers!

Today, we are going to help you write succinct, clear, and enticing subject lines for an Educational Email Course (EEC) using ChatGPT.

So you can get readers to:

  • Click

  • Open your email

  • Read through to the very end

And then set an alarm, so they don’t miss the next email you send!

Unfortunately, when it comes to writing subject lines many writers think the “trick” is to be clever, not CLEAR.

Trying to be Clever, not CLEAR.

The reader doesn’t care how clever you are.

Because clever is confusing! And when you confuse the reader—you lose. They don’t open your email and they move on to the next one in a list of 237 unread messages in their inbox.

For example:

"Unlock the secrets of health!"

Intriguing? Maybe, but it's vague and doesn't tell the reader what's inside. What problem does this solve? What tangible and specific benefit will you get for reading?

Feels ignorable, right?

The subject line should grab attention and hint at what the reader can expect:

  • What’s in it for me? "Lose 10lbs in 30 days."

  • And what’s inside? “Free eBook”

Much better.

Be CLEAR, not clever.

BUT it’s not just being clever that trips up subject line writers.

4 Mistakes Every Email Subject Line Writer Makes

There are 4 mistakes that turn potential readers away before they even open your email.

  • Mistake #1: Long subject lines. If a subject line gets cut off before “the hook” is revealed, the chances of a reader clicking is very low. Why? Because they don't have a reason to!

  • Mistake #2: Formal tone. Your email is personal. What email are you more likely to open? The one from your friend, “Barbie movie tonight?!” or “Tax Strategies Inside?” Casual and clear win here.

  • Mistake #3: Ineffective hooks. There’s no need to re-invent the wheel. Use what’s been proven to work for 100’s of years! Promise a lot for a little or instantly! (Just make sure you are keeping the promise.)

  • Mistake #4: Ignoring the preview-text. The preview-text is the grey sentence next to the the subject line. It’s like a “whisper.” Hey…psst…here’s what’s inside. It’s the perfect way to reinforce your hook and set clear expectations for your email (without making your subject line too long).

The good news?

With a touch of ChatGPT, you can turn your subject lines and preview-text into irresistible invitations that elevate your Educational Email Course from just another email in the inbox to an email you reader can’t wait to open and avoid all these mistakes in 1 sitting!

The Secret To 2X, 3X, Even 5X’ing Email Course Opens

The real “trick” to hooking your reader’s attention in their inbox is to include something that will make them want to “click.”

  • Here's a ton of value for minimal time.

  • Here's a ton of value for minimal cost.

  • Here's how to solve your problem without much effort.

  • Here's how to unlock this desirable outcome, instantly.

Nail these, and you’ll be watching your open rates climb from a dismal 7% to an eye-popping 50%+. Our Educational Email Course for startwritingonline.com is 70%!

There’s a reason our open rate is so high—clarity.

We offer a clear promise to the reader for taking the course “Become A Prolific Writer In 7 Days.” Then, for each day of the course, we have a crystal clear email subject line, so the reader knows what to expect.

And then we deliver on the promise.

Let’s call in the intern!

How To Write Subject Lines For An Educational Email Course Using ChatGPT

We are going to break this down into 4 steps.

Step 1: Train ChatGPT On Email & Preview-Text

In this prompt we give ChatGPT the role of email marketer.

We ask it to leverage the 4 proven types of hooks, “whisper” in the preview-text, and avoid the most common mistakes we outlined above. Lastly, for this prompt we offer examples to help guide the AI on the types of output we are looking for.

Act like an expert email marketer with 10 years of experience.
You are a master at grabbing people's attention in their email inbox.

I want to write an email subject line and subtext to capture my target audience's attention.

The job of the subject line is to hook the reader (in an informal fashion, like a note to a friend)

The job of the subtext is “Whisper.” A “here’s what’s inside” sentence. (think parenthetical in copywriting)

Here's what we are going to do:

I am going to give you a topic, an audience, and an outline for a 5-Day Educational Email Course.

After I have provided you the topic, audience,and outline, you will ask me: "How would you like preface each subject line?" You will let me know what my choices are. 

The subject line preface choices are: 
1) Preface the subject with "Day #:", like "Day 1:" for Day 1 in the course. 
2) Name & Claim an idea. Like "Life Coach Money Manual:" 
3) No preface

Once you have everything you need, you will create 3 email subject lines with a corresponding subtext line for each type of the 4 proven hooks for each day in the Email Course.

You will write the subject lines and subtext 1 day at a time, starting with the 1st day.

Here are the 4 proven hooks:
    1. A ton of value for minimal time.
    2. A ton of value for minimal cost.
    3. How to solve your problem without much effort.
    4. How to unlock a desirable outcome, instantly.

Some good subject line example to follow:
- "Your entire Life Coach career path blueprint… in 1 email!"
- "Here's our entire $1M marketing plan. Steal it!"
- "Losing money in the stock market? Just buy these 3 companies."
- "3 keys to land $10,000 consulting clients, from home, in your pajamas, right now."
- "5 steps to write your first viral Twitter thread as a Digital Creator (even if you have 0 Followers)"

The things you can “whisper” are:

- A “trust me” sentence
- A “without this obstacle” sentence
- A “and with this additional benefit” sentence
- A “and so you can achieve this outcome too” sentence

For example:

- Trust Whisper = "Written By A Twitter Creator With 100k Followers"
- Obstacle Whisper = "Without Spending Any Money On Ads"
- Benefit Whisper = "And How To Solve It"
- Outcome Whisper = "And Start Earning $250,000 Per Year In Your Sweatpants"

Here's is and example of what your output will look like for each subject line and pretext you create:
- "Subject Line 1 (Value for Time): Avoid 3 common Twitter pitfalls in minutes."
- "Subtext (Outcome Whisper): And save months of misguided effort."

A few rules:
- Rule #1: Use 15 words, max.
- Rule #2: The subject line should read like you're talking to a friend. (use sentence case)
- Rule #3: Use visceral TANGIBLE language
- Rule #4: Intrigue the reader
- Rule #5: Use numbers
- Rule #6: Be SUPER SPECIFIC

Remember: DO NOT exceed the word count limit for effective subject lines. 

Do you understand?

Let me know when you are ready for topic, the outline and the audience.

Once ChatGPT confirms it understands the prompt, move on to the next step.

Step 2: Feed ChatGPT Your EEC Content/Outline

In this step, you are going to give ChatGPT your topic, outline, and target audience.

Feeding ChatGPT this context will ensure the subject lines it writes are on par for your course.

For example:


Topic = 5-day “Crash Course” on how to get started writing on Twitter

Audience = Beginner Writers

Outline = 
- **Day 0: Introduction & Email Course Outline - “Here’s what you can expect”**
    - Introduction to the email course and what participants can expect to learn.
    - Overview of the 5 things that will be covered in the course.
- **Day 1: 3 Most Important Things To Know**
    - Teach, Don’t Entertain
    - You Should Aim To Post 2-3x Per Day
    - The Secret Is To Niche Down
- **Day 2: 3 Mistakes Most People Make**
    - Trying To Write For “Everyone”
    - Failing To Double-Down On Your Winning Topics
    - Giving Up After 30 Days (Or Less)
- **Day 3: 3 Steps To Go From Zero to 1**
    - Name & Claim Your Niche In Your Bio
    - Turn Your Best Tweets Into Threads
    - Respond To Every Single Comment
- **Day 4: 3 Simple Upgrades To Accelerate Growth**
    - Upgrade Your Bio Headshot & Banner Photo
    - Upgrade Your “Pinned Asset”
    - Upgrade Your CTAs
- **Day 5: 3 Best Practices Moving Forward**
    - Comment On Other Creator’s Most Viral Tweets & Threads
    - Review Your Analytics Once Per Month
    - Once You Find A Winning Topic, Repeat It (Endlessly!)

Here’s a template for you to fill in with your outline:

Topic = {Your Topic}

Audience = {Your Audience}

Outline = {Your Outline}

Step 3: Choose A Subject Line Preface

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!09f4!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb4e781fb-b222-48f0-b2d8-ad6c229ae8a9_1050x428.png)

The last step before we generate the subject lines is to decide if you want to preface each day in your course to start with the “Day,” so your reader is reminded about what day in the course they are on.

Alternately, you can ask ChatGPT to Name & Claim your ideas in the subject line.

For example, instead of “3 easy ways to make 10x more money as a life coach (nobody tells you this”, you could Name & Claim your email series as, “Life Coach Money Manual: 3 (little known) ways to 10x your monthly earnings.”

Notice how much more it feels like you are getting “something tangible” as a reader when you use these techniques. And when you feel like you are getting something, you open the email!

Step 4: Generate And Choose Your Subject Line

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!j2Ez!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2a64796f-c6b4-462a-96b8-cd1227cc9ea3_1200x860.png)

Bang!

Now you’ve got a batch of subject lines and preview-text you can use for each day in your Educational Email Course.

Read through each one—out loud.

Ask yourself:

  • “Would I open this?”

  • “Do I want what’s inside?”

  • “What question does this raise for me?”

If it’s unclear to you, look to see if ChatGPT is making any of the common mistakes we covered and then redirect it to make those corrections for you, or make the adjustments on your own!
