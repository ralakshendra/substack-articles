    Step 1: Brainstorm Lead Magnet Ideas With Claude

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0PYM!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F96b25c0b-4f93-489b-9c34-3653578a8c72_2194x3866.png)

    Every successful lead funnel starts with a trade.

    You give away something of value in exchange for an email address. So this first prompt will help you brainstorm app lead magnet ideas - which is going to give you the foundation for growing your audience and getting you leads.

    The reason an app is a great lead magnet is because it gives your audience something they **can use—not just download and forget.

    Most lead magnets are passive: PDFs, checklists, even free courses.

    An app, on the other hand, delivers active utility.

    It helps your audience do something:

    • Calculate something

    • Organize something

    • Track something

    • Spit out an answer

    • Remove a step

    It gives them a result.

    It works because it actively solves a problem for them in less time with less headaches.

    Now, if you already have a lead magnet, you can skip this step. But if you want an app idea, then make sure you run this prompt. So, crack open Claude, copy/paste the prompt below, and then fill in the variables in the input section with your personal info.

    Here’s the prompt:

    You're a business strategist who specializes in creating problem hierarchies for digital products and services. I need you to map out a complete problem hierarchy for my client's business.

INPUT:
- Industry/Niche: {Your Industry}
- Target Audience: {Your Audience}
- Main Offer: {Your Main Offer}

TASK:
Create a 2-tier problem hierarchy that flows from small problems to the ultimate transformation.

OUTPUT FORMAT:
TIER 1: THE MAIN PROBLEM (What their paid offer solves)
- State the big, expensive problem their audience faces
- What happens if this problem isn't solved?
- What's the ultimate transformation the main offer provides?

TIER 2: SMALL PROBLEMS (What lead magnets solve)
Create 5 medium-sized problems that are:
- Components of the main problem
- Solvable with a single resource/guide
- Valuable enough that people would opt-in for the solution

Format: 
"Problem: [SPECIFIC ISSUE]" → 
"Solution: [WHAT YOU'D CREATE]"
"Lead Magnet: [WHAT YOUR AUDIENCE GETS]"

NATURAL PROGRESSION:
Show the customer journey from free solution to paid comprehensive solution.
Make this specific to their expertise and audience psychology.

MINI-SAAS IDEAS:
Recommend 3 potential "Mini-SaaS" ideas I could create as a lead magnet.
A Mini-SaaS solves one hyper-specific problem in as few interactions as possible.
Think simple inputs to automate what normally takes 10X longer.
Must be capable of prototyping with Claude artifacts / AI-powered apps.

    After hitting “send” on the prompt above, Claude will give you a list of 5 different lead magnets and app ideas you could run with based on your specific niche & target audience.

    Spend a few minutes reviewing the options and choose the one you like the most.

    Once you have your app idea locked in, move onto the next step.

    Step 2: Write Your Landing Page Copy

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!90AZ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F84fccb13-a686-4df3-acbd-9e8272e36f67_1942x1718.png)

    Now that you have a lead magnet idea, the next step is to write your opt-in copy.

    This is how you offer your app.

    Great lead magnets die on poorly written landing pages, so this is a critical step in the process. Writing the landing page copy forces you to get crystal clear on what transformation you're promising and why someone should even care in the first place.

    The good news is you only need to know 5 things to write your landing page:

    1. Your ideal customer.

    2. The promise you are making.

    3. The reason people might object to your promise.

    4. The way you deliver on the promise.

    5. And how you will call your reader to action

    That’s it.

    Think through the above 5 points first and then run the below prompt to write all your above-the-fold landing page copy in 5-10 minutes. Claude writes each part of the landing page one step at a time. So, all you gotta do is review the output it gives you at each stage and tell it how you’d like to proceed (so it can move onto the next step).

    Remember to insert your lead magnet idea from Step 1 into the prompt before you run it!

    Here’s the prompt:

    I would like to run with this lead magnet:

{Insert lead magnet concept}

Now, let's move onto the next thing I'd love your help with:

Building a high-converting landing page for my lead magnet.

More specifically, I'd like your help writing the "above the fold" copy for my lead magnet landing page.

There's 5 main components the landing page should have:

1. A pre-headline to grab the target audience's attention.
1. A headline making a compelling promise to the reader
2. A sub-headline explaining how the lead magnet delivers on the promise I'm making in the headline
3. A CTA inviting people to sign up (for the opt-in form button)
4. A social proof statement. 

But each of these elements should be created in order - so instead of writing all the copy at once, I want you to go element by element & brainstorm 20 potential variations for each.

Then, I will either choose one or share some feedback so we can keep iterating & brainstorming new variations until I'm satisfied with one.

Now, before we kick things off, I would like to share a few rules & guidelines I'd like you to keep in mind as we brainstorm variations for each element.

1/ PRE-HEADER COPYWRITING GUIDELINES
• Grab attention by either calling out the ideal reader, asking a curiosity-piquing question, or stating the name of the lead magnet

2/ HEADLINE COPYWRITING GUIDELINES

• Aim to write each of these variations like an experience direct response copywriter and don't shy away from being "salesy" or "click-baity" - as long as the promise we're making is realistic and aligned with the value prop of our newsletter, we're good.
• Half of the headline variations should be benefit-driven and then the other half should be transformation-driven. In both cases, though, our goal is to tap into the deepest desires of our target readers.
• Each headline variation should end with an objection handling statement addressing a potential reason or excuse that my target reader might have for not signing up. 

My 2 go-to frameworks to write these statements are: "Without {thing people in my niche usually think they need}" and "Even if you {common limiting belief or excuse people have for not achieving desired outcome}." Importantly, we should always put these objection handling statements within parentheses.

• Half of the headline variations should start with a verb - but we should avoid using the word "learn." Because even though we're going to share information and we can't guarantee a result, people aren't interested in learning - they're interested in solving a problem or achieving a specific outcome. You can decide how to start the other 10 variations.
• When possible, let's aim to use big numbers in the headline.

3/ SUB-HEADLINE COPYWRITING GUIDELINES
• Mention what's inside and how it works.
• Each sub-headline variation should make it clear *how* the lead magnet is going to deliver on the promise we're making in the headline - the more specific, the better.
• Each sub-headline variation should be 3-4 sentences long (max).
• If it makes sense, we can use bullet points to make the sub-headline easier to read.

4/ CTA COPYWRITING GUIDELINES

• Each CTA variation should be action-oriented and start with a verb
• Each CTA variation should be tied to the outcome/transformation of the headline we ended up choosing
• Each CTA variation should have 3-5 words max. Though we should favor shorter words over longer ones so we can save as much real estate as possible.
• Bland & generic CTAs like "Sign up" or "Join {newsletter name}" are strictly banned. Also avoid the word "join" in general.

5/ SOCIAL PROOF
• Show social proof with some of sort of "credibility statement" or stat that shows that you're legit/you know what you're doing.
• The more "tangible" & concrete you can make this section, the better.

GENERAL GUIDELINES

•  Each time you brainstorm new sub-headline or CTA variations, please remind me of the copy we've already decided on for all of the previous elements we've written copy for.

• Once we've brainstormed & decided on the final variation for each of the landing page elements I want you to share all the landing page copy without any formatting using this Markdown framework:

(Regular text) {Pre-Header}
(Heading 1) {Lead Magnet Headline}
(Heading 3, italics) {Headline Objection Handling Statement}
(Regular text) {Lead Magnet Value Prop}
(Regular text, italics) {CTA}
(Regular text, italics) {Social Proof}

That's all!

Now, let's get started.

Please go ahead and brainstorm 20 potential headline variations following the framework I just outlined above.

    Step 3: Write The Product Requirements Document For Claude

    Boom - if you’ve made it this far that means you already have:

    • A lead magnet idea

    • A conversion optimized landing page

    But why stop there?

    Next, I’m going to give you a prompt that’s going to help you write your app requirements. This prompt expands your lead magnet idea into a document that you can use to tell Claude what you want to build.

    This does two things:

    1. It helps you get clear on what you want to build

    2. It shows you what Claude needs in order to create a prototype.

    This eliminates the back-and-forth when working with AI. Instead of Claude asking clarifying questions or building something close to what you want, you get exactly what you need on the first try.

    Here’s the prompt:

    Create a Product Requirements Document (PRD) for: 

{Insert your lead magnet details} 

This will be built as a single Claude Artifact.

**This PRD is specifically for Claude to use as build instructions.** 

Make the PRD detailed enough that if I copy-paste it to a fresh Claude chat, Claude could build the tool immediately without asking questions. 

Focus on:
• Build Instructions: What Claude needs to create
• Functional Requirements: Exactly what the tool should do
• Technical Specifications: How Claude should implement it
• No irrelevant sections: Skip implementation phases, success criteria, or other planning elements that don't help Claude build

The goal is a clear instruction manual for Claude, not a traditional business PRD.

Output format in markdown.

    Step 4: Build Your Mini-SaaS Lead Magnet

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0zPe!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F03bb820a-8cf6-4950-acaf-fa7f349823b5_2950x1750.png)

    Ready to build a software app?

    This will blow your mind.

    We’re going to build an app using the Claude Artifacts feature. Artifacts allow you to turn your ideas into shareable apps, tools, or content by simply describing what you need.

    Here’s how:

    1. Start a new chat by clicking on Artifacts → New Artifact

    2. Click “Apps and websites”

    3. Copy/paste your PRD from Step 3 into the chat and hit enter.

    Stand up, stretch, and go grab a cup of water while your Digital Intern builds your app.

    It’s literally that simple.

    When I asked Claude to build my Social Media Content Generator, in 3 minutes I could click buttons, enter text, and get post-ready content using my 5 different proven social post formats.

    Once the basic version worked, I added features through conversation:

    • _"Can we add different writing styles? Like sh_t poster, opinionator, or fact presenter?"* Claude updated the tool instantly. New dropdown appeared.

    • "What about hook variations for each idea? Give me 3 different hooks per post." Another update. Now each content idea had multiple hook options.

    • "Let's add a copy-to-clipboard button for each section." Boom. Professional-looking copy buttons appeared.

    Each conversation improved the tool.

    I could see changes happen in real-time.

    I got excited and tried to add a "Niche Market Assistant" that would drill down Health → Fitness → Strength Training → etc. It was buggy, complicated, and I spent way too much time trying to make it work before finally scrapping it. The lesson here is just because you can add a feature doesn't mean you should. More features can make an app worse, not better.

    This led to a complete redesign of the user flow based on a writer's natural thought process:

    1. Topic first (big, prominent input)

    2. Format selection (visual cards, not dropdowns)

    3. Voice options (optional, at the end)

    It went from feeling like a form to feeling like a modern app.

    Tip: If your first design feels clunky, ask Claude to act like a UX designer and apply modern design principles to your artifact. It will make recommendations and clean up the user interface automatically.

    Now you’ve got your lead magnet, but you still need a way to capture emails and deliver it automatically.

    Which takes us to the last step.

    Step 5: Setup A Simple Lead Funnel With Lovable

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Mkq0!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F8fd58b30-a18a-407b-b3c8-3ec78b0b61e2_1824x828.png)

    In this step we are going to wire your lead funnel together.

    To do this, I used Lovable. Lovable helps you build full-stack web applications using natural language prompts (no coding required).

    Most email services like Kit or Mailchimp offer basic landing page builders. They work fine for simple pages, but they're limited. You get templates that look like everyone else's, restricted customization, and basic functionality.

    With Lovable, you describe what you want in plain English, and it builds a custom web application that does exactly what you need.

    Here's what you're building:

    Landing Page → Email Capture → Thank You Page → Lead Magnet Delivery

    How to do it:

    Create a free Lovable account and prompt it to build your lead funnel. Lovable will generate a functional app in less than 10 minutes.

    For my Social Content Generator, I used this prompt structure:

    I want to build a lead funnel.

Landing Page --> Thank You Page

Lead Magnet = 

{Insert Your Landing Page Copy}

---

Only capture the email address.

Integrate with ConvertKit

ConvertKit javascript embed = 

{Insert Your Convertkit Javascript Embed}

Convertkit form ID =

{Insert Your Convertkit Form ID}
---

These are my colors:

{Insert your preferred background and acceent colors}

And use Google Font: 

{Insert the Google Font Style}

---
Lead Magnet URL

{Insert the Claude URL or URL to your lead magnet}

    The prompt above gives you lots of placeholders to fill in, but you don’t have to include everything.

    Lovable is good about filling the gaps for you. Then you can provide feedback as you test and interact with your landing page.

    A few things I learned:

    1. Start with the essentials. Just like building the app in Step 3, don't try to build everything at once. Get the core flow working first - landing page, email capture, thank you page. You can always add features later.

    2. Test your email integration early. Make sure your email service is actually capturing addresses before you launch. I spent an hour troubleshooting because I forgot to activate the form in Kit.

    3. Use your brand colors. I used our PGA colors (navy blue and orange) with "Big Shoulders" Google Font so the landing page looked consistent with our other assets. This makes everything feel professional.

    If you already use Kit or another email service with landing page builders, you really don't need Lovable. But if you want something more custom or interactive, Lovable makes it easy to build exactly what you want.

    The key is testing everything before you go live. Click through the entire flow yourself. Make sure emails get captured, redirects work, and your tool loads properly.

    And finally…

    Step 6: Publish And Promote Your Lead Magnet

    Your funnel is built.

    Now you need to get it in front of people.

    Start simple:

    • Post about it on social media

    • Send it to a few friends and ask for feedback

    • Mention it in existing content when relevant (just like this post)

    The mistake most people make here is overthinking promotion. They wait for the perfect launch strategy or try to coordinate some big campaign.

    Mentioning your lead magnet regularly works better than one "perfect" launch post.
