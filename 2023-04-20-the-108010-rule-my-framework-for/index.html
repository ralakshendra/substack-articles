The 10/80/10 Rule: My Framework For Using ChatGPT As A Template "Executor"

This 3-step process is the future of writing with AI.

Author: Dickie Bush
Date: 2023-04-20

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!v4Bi!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9a3d2421-08ba-4795-a57d-97703569805b_1920x1080.jpeg)

Ahoy, Digital Writers!

Today I’m going to walk you through my framework for using ChatGPT as a template “executor.”

From a high level, templates are a set of constraints that allow you to create something quickly by “filling in the lines” rather than starting from a blank page. Yes, templates are effective for human writers (and the best digital writers use them heavily). But templates are *even* more effective for personal AI writing assistants like ChatGPT.

With that in mind, let’s walk through an example.

Here’s a tweet I posted yesterday that had excellent engagement.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!kopI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F598924e3-bf6c-4c21-8ffc-ef29b828aaa7_1418x704.png)

38.7k views. 525 likes. And 103 replies (which is something Twitter is weighing more and more positively in its algorithm).

And for anyone familiar with what we teach in Ship 30 for 30, you know whenever we Digital Writers see something that works, we try to reverse engineer it into a template.

The template here is:

Good morning to everyone {Doing Positive Personal Growth Trait}

Easy enough.

From there, I started to brainstorm all of the different ways I could write a tweet similar to this one. And after about 15 minutes of brainstorming, here’s what I came up with.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!8I0l!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F448c58bd-d2d5-460d-919c-bfafd0c92cb1_1002x450.png)

Not bad for a few minutes of work. But as I sat there thinking some more (and racking my brain for ideas), I realized: my personal writing intern ChatGPT is much better suited for a task like this.

You see, I did the “thinking” of recognizing something that worked and creating a template from it. ChatGPT is unable to do this—because it doesn’t understand the context of things that “work” and things that don’t.

Said another way, ChatGPT lacks the skill of “judgment.”

But, ChatGPT is *way* better at “executing” the template than I am.

So, rather than spending another 15 minutes coming up with a few more tweets, I spent 15 minutes writing a prompt to train ChatGPT to write tweets with this template.

Here’s what the prompt looked like:

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!-MNt!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F13632b43-31fd-4bdd-a751-b74dcde96a55_860x1652.png)

And in about 6 seconds, I had 30+ ideas of potential tweets I could use.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!kq21!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb080a9db-fef0-4ee5-bf38-bfbcb57452a3_1136x1562.png)

Now, I will by no means use every single one of these. And very rarely do I take words ChatGPT generates and copy/paste them directly into a post.

But I immediately see a handful that I could tweak slightly to better mirror my voice and writing style (that I wouldn’t have come up with on my own). And now, rather than have to constantly think about new ideas when I want to write posts like this, I can simply repost my prompt into ChatGPT and get a fresh slate of ideas as a springboard.

And this, my fellow Digital Writers, is how you write with AI.

  1. You will continue to use your judgment skills and keen eye to test tons of ideas. From here, you identify things that “work” & reverse engineer them into templates

  2. Then, rather than *spend* time executing that template, you *invest* time training ChatGPT to do the executing for you, at will, forever.

  3. And finally, you review your intern’s work to make sure it upholds a certain standard of quality before publishing

I think about this as the “10% / 80% / 10% rule” of writing with AI.

  1. You do the first 10% of the work of coming up with the template

  2. ChatGPT does the middle 80% of the execution

  3. You do the last 10% of edits to polish it off before publishing

Now, this way of writing & thinking takes time to train yourself to do.

I still defaulted into the trap of “doing” this morning when I began to generate the ideas on my own (and I’ve been writing with ChatGPT as my assistant for months now).

But, this way of writing is the future.

And if you’re reading this, you are hundreds of steps ahead of everyone else.
