5 reasons to build a trend jacking agent as a beginner

Author: Nicolas Cole
Date: 2025-12-18

What Is Trend Jacking

Whenever something big happens in the news, it becomes a “trend” for a month, a week, a day, or sometimes even a couple of hours.

For example, when:

  • A major company makes a public mistake

  • Elon Musk just Tweeted a meme and it’s going viral

  • OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google drop a new AI feature

These “trends” spark a crazy amount of engagement on social media-which you can use to your advantage.

For example, in 2017, while Cole was working for Inc. Magazine, Mark Zuckerberg gave an interview announcing a change to Facebook’s mission. The company’s previous mission, “To give people the power to share and make the world more open and connected,” was under fire after playing a controversial role in the 2016 presidential election. In this particular interview, Zuckerberg said Facebook was planning to double down on Facebook Groups, and its mission had evolved to, “Giving people the power to build community and bring the world closer together.”

Knowing Facebook was all over the news, and knowing Inc Magazine wanted to jack the trend, Cole wrote a piece titled, Mark Zuckerberg Just Changed Facebook’s Mission- Here It Is In 1 Sentence.

That article became one of his most popular articles ever, accumulating hundreds of thousands of views in a matter of days.

Why Build A Trend Jacking Agent

[

](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nicolascole_timely-content-generates-short-term-attention-activity-7050568397612314624-x_7w/?originalSubdomain=lb)

Creating timely “news” content is exhausting.

It’s hard to stay on the hamster wheel longer than a few years. But with a trend jacking agent, you can capitalize on timely opportunities without doom-scrolling Twitter for 3 hours a day.

Here’s why agents are perfect for beginners:

  • Reason #1: The input is structured News articles have headlines, dates, URLs, and summaries. This is easy for an agent to process. Compare that to “audience insights” or “personal experiences”—those are harder to automate.

  • Reason #2: The output is formulaic Trend-jacking posts follow a simple formula: Reference the trend (what happened), add your perspective (why it matters), and connect it to your audience (what they should do). This formula is easy to teach an AI agent. Compare that to “thought leadership” or “storytelling”—which is also harder to automate.

  • Reason #3: The workflow is repeatable Every single day, your agent does the same thing: check sources → filter trends → generate posts. Compare that to “create original frameworks” or “interview experts”—those require you to change the workflow every time.

  • Reason #4: The timeline is predictable Trends happen every day. News is published on a schedule. Your agent can run at 5am every morning and always have new material. Compare that to “wait for inspiration” or “monitor audience questions”—those are unpredictable and inconsistent.

  • Reason #5: The work is easy to verify When you're building your first agent, being able to debug quickly matters. With trend-jacking, there's always a source of truth (the original article) to check against. You learn faster because you can see exactly where it went wrong.

Plus once you build your first Trend-Jacking Agent, you’ll understand how to build ANY content agent because you’ll have learned the core skills:

  • Connecting data sources (news searches and trends)

  • Filtering and processing information (trend valuation)

  • Executing prompts at scale (content generation)

  • Delivering outputs to yourself (email/Slack/Notion)

Those same skills apply to every other agent you’ll ever build.
