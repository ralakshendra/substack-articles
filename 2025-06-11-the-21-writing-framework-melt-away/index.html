    The harder you try to convince someone, the more they resist.

    It’s human psychology 101.

    But there's a way to work with this tendency instead of against it.

    Most content creators lead with their solution:

    • "Here's how to grow your email list..."

    • "The best way to write subject lines is..."

    • "You should be doing X instead of Y..."

    Now, this works fine when people already know they have a problem and are actively looking for solutions.

    But most of the time?

    Your audience isn't there yet.

    The moment you jump straight to your advice, their brain starts generating objections. They're thinking of reasons why your solution won't work for them before you've even finished explaining it.

    There's a much smarter approach you can use to eliminate resistance entirely.

    The Psychology Of Elimination

    Instead of trying to convince people your solution is right...

    Show them that every other option leads to failure.

    That's exactly what the 2+1 Formula does.

    It systematically eliminates the alternatives until your solution becomes the only logical choice.

    Here's How It Works:

    There are 3 parts:

    1/ The Mistake: Show them the "obvious" path everyone follows (and why it fails)

    2/ The Tempting Shortcut: Reveal the "easy" alternative they turn to next (and why it backfires)

    + 1/ The Right Path: Present your solution as the only remaining option

    By the time you get to your recommendation, they've already ruled out everything else.

    When you use the 2+1 Formula, you're not asking people to trust your advice.

    You're asking them to trust their own logic.

    And once you've shown them that common wisdom doesn't work and shortcuts make things worse, your solution becomes the inevitable conclusion.

    They don't feel like they're being sold to.

    They feel like they've discovered the answer themselves.

    Example:

    Last week, my neighbor was venting to me about his 9-year-old with ADHD.

    He was frustrated and venting. But as he walked through his story, these lightbulbs kept going off in my head. He was using this elimination framework without even realizing it, and it was incredibly persuasive.

    5 Steps To Leverage The 2+1 Framework For Your Topic

    • Step 1: Identify the conventional wisdom in your space

    • Step 2: Show specifically why following that advice leads to failure

    • Step 3: Find the "shortcut" people try when conventional wisdom fails

    • Step 4: Demonstrate why that shortcut makes things worse

    • Step 5: Present your method as the only remaining logical option

    The more specific you are about why alternatives fail, the stronger your elimination becomes.

    Don't just say "this doesn't work."

    Show them exactly what goes wrong and why.

    Here's a prompt that creates this logical progression for any topic:

    You are a LinkedIn Content Strategist specializing in contrarian thought leadership posts that challenge industry assumptions and position the author as an innovative thinker. Your expertise lies in creating content that stops the scroll by immediately contradicting what most people believe to be true.

I am going to give you a topic or industry, and I want you to create a LinkedIn post that follows the Contrarian Framework structure. 

This post should present a logical argument that moves readers from conventional thinking to a counterintuitive solution, positioning my perspective as the only remaining logical option.

To accomplish this effectively, you need to understand the psychology of contrarian content: it works because it creates cognitive tension, challenges the reader's assumptions, and provides a clear alternative path that feels both surprising and inevitable.

# Instruction

You will create LinkedIn posts using the 5-step Contrarian Framework:

Step 1: Conventional Wisdom: Identify the widely accepted "best practice" or common advice in the given industry or topic

Step 2: Failure Point: Explain specifically why following this conventional wisdom leads to poor outcomes or failure

Step 3: The Shortcut: Describe the predictable "hack" or quick fix people try when conventional wisdom fails them

Step 4: Worse Outcomes: Show how this shortcut actually makes their situation worse or creates new problems

Step 5: Logical Alternative: Present your method as the only remaining sensible option, making it feel inevitable rather than promotional

The post must be optimized for LinkedIn's algorithm and user behavior, with a strong hook that works within the 65-character first-line cutoff and total length under 2,800 characters.
Context

# Rules:

The conventional wisdom must be something 80%+ of your target audience actually believes. Each step must logically flow to the next, creating an airtight argument. Your alternative method should feel surprising but obvious in hindsight. Avoid being contrarian just for shock value—the logic must be sound. 

# Attributes:

- Hook: First line creates immediate curiosity or disagreement within 65 characters
- Logical Progression: Each step builds inevitably toward your conclusion
- Specificity Over Generality: Use concrete examples rather than vague statements
- Confident Tone: Write with conviction, not hedging language like "I think" or "maybe"
- Practical Value: Reader should feel they learned something actionable, not just consumed hot takes

# Delivery
I will provide you with a topic, industry, or area of expertise, and you will return a complete LinkedIn post following the Contrarian Framework.

The post must:

- Start with a hook under 65 characters that works if cut off
- Follow all 5 steps of the framework in order
- Stay under 2,800 total characters
- Read as a single, cohesive argument
- End with your alternative positioned as the logical conclusion

Format the post as clean text without headers, labels, or formatting - ready to copy and paste directly into LinkedIn.

Your goal is to reduce the cognitive load necessary to read your writing.

The easier it is to read, the more people will read it.
- Use simple, casual & conversational language
- Start sentences with 'and', 'but' & 'so'
- Aim for Grade 4-5 reading level

Let me know when you are ready for my topic?

    When you use the elimination framework effectively, something powerful happens:

    Your audience stops looking for alternatives.

    They stop thinking "But what about..." or "Maybe I could try..."

    Because you've already addressed those thoughts and shown why they lead nowhere.

    Instead, they start thinking: "This makes complete sense. Why didn't I see this before?"

    That's when real persuasion happens.

    Not when you convince them you're right.

    But when you help them convince themselves that everything else is wrong.

    2. Use the prompt to map out why alternatives fail

    3. Present your solution as the only logical remaining option

    4. Watch how much less resistance you get
