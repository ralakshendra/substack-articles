Category Of One: Follow These 7 Simple Prompts And ChatGPT Will Help You Niche Down

This is how you standout online.

Author: Dickie Bush
Date: 2023-06-14

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!wxBZ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe84a43d8-cbb7-4c87-8edb-34a18fa06c89_1260x900.png)

Ahoy, Digital Writers!

Today we want to help you think like a Category Creator and take your first step to becoming known for a niche you own.

Whether you’ve been writing online and creating digital products for a long time, or are just getting started, you might still be asking yourself:

“What in the scurvy sea-dog is my niche?!”

Everyone struggles with this. It’s one on the Top 10 biggest questions, if not THE #1 question, people have when it comes to creating content, launching products, and building businesses. And the question behind the question is:

“What makes me different?”

And so when people ask the question, “How do I create a niche for myself?” that’s a very powerful and valuable question to have answered.

Right?

Because if you can figure out what makes you different—and you have gained the skill of being able to CREATE what makes you different over and over again—then you possess a superpower not very many people have.

Let’s dive in!

You do not want to be “better.”

Think about what “better” means for a second.

“Better” implies you are similar to what already exists. “Better” means there actually isn’t very much separating you from the next person. And even if you are “a ton better,” you are still existing within the context of someone else. Pepsi always mentions Coke. Coke never mentions Pepsi.

Category Kings never acknowledge they have any competition.

They are the 1 and only.

Instead, what you want is to be DIFFERENT.

Remove “better” from your vocabulary.

Get rid of it. If you find yourself writing “better” anywhere to describe who you are and what you do, that’s a neon signal you haven’t yet pinpointed what makes you different. You are still rooted to “what exists.” And you haven’t pioneered new ground… yet.

When you are different, you force readers, followers, and customers to make a choice.

You want your audience to have to choose between the “old thing” and the “new thing.” And the bigger the gap you create between the two, the easier it is for the person to decide: “Yes this is 100% what I’m looking for” or “No this is 100% NOT what I’m looking for.”

And that’s good news for you.

“OK this makes sense. But how do I stop being ‘Better,’ and how can I actually start being ‘DIFFERENT?’”

  • You can speak to a different problem. For example: if a conventional problem in personal finance is, “I don’t know how to invest money,” don’t pick that problem. Pick something different!

  • You can speak to a different solution. For example, if a conventional solution in health & wellness is to “eat healthy,” then don't waste your effort running around trying to evangelize the same obvious solution. Recommend something different!

  • You can speak to a different audience. Instead of talking to college graduates “better” than all the other people who talk to college graduates, talk to a different (more specific) audience: high-school graduates, middle-school graduates, grad school graduates, etc. These are DIFFERENT audiences.

The big idea here is that whenever you see lots of people all speaking to the same audience, about the same problem, recommending the same solution, within the same time horizon, in the same context, go do the opposite.

Most people understand this in theory, but when push comes to shove, they chase acceptance.

They can’t do it.

They would rather “fight” to fit in, than go their own way and easily stand out.

Our goal is to help you do the latter.

Start Broad, Niche Down: 7 Steps To Naming Your First Niche.

So, let’s get you started on your path—and because this is Write With AI, we are going to call in the intern!

(Then, over the next few weeks, we will help you clarify your niche further and further.)

Step 1: What do you want to write about… generally?

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!DN2p!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ed9360a-f2eb-49d3-8fa1-a764544d5ec5_1324x380.png)

If you want to take a data-driven approach, look through your Twitter, LinkedIn, or Google analytics.

  • What topics perform the best?

  • Which of these topics do you enjoy writing about the most?

  • Choose the 1 that fits both: what’s working for readers, and what genuinely interests you.

If you don’t have as much data yet, then just pick something. Anything. Literally. It’s better to have “a niche” than to have no niche at all.

  • “I want to write about running.” Amazing.

  • “I want to write about yoga.” Awesome.

  • “I want to write about sales.” OK, perfect.

Still stuck?

Run the Endless Idea Generation prompt for any topic you are interested in writing about to generate ideas on 7 different subtopics. Or, do the 2-Year Test on yourself! Just pick something so that you can walk through the exercise with us—and learn how to create a niche for yourself.

You can always change it later if you’d like (but at least you know the steps).

Step 2: Take your general topic and make a list of the Top 10 problems people experience related to this topic.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!eZys!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd3db752a-006c-44a6-83e3-16829101c023_1190x874.png)

Most people say they have trouble niching down, but in reality, they can’t even name the Top 10 problems someone faces in their broad topic category. So that’s Step 1.

Do a little research. Talk to your intern.

Step 3: Choose 1 problem to solve.

You can build an entire career around helping people solve 1 problem.

In Ship 30 for 30, there are hundreds of problems we could solve for our members, but we focus on just one: “writing consistently.” The big mistake people make when trying to create their niche is they think, “Well people have all these problems, and so I need to solve all of them.” Which, by definition, makes it very hard to know what your niche is!

But if you choose just 1 problem, all of a sudden, you have a niche.

So, pick something that gets you going—and you’ll keep going for decades.

See? Easy.

Step 4: WHO specifically is not getting the help they need (and is willing to pay for a solution)?

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!OYHG!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ea47ee4-1c12-4b1a-ac71-4141c26e5dd6_1336x900.png)

Your niche starts with WHO you want to help.

It’s not about you. So, who do you want to help? What’s their experience level? Where are they in life? What do they need help with?

When you create something for “everyone,” you end up creating something for no one.

Make a choice and keep going.

Step 5: Take the 1 problem you are setting out to solve for the 1 person and make a list of the Top 10 “weird,” opportunistic solutions.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!KjFD!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F097582bb-ddc8-4c50-9687-a5a3d0ab5eb1_1340x858.png)

This is where “creating your niche” starts to get really fun.

Similar to the “problem,” make a list of the Top 10 solutions to to solve the 1 problem you’ve chosen to laser-in on—and go through the same process:

  • Which one of these solutions is the weirdest one? The most ignored?

  • Which one of these solutions is the “most valuable?”

  • Which one are you most qualified to execute or learn about?

As much as possible, you want to avoid creating a niche that solves a popular problem with a popular solution. That’s where all the competition is.

To avoid competition, pick problems that are weird and commonly ignored, and solve those weird problems with unconventional, unpopular solutions.

Step 6: Niche down again—getting even more specific.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!C8SJ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6e480a2f-0024-44d6-bdca-99a3e95cf789_1352x994.png)

All remarkable Categories can be reduced down to 1 word:

Specificity.

The more specific the idea, the more engaging it is.

And there are 7 proven ways to niche down even further on your target audience:

  • By industry (X for healthcare startups)

  • By demographic (X for middle-aged women)

  • By physical location (X for Chicago)

  • By digital platform (X for Twitter)

  • By price (X for free)

  • By distribution (X but delivery)

  • By problem (X but w/o Y)

The goal here is to get so specific that you feel like you actually can’t get anymore specific. You are trying to make it abundantly clear who (exactly) you are for, and more importantly, who you are NOT for.

A lot of creators shy away from this level of specificity—don’t be one of them.

Step 7: Name your niche.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!y5gE!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2472ce6f-7c2b-4c90-af3e-5357fd620b91_1280x316.png)

You’re almost done creating your niche!

Can you feel the level of clarity?

Choosing 1 specific problem to solve, and finding or inventing an unconventional, “Woah I never thought of that” solution is the key to niching down. Because the more specific the problem, and the more specific (and “new”) the solution, the more you are going to stand out.

Category Creation is how you achieve true differentiation.

The “hey-I’m-slightly-cheaper-slightly-faster-slightly-cooler-slightly-more-refreshing” stuff is a rat’s game, and forces you to live in a constant state of competition.

To design a category, you have to frame a new problem and a new solution in a provocative way. It should make the customer STOP, tilt their head, and immediately wonder, “This is for something different. Do I need this?” And in order to do that, you have to be the trusted authority on that new language—and subsequently, that new category.

It all starts with how you Frame, Name, and Claim it.

And the prompt you’ve been waiting for…

Act like a Category Creator.

You are going to help me pinpoint what makes me DIFFERENT.

We are going do this by choosing 1 specific problem to solve, for 1 specific person, and finding or inventing an unconventional, “Woah I never thought of that” solution. Because the more specific the problem, and the more specific (and “new”) the solution, the more you will stand out as DIFFERENT.

There are 3 ways you can be different.

1. You can speak to a different problem.
2. You can speak to a different solution. 
3. You can speak to a different audience. 

The big idea here is that we don’t want to speak to the same audience, about the same problem, recommending the same solution, within the same time horizon, in the same context, you are going to ****do the opposite so that I stand out.

We are going to start broad and then niche down.

Here are the steps:

**Step 1:** I will give you a topic that I want write about. For example, education, yoga, sales, etc.

**Step 2:** YOUR task is to identify and succinctly list the top 10 prevalent challenges associated with the given topic, using no more than 10 words for each problem.

**Step 3:** Once you have presented the list of challenges, I will select one problem for you to focus on solving or alternatively, I may suggest a problem of my own for you to address.

**Step 4:** Given the problem I provide, YOUR task is to identify 10 distinct target audiences that not only face the problem but are significantly underserved, yet possess a willingness to pay for its resolution. Be specific in identifying the ignored groups that need assistance.

Provide a concise list of 10 target audiences that meet the criteria mentioned above. Focus on those who have been overlooked and require help in addressing the problem. Each target audience should be described using no more than 10 words.

**Step 5:**  Once you have presented the list of target audiences, I will choose 1 of the 10 target audiences or provide 1 of my own.

**Step 6:** Given the target audience I provide, YOUR task is to take the target audience I provided and act like a forward-thinking, creative, and curious problem solver with a deep understanding of human psychology. Using 10 words or less, you will list 10 possible solutions to the problem I’ve chosen for the target audience.

**Step 7:** Once you have presented the list of solutions, I will choose 1 of the 10 solutions to the problem or provide 1 of my own.

**Step 8:**  Given the solution I provide, YOUR task is to take the solution I provide and act like a digital entrepreneur. You will give me with 21 niches, divided across the 7 proven ways to niche down, each with 3 ideas nested under each proven way. You are targeting a specific, and hyper-relevant audience that needs help solving the problem for each niche (use 10 words or less per niche). Be clear about WHO the target audience is for each niche.

Here are the 7 proven ways to niche down:

1. By Price

2. By Industry

3. By Location

4. By Experience

5. By Situation

6. By Demographic

7. By Problem

**Step 8:** Once you have presented the list of 21 niches, I will choose 1 of the niches or provide 1 of my own.

**Step 9:** Given the niche I provide, YOUR task is to take the niche I provide and return the following list:

**Summary**

- **Niche:**  the niche
- **Problem:** the problem
- **Solution:** the solution
- **Benefits:** the benefit, the future benefit, the ultimate benefit of those benefits

Do you understand?

This was a doozy!

Remember: you still have to think! For each step in this process we encourage you to take what’s inside your head and use AI to help you shape the outcome. When you invent the language to solve a problem, you become the trusted authority to educate people on the definition of that new language — and subsequently, that new category.
