[Shorts Studio] #13 How To Edit Shorts With CapCut (5 Easy Steps)

Author: Nicolas Cole
Date: 2025-04-04

Welcome to Module 4-Lesson 3 of AI Shorts Studio: Editing.

By the end of this lesson, you’ll know exactly how to:

  • Edit your video from scratch using CapCut

  • Use the core CapCut features that save you time and make your videos pop

  • Avoid the 80% of features that don’t matter (yet)

We’ll start with a quick overview of the 2 workflows—pick the one that fits how you create.

Let’s get into it.

Choose Your CapCut Editing Workflow

Before we touch the timeline, let’s clarify something:

There are two primary ways you might be building your Shorts—and they each come with different editing needs.

Workflow A: Record Yourself (Talking Head)

This is the most direct way to make Shorts.

You open CapCut, hit record, and film:

  • Your face (webcam)

  • Your screen (for tutorials or walkthroughs)

  • Or both, picture-in-picture

After recording, your focus is:

  • Adding captions, b-roll, and images

  • Cleaning up mistakes and filler words

  • Making it visually clean with basic transitions and music

You can go idea → filmed → edited → published without ever leaving CapCut.

Workflow B: Faceless / Assembled

This is more “drag and drop.”

You’re importing assets you’ve already created using other tools, like:

  • ElevenLabs (voiceovers)

  • HeyGen (avatars)

  • Sora or stock b-roll (visuals)

  • ChatGPT 4o image generation

  • Background music or sound effects

You can also generate most of the same media directly inside CapCut, but with less control and lower quality.

Once You’ve Recorded or Imported, the editing process becomes the same. You’re lining up clips, trimming dead space, adding visuals, and packaging it up for publishing.

Now, there’s a ton that goes into editing.

But if you zoom out and focus on the core principles of video, everything becomes way easier.

Your 80/20 Shorts Editing Checklist

Shorts live and die by attention.

And we’ve already talked about how attention is earned through movement and sound.

Let’s break it down.

With video, you can create movement in 3 primary ways:

  1. You physically move (gesture, lean forward, nod)

  2. The camera moves (zoom, pan, crop, cut)

  3. The screen changes (text pops in, image slides over, color fades)

And underneath that movement, there are 5 core editing techniques every good Short uses:

You can apply these to anything: video, text, b-roll, images, effects.

Whether you’re using CapCut or not, these concepts apply everywhere.

You can use 1 concept at a time or all 5 in one scene. This is the art of video. Now, we’re not going to cover all of video editing in this one lesson. That would take hours. Now let’s talk Sound.

If movement gets attention, sound carries the energy.

This is what keeps people emotionally engaged—even if they’re just watching a 30-second clip.

There are 3 primary tools you can use:

  • Your voice (emphasis, tone, pacing, pauses)

  • Music (upbeat, suspenseful, emotional, ambient)

  • Sound effects (camera clicks, whooshes, dings, risers)

Sound tells the viewer how to feel about what they’re seeing on screen.

Add an emotional music track or a single “ding” at the right moment…and suddenly, your video feels 10x more polished.

But there’s one last thing…

Change Something Every 1–2 Seconds

In most successful Shorts, something changes on screen every 1–2 seconds.

This can be:

  • A jump cut to a new shot

  • A zoom in on your face

  • Text animating in or changing color

  • B-roll popping up

  • A sound effect hitting at the right moment

This is how you keep the viewer’s brain stimulated.

The good news?

You can do all of this inside CapCut.

How To Start Creating Shorts With CapCut (5 Steps)

Open CapCut.

Click New Project.

When you open a project, you’ll see 4 main areas:

  • Top Left: Your asset library—videos, text, transitions, and more.

  • Center: Your video preview.

  • Right Side: Editing options like size, color, filters.

  • Bottom: The timeline. That’s where you do your actual editing.

Let’s break down the main steps you’ll need to get started.

Step 1: Import Your Assets

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!MmmT!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb4219be7-b20e-4e27-b1b4-d47a34b7a952_1256x1174.png)

Drag in your video, audio, B-roll, or anything else—whether it’s from ElevenLabs, Sora, or screen recordings.

You can also record directly inside CapCut, like a Loom-style talking head and CapCut will drop it right into a new project for you so you can immediately start editing.

Once a clip is on the timeline, you can trim it, split it, and delete sections.

Let’s say you recorded yourself talking head style.

You’re sitting at your desk. You’ve recorded a 1-2 minute clip walking through your talking points. But now… there’s a lot of pauses, “ums,” and “uhhs.” You repeated yourself 3 times in the middle. And you’re wondering:

“Do I really have to manually chop it up?”

Nope.

CapCut does it for you.

Step 2: Add Auto-Captions

If you’re only going to do one thing to make your Shorts feel more premium… it's this:

Add captions.

Here’s why:

  • 70–80% of people watch Shorts without sound

  • Captions boost engagement and retention

  • AND you can take automatically remove filler words

It takes less than 2 minutes.

Go to Text > Auto Captions > Generate.

Once it’s processed, you can clean it up by clicking “Remove Filler Words.”

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!L0e3!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F12cc3b5a-3ff5-43e3-8328-a459f061aafd_1986x1010.png)

You can also highlight and delete specific lines directly from the transcript view — way faster than scrubbing and cutting manually.

CapCut also lets you highlight keywords and apply text templates—like one-word-at-a-time animations—to make the text pop.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vKTW!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4158b300-c1b4-42da-928b-bbc4fa283552_510x838.png)

Step 3: Adjust Aspect Ratio

For Shorts, change your canvas to 9:16.

If your video looks small, grab the corners and scale it up.

[

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Xez1!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb465407f-479e-4037-ae9a-a2e5fcbdf6ab_1926x960.png)

Do the same for text.

Step 4: Add B-Roll

Drag B-roll on top of your primary video in the timeline.

If you recorded it yourself, select the clip from your Media Bin and drag it above your main footage. If you’re using AI-generated or stock footage, do the same.

A few tips:

  • Trim the edges so that it only plays during the relevant portion of your voiceover or dialogue.

  • To make the B-roll feel naturally integrated, try using a simple fade or dissolve transition between your A-roll and B-roll.

  • If your video has a key point, a quick jump cut to a related clip can add punch without distracting the viewer.

Step 5: Add Background Music And SFX

Click Audio, pick a track (like “Chill Vibes”), drag it into the timeline.

Use the fade in/out handles and lower the volume so it sits behind your voice—usually around -25 to -35 dB.

Next, add in your sound effects (SFX) like whoosh, click, or pop sounds. Place them at key moments—such as when transitioning between clips or when a caption pops in. Don’t overdo it; 1-2 well-placed effects per video is plenty.

That’s it. You’ve got a basic edit, captions, B-roll, and music—all in CapCut.

Before you export, watch your video all the way through.

Ask yourself:

  • Does it feel smooth?

  • Is anything awkward, silent, or too slow?

Cut out the dead space.

If a part drags, trim it. If something feels rushed, let it breathe for a half second longer.

And when you’re ready…

Export Your Video & Post It To YouTube, TikTok, Or Instagram

In CapCut, once you’re happy with your video:

Click Export (top right corner).

Choose your settings:

  • Resolution. 1080x1920 (standard for Shorts, Reels, TikToks)

  • Frame Rate. 30 fps (unless you shot in 60fps for a cinematic effect—most of the time, stick with 30)

  • Bitrate. Standard is fine, but bump it to 10,000–15,000 kbps if you want extra sharpness (optional)

  • Format. MP4

  • File Name & Location. Name it clearly so you can find it easily.

Hit Export. CapCut will render your Short in a few seconds to a few minutes depending on your hardware.

If this is your first Short, just post it. You’re not optimizing yet. You’re just building the habit of publishing. You’ll get better by doing, not by tinkering.

Start with: YouTube Shorts (if that’s your focus)

Then repurpose to TikTok, Reels, etc.
