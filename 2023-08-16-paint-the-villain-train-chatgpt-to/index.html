    Ahoy, Digital Writers!

    Ever felt like ChatGPT was supposed to help, but your AI-generated content just falls flat?

    Yeah, me too!!

    AI is powerful, but it doesn't "get" virality or human engagement like we do (yet). **But _a_fter spending what feels like a 1,000 hours training ChatGPT to write content that doesn’t suck, I’ve finally figured it out.

    And today, I want to share my 7-step framework for writing short-form internet hits with AI.

    Let’s dive in!

    First, Why Does Most ChatGPT Content Suck?

    ChatGPT is a super-efficient intern. It's eager, quick, and follows instructions. But if you don't guide it properly, you end up with generic, forgettable writing.

    It's not just about what you ask, but how you ask.

    Ready?

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!X661!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3347429e-04e9-45da-adb0-e45588c75d37_1208x308.png)

    Easy!

    100%, the internet will go bananas! (Kidding of course)

    People who say ChatGPT "creates mediocre content" are giving it mediocre prompts. And as much as it is “AI cliché,” it’s true—what and how you ask ChatGPT (input) determines the quality of what ChatGPT gives you in return (output).

    Let’s dig into the framework.

    7 Steps To Writing Short-Form Internet Hits With ChatGPT

    This framework follows the 10-80-10 rule for writing with AI.

    • You do the first 10% of the work of coming up with the idea

    • ChatGPT does the middle 80% of the execution

    • You do the last 10% of edits to polish it off before publishing

    And instead of using a MEGA-prompt, I chained together several smaller prompts.

    With the shorter prompts, I, as the “AI orchestrator,” can iterate on what ChatGPT returns. Through a series of quick feedback loops, each prompt guides my Digital Intern in the direction I ultimately want.

    However, before I prompt ChatGPT with anything, I start where all Digital Writer’s should start:

    With the READER!

    Step 1: Clarify What Your Reader Wants

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!uASg!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3905a2d9-1e5a-4929-a2f8-11da71a56b58_908x96.png)

    Jump in your reader’s shoes for a minute.

    • What do they really want

    • What keeps them up at night?

    • What do they daydream about during boring Zoom meetings?

    Start with a goal you know you reader wants to accomplish.

    When you do, you open the door to a resonant connection. For example, if you write for fitness enthusiasts, what are they daydreaming about? Are they looking for the perfect summer body? 13% body fat? Adding more fruits and veggies into each meal? Once you name the specific “thing” the reader wants, you’ll have their attention and go from a a writer who "guesses" to one who truly "understands" their readers.

    Pick a goal you can focus on and let’s move to the next step!

    Step 2: Paint The “Villain” In Your Reader’s Story

    Problems are like a villain—coming to steal your reader’s hopes and dreams.

    Nobody loves the bad guy, because people don’t like pain! And when you poke the bear in a sore spot, by pointing out an obstacle, you get attention. “Oh, you want that perfect summer beach body so you can impress your friends at the beach after being made fun of all last year for your beer belly? If you let that happen again, you’ll be the butt of your friends jokes at every poker night for the rest of your life! Yuk!”

    Feel the difference?

    To do this, we are going to use ChatGPT to find a “villain.”

    The prompt is inspired by Thomas Strider’s “Painting a Villain” framework.

    It does two things:

    1. Identifies the primary reasons why the reader wants their goal.

    2. Finds the negative consequences of taking zero action on the goal. (e.g recurring shame at the poker table for failing to shed the beer belly)

    Take a look!

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!I17z!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F485090a6-4b15-4d5a-9a4d-5109512a8074_912x1298.png)

    Here’s the prompt:

    You are an AI that specializes in assisting Digital Writers. You are an expert in writing, copywriting, marketing, behavioral psychology,and entrepreneurship.  You are specific and your output is specific. 

Now, I am going to train you to find a "villain".

I will give you a [goal] and you will identify 10 "villains."

A "villain" is a situation.
- Evil things that happen in your industry
- Terrible scenarios that have happened to your tartget audience

We are going to do this in 2 steps.

Step 1: Identify the motivations people have for acheiving [goal]. They can be positive or negative.

For example, if you want to start a side hustel, then your motivations might be:
- They want to leave their full-time job
- They want the freedom to control their income
- They don't feel safe in their current job
- They hate corporate life
- The never want to work a 9-5

Step 2: Pinpoint the potential negative consequences of taking zero action on the [goal]. Make it 10X worse.

For example: If you don’t like your job, then the consequence is going the rest of your young and abled adult life spending 40+ hours a week doing something you hate - ouch.
And if you don’t feel safe at your job, then the fear of getting laid off with 0 backup plan is frightening. You might lose your income, health insurance, be unable to pay your mortgage, and a host of other issues.

So, your villain would be: 
- Dissatisfaction with 9-5 jobs.

And the reasons it's a villain: 
- lack of security
- poor management
- An unfulfilling and dreadful life

Bias toward short, concise sentences.

Do you understand?

    Now that we have our villain, let's leverage the knowledge to reel in the reader.

    Step 3: Train ChatGPT To Write An Emotional Hook

    In this step, we turn each of our villains into an emotional hook.

    Emotional connection makes your content stickier and it drives action, more than logic ever will. When the reader feels something, whether it's joy, sorrow, or excitement, you've won half the battle. Without it, content is bland, flat, robotic, and…forgettable.

    The best way I’ve found to do this with ChatGPT is to feed it examples of strong emotional hooks, which is what we do in this prompt—turning our “villains” into hooks.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!FANW!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2796101d-0454-4a1a-81d6-3fca344573ab_384x1020.png)

    Looking better!

    Note: You can swap out the examples in the prompt with hooks you want ChatGPT to model for a variety of different emotional frames.

    Here’s the prompt:

    Great, now turn the villains into a piece of content.

The (villain) is (strong negative word).

For example:
- Everyone sucks at LinkedIn when the start.
- There are 7 types of creators that make $0 on LinkedIn.
- "How to" content is the plague of LinkedIn
- The 9-5 being safe is some sick joke.

The best hooks rile up emotion - period.

Write 3 pieces of content for each villain.

Do you understand?

    Step 4. Steal A Proven Short-Form Pattern

    Ok, we have our hook, now let’s apply a proven viral short-form pattern.

    Why reinvent the wheel when you can make use what you know works?!

    • Browse “X” for trending content

    • Reverse engineer short-form content into a template

    • Or jump into Typeshare and pull from hundreds of different templates

    Once you have your template, ask ChatGPT to execute it for you.

    For example:

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!x3RI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F331878ff-3de4-41dc-b056-4e9d075e71b7_876x1150.png)

    Our short-form content is starting to look better, but it’s still bland and vague.

    We will fix that in the next step.

    Here’s the Proven Template prompt:

    Now, for each hook in ***[Insert Section]***

I want you to turn it into a ***[type: e.g. benefits, mistakes, etc.]*** short-form content.

All sentences should be direct statements that end in a period. All sentences are 8 words maximum. The tone should be casual and conversational, and the language should be written at a 5th grade level. Bias toward short, concise sentences.

I want you to get specific with details. Instead of using words like ‘good’ and ‘fast’ define what they mean. Use nouns.

Short-form template for you to follow:

***[Insert Template Here. For example:
{Hook}

{Solution}

• {Benefit 1}
• {Benefit 2}
• {Benefit 3}

{Reason why}]***

    Step 5: Train ChatGPT For Clarity (Not Fluff)

    Content that’s relatable and down-to-earth, builds trust.

    The best way to do this is by being specific. For example, instead of saying, "Invest in good equipment," say, "Swap your regular Chuck Taylors for cushioned Hokas." Think nouns instead of adjectives and examples over superlatives.

    Unfortunately, ChatGPT loves adjectives and vague flowery language.

    So we need to tell our intern what we want—to be specific.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!5HZZ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F3a38dc5a-bdb8-4339-9015-8065b1330386_1058x502.png)

    Keep running this prompt until you get the level of specificity you want.

    One adjustment, I want you to get specific with details. 

Instead of using words like ‘good’ and ‘fast’ define what they mean. 

Use nouns and noun modifiers.

    Step 6: Push Past Boring With "The Tequila Test"

    ChatGPT loves the obvious.

    It's been trained on the most popular (cliché) work on the internet. So, you have to push AI's thinking to get rid of boring defaults. Which is why our last prompt is to run "The Tequila Test" and get ChatGPT to say something DIFFERENT, fresh, vibrant, and zesty (much like a good tequila)!

    Find a piece of content you want to “twist,” and run it through the prompt.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!spC9!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fb09487f7-71b3-45ad-a15a-acf4e7b76ae1_1174x416.png)

    Almost there!

    Step 7: Edit The Last 10%. Hit Publish! (Repeat)

    Congratulations, you made it to the last and most important step!

    Add your personal tweaks to better mirror your voice and writing style. Then head over to LinkedIn, X, or Threads and hit publish. Then do it again.

    [

    ](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!6g3P!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fcc77e125-b13d-4309-ac75-40c307e06cca_820x604.png)

    Rather than always having to think about new ideas when you want to write posts like this, you can run through these prompts with ChatGPT and get a fresh slate of ideas as a springboard.

    Phew, this was a long one!

    A Few Final Thoughts

    This approach isn't just limited to short-form content. Now that you have the strategy, you can apply the same principle to anything:blog posts, Atomic Essays, email subject lines, etc.

    Remember:

    • Clear Goals: Understand what your readers want.

    • Emotion is Key: You want your reader to feel something.

    • Specificity is the secret: The more context, the better the output.

    With the right guidance, even ChatGPT can be the best “viral” writing intern you’ve ever had.
