    Part #1: Brainstorm 15 Subject Line Ideas Inspired By Your Hook

    Go ahead & open your favorite LLM, and then copy/paste the prompt below.

    Hey ChatGPT, I need your help.

I want to turn a viral LinkedIn hook I recently found into an attention-grabbing subject line.

So, here's what we're going to do:

I'm going to share with you the LinkedIn hook and then I want you to come up with 15 different subject line variations inspired by that hook.

Now, here are a few important guidelines you should follow when brainstorming each subject line variation:

• The subject line shouldn't have more than 45-50 characters (including spaces)

• The subject line needs to be clear (not clever) but also create a "curiosity gap" (i.e. We can't give the reader the "answer". Instead we need to give them a reason to want to open the email.)

• The subject line should be formatted in sentence case (never capitalize the first letter of each word because we don't want our subject lines to read like an article headlone).

• For each subject line variation we come up with, we also need to generate a "preview text." (For context: the "preview text" is the copy that shows up right next to or underneath the email subject line on people's inbox, and it acts almost a sub-headline.)

• The preview text should *complement* NOT repeat the same idea that's being communicated in the subject line. We also want to avoid repeating any words that have already been used in the headline. (Again, we want to use the "preview text" as a sub-headline.)

• Lastly, please structure your output in the following way:

[Heading] Subject Line X

• Subject line: [insert subject line variation]
• Preview text: [insert preview text varation]

Does that make sense? Are you ready for me to share the first LinkedIn hook?

    Once the LLM responds saying it’s ready to start brainstorming subject line ideas, give it the Twitter/X or LinkedIn hook you’d like to use as the starting point.

    Why This Prompt Works

    This prompt is designed to create subject lines that do two things really well:

    • Creates A Curiosity Gap: Each subject line hints at something significant—whether it’s a near-collapse, a bold decision, or a surprising partnership—but it doesn’t give away the punchline. It gets the reader enough to be interested but not enough to feel satisfied.

    • Prioritizes Clarity Over Cleverness: These subject lines avoid being clever or cryptic. They’re direct, telling the reader exactly what the email will be about (Apple’s comeback, Steve Jobs’ pivotal decisions, etc.), but in a way that invites further exploration. This works because readers are drawn to clarity—they want to know what they’re getting into before they click.

    Bottom line: A clear but incomplete subject line, draws your reader in, because it doesn’t give away the full story, enticing them to click to read the details.
